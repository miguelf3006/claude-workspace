# Chapter 5: Attraction Offers — The Decoy Offer

## Core Idea
Advertise a **lesser, smaller, or simpler version** of your premium offer to get leads in the door. When they engage, present **both** options side by side — and emphasize the premium one. The contrast sells it.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Two-Step Decoy Structure**:
  1. Advertise a lesser, smaller, or simpler version of your premium offer as the **decoy**
  2. When leads engage, offer **both** options, but emphasize the **premium** one
- **How to Build the Decoy**: Fewer components · older models · fewer personalized versions · **and remove any guarantees.** "Your attraction offer only has to get leads to engage. Nothing more."
- **The Contrast Rule**: The value of the premium option comes entirely from its difference to the decoy. **Make the decoy as basic as reasonable and the premium as awesome as possible.** The bigger the contrast, the better the deal reads, and the more customers take it.
- **The Permission Question** (present the premium first): *"Are you here for free stuff or lasting results?"*
  - Says **results** (most do) → skip straight to the premium offer
  - Says **free stuff** → present the decoy, then immediately contrast it with the premium
  - Then, **only after presenting both**: *"Which do you think will get you to your goal faster?"* or *"Which would you prefer — X (less valuable benefit) or Y (more valuable benefit 1, 2, 3)?"* At this point they have to say the premium offer, and you move forward mutually agreeing it's best for them.
- **The Four Ways to State a Discount** (all mean the same thing — test which converts in your market). On a $100/month annual product sold at $900:
  - **Percentage off**: 25% off
  - **Absolute amount**: $300 off
  - **Free portion**: 3 months free
  - **Demised total package**: one year for $900

## Key Concepts
- **Advertise benefits, not features.** Sell the dream outcome — *"a transformation in 21 days,"* not *"workouts and meal plans."* Product details belong in the sales presentation, not the advertising. *"Private jets and rowboats can both get you to an exotic island, but the premium option is certainly more enjoyable."*
- **Discount offers have higher show-up rates than free offers.** Free gets more leads; discount gets fewer leads but a higher percentage who show. **If show-up rates are your problem, run a discount offer instead** — critical for businesses with high no-show costs like doctors, lawyers, and dentists.
- **The assumed close**: Talk to the lead as if you already know they'll accept. *"This is what everyone does. This is just a formality. Let me get your ID and credit card so you can get your value."* No hype — a friendly disposition, almost bored at how regularly people buy.
- **The surprise benefit (optional)**: If someone takes the decoy, throw in a few low- or zero-cost features from the premium tier. *"I'm going to throw this in even though it's part of our premium offer, just because I want you to get great results."* Builds goodwill, overdelivers, and raises the odds they take later upsells.

## Mental Models
- **"Give customers what they want now so you can give them what they need later."** John's philosophy, and the spine of the whole play.
- **The decoy isn't a loss — it's a laboratory.** "You'll learn the best way to deliver your decoy product and maximize upsells from it."
- **If it isn't making money fast, the contrast is too small.** That's the first diagnostic, not the last.

## Anti-patterns
- **A decoy that's too good**: If the gap is small, there's no reason to upgrade.
- **Leaving guarantees on the decoy**: Guarantees belong to the premium tier — they're a contrast lever.
- **Presenting the decoy first by default**: Ask the permission question and lead with premium whenever you legally and ethically can.

## Worked Example
**Where it came from — the $5 VIP tanning pass.** John, a retired mentor, explained the play on drives to his lake house:

> "The beauty of the 5-day pass is everyone thinks they can get tan in 5 days — and they can. But it's never as tan as they want to get. And if they try to speed it up, they'll burn."

When someone came in with the pass, staff asked how tan they wanted to get. The moment they said *a few shades darker*, they got **the turkey talk**:

> "A Thanksgiving turkey takes 3 hours to cook. We all know what happens if you double the temperature to cook it in half the time — you burn it. It takes at least 5 to 10 sessions to get the colour you want without burning. And since you need time between sessions, it always takes more than 5 days."

Then the close: *"Let's just credit your VIP pass toward your first month. Why buy so many $25 day passes when members get unlimited access for just $19.99?"*

**The version that saved the gym.** Years later, fitness leads got too expensive — most operators were barely breaking even. Hormozi remembered the tanning pass: *"Why don't we offer something super cheap to get the leads, but when they come in, make them a crazy offer that costs more but is 100× better? They can still take the cheap thing — we'll just explain they'll get way better results with extra accountability, nutrition, etc."*

| | Decoy | Premium |
|---|---|---|
| Price | **Free** | **$399** |
| Sessions | One per week | Unlimited |
| Coaching | — | One-on-one |
| Personalization | — | Yes |
| Guarantee | — | Get results or repeat the program free |

**Take rate on the premium option: 8 out of 10.**

**Templates across industries:**

| Business | Attraction offer | Decoy option | Premium option |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lemonade stand | Free week of lemonade | Water + powdered lemon + corn syrup | Organic, all-natural, cold-processed imported Italian lemons shipped to your door, plus other flavours |
| Float tank centre | Free 6-week stress release | One float per month + at-home DIY exercises | Two floats per week for 6 weeks, 1-on-1 consulting, journal, sleep routine, satisfaction guaranteed |
| Gym | Free 21-day transformation | Group workouts once a day, general nutrition plan, recordings, **no support, no guarantee** | Unlimited workouts, personalized nutrition plan, 1-on-1 accountability, **results guaranteed or another 21 days free** |

## Key Takeaways
1. Advertise the stripped version; sell the premium version in the room.
2. Strip the decoy hard — fewer components, no personalization, no guarantee.
3. Ask "free stuff or lasting results?" to earn permission to lead with premium.
4. Present both, then let them choose out loud which reaches the goal faster.
5. Test all four discount phrasings; they're arithmetically identical and don't convert identically.
6. Use discount rather than free when no-shows are expensive.
7. Not making money fast? Widen the contrast.
