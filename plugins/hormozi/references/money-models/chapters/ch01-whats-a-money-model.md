# Chapter 1: What's a Money Model?

## Core Idea
**A money model is a sequence of offers.** At its core, you find every opportunity to solve a customer's problem and then offer to solve it — which is why money models tend to have many offers in a specific order.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Money Model Definition**: A sequence of offers, ordered so each one arrives at the moment the customer realizes they need it. *"If you offer the right thing when customers realize they need it, you can make as many offers as you like."*
- **The 30-Day Cost Recovery Rule**: Cover the cost of getting a customer **within 30 days**.
  - Why 30 days specifically: *"Any business can get interest-free money for 30 days in the form of a credit card. If you clear your balance before the end of the month, it works just like normal money."* So you can use credit to get a customer, pay it back, and use it again for the next one. Rinse and repeat.
- **The Two Options Every Business Faces**: (1) wait two years to get your cash back and pray you don't run out of money, or (2) **get paid fast and grow as much as you darn well please.** A good money model is option two.
- **The Compounding Growth Math**: If you make customers twice as valuable, get twice as many of them, and get them at twice the speed, your business grows **8× faster**. Triple all three and it grows **27× faster.**

## Key Concepts
- **Bad money models kill businesses**: It costs many businesses more to get somebody to buy a thing than they make in profit off the thing. They lose money getting new customers.
- **Cash constraint**: Most businesses spend too much to get customers and make too little from them. Cash is what buys more customers — so this is the first thing to solve.
- **The virtuous loop**: More offers → people buy them → more money → more customers → and if they pay faster, you acquire faster and stay profitable.
- **"With a $100M money model, you make so much money in the first 30 days that the cost of getting more customers will never be a problem again."** The consequence: you'll be forced to work on everything else in the business just to keep up. *(A problem for another book.)*

## Mental Models
- **Every problem you spot is an offer you haven't made yet.** The discipline isn't inventing products; it's noticing the sequence of problems a customer hits and having something ready at each one.
- **Think in speed of cash, not just size of cash.** A great business with a two-year payback is only great if you already have tons of cash in the bank. Otherwise you run out of money.
- **All businesses already have money models.** The question is never *whether* you have one — it's whether it was designed.

## Anti-patterns
- **Making one offer and stopping**: "Making one offer works better than making none. And making more offers works better than making one."
- **"This won't work for my business"**: Hormozi's explicit reframe — swap the poor person's mantra *"this won't work for my business"* for the rich person's mantra **"how will I make this work for my business?"**
- **Copying a money model verbatim**: "If you just try to copy what they do, you'll be disappointed. To make it work for your business, you have to design your own."
- **Hard selling**: "Hard selling is for weak products. If someone doesn't want something, that's okay. Don't convince someone against their will."
- **Fighting refunds**: "If a customer asks for their money back, give it back. Avoid the headache. And if you made a goof, fix the goof."

## Worked Example
**The rental car.** Hormozi reserved a car; the counter agent walked him through a sequence:

| # | Offer | Problem it solved |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Vehicle upgrade to a roomier pickup | "Big man in a small car" |
| 2 | Late return, no late fees | Checkout timing vs. an evening flight |
| 3 | Premium insurance covering all damage | Worry about dings and scratches |
| 4 | Minimum insurance *(downsell after he declined #3)* | Still needed some coverage |
| 5 | Prepaid fuel, return on empty | Risk of missing the flight while refuelling |

He came for a **$19/day car and left paying $100/day — a 5× difference.** *"They knew everything I wanted and things I didn't even know I would want. And when they offered them, I happily bought them."*

Note the structure: every single offer was framed as **the solution to a problem she named first**. She told him about the problem, then made her solution available. That's the whole mechanic, and it's why car rental brings in billions per month in the United States alone.

## Key Takeaways
1. A money model is a *sequence*, not a product. Order and timing are the design work.
2. Recover the cost of acquiring a customer inside 30 days so you can recycle the same cash.
3. Speed of payback determines how fast you can grow, independent of margin.
4. Value × volume × speed compound multiplicatively — 2× each is 8× growth.
5. Name the problem before offering the solution. That's what makes an offer feel like help.
6. Design your own model; copying someone else's will disappoint.
7. Be transparent, obey advertising law, and never take money from someone who wants it back.
