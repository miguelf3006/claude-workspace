# Chapter 13: Continuity Offers — Keeping People Buying

## Core Idea
Continuity offers keep customers paying month after month. On their own they generate too little cash to acquire customers profitably — so you **run them last in the sequence, sell the bonus rather than the membership, and upsell prepaid blocks** to hit your 30-day profit target anyway.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Three Continuity Plays**:
  1. **Continuity bonus** — give an awesome thing free if they sign up today. Typically the bonus is worth *more than the first continuity payment.*
  2. **Continuity discount** — give products or services free if they commit to buying more over time.
  3. **Waived fee** — charge a large setup fee for month-to-month, and waive it entirely for a term commitment.
- **The Sequencing Rule**: Run all your big-cash attraction, upsell, and downsell offers **first**. Continuity collects a little from the first month's payment. **Then offer people who bought one month a discount on prepaying more months** — this boosts 30-day profit, funds more advertising, and stacks recurring revenue.
- **The Standalone-Price Dial** (how to control what percentage choose continuity): the larger the standalone price relative to the continuity price, the more people pick continuity.

| Target % choosing continuity | Price the standalone offer at |
|---|---|
| 50% | ~1.33× |
| 60% | ~1.6× |
| 70% | ~2× |
| 80% | ~2.33× |
| 90% | ~2.66× |

  *"The exact numbers matter less than the principle."* Corollary: **people pay ~33% more to avoid continuity** — even at 33% more for a one-time purchase, half will take it.
- **The Four Ways to Apply a Continuity Discount**:
  - **Upfront** — free time first, term starts after. Best where contracts are historically enforceable (cell phones, storage, real estate, equipment, anything with collateral). *Skip it if you have high churn; it gets customers but delays cash.*
  - **At the end** — they earn the bonus time by making every payment on time
  - **Evenly spread** — keeps cash flowing while delivering the full discount
  - **After the first one or two payments** — *Hormozi's preference.* Present as first-and-last month, last month upfront, or an activation fee. Collects cash for advertising and delivery, **and confirms the payment method is valid**
- **The Waived Fee Structure**: Startup fee of **3–5× the monthly rate**, waived for a term commitment; payable if they break the term early.
  - *Option A*: one-time $5,000 fee + $1,000 first month, then $1,000/month, cancel whenever
  - *Option B*: **$5,000 waived** if you commit to 12 months at $1,000/month — pay the $5,000 only if you break the commitment
  - **Have them initial that they understand they can quit early by paying the waived fee.**

## Key Concepts
- **No successful continuity business has a standalone membership offer.** They all have other bells and whistles to upsell, because *"nobody wants to make a recurring commitment to something they haven't tried."*
- **Bill weekly, not monthly** — Hormozi calls this *"the highest value per word note in this book. Skip this if you hate money."* There are 12 months in a year but **13 four-week cycles — an 8.3% difference.** Same number of people buy at $100 every 4 weeks as at $100/month, but you make 8.3% more annually. **At 20% margins, that raises annual profit by 41%** for no extra work.
- **The 3% processing fee** — *"Yeah, it's $X plus a 3% processing fee."* Five extra words. *"I've never had anyone not buy because of a processing fee."* At a 10% profit business, adding 3% to the top line adds **30% to profit.**
- **Two forms of payment**: Recurring businesses lose mountains of cash to expired cards and insufficient funds. Fix both by offering to waive the 3%: *"Want to save the processing fee? Give us a second form of payment in case anything happens to the first."* If asked why: *"We only have the processing fee because it costs us man-hours to get new payment information every month. If you save us time, we pass the savings on to you."* **Get ACH if you can** — cheapest to transact besides cash.
- **Extend the term, don't eat into it.** "3 months free with a year" should mean pay 12, get 15 — not pay 9, get 12. Start with extending; you can always feature-downsell a shorter version.
- **Lifetime discount at the churn point**: Find the month your average customer drops off, then advertise a lower rate they *earn* by staying past it. As the date approaches, tell them their new lower rate is right around the corner. *(A rice company sold: one-time price · 5% off subscription · **15% off if you stay subscribed five straight months.** Almost certainly set just beyond where most people cancelled.)*
- **Bulk prepaid upsells**: Offer "buy five months, get one free." **Only 1 in 8 people need to take it to raise 30-day profits by 50%** — that can make or break a money model.

## Mental Models
- **Advertise the bonus, not the membership.** *"'Join my membership program' isn't nearly as compelling as 'get this free valuable thing.'"* Sell the bonus's benefits, let the price anchor shock them, then ask: **"Do you want to know how you can get this for free?"** They always do.
- **Customers stay when leaving costs more than staying.** The entire logic of the waived fee — *"it costs a lot to quit in the beginning, so that keeps them engaged. And once they pass that point, it costs about the same to cancel as it does to stick it out. So they just stick it out."*
- **Bonuses should be things you already have and do.** Two years of past newsletters cost nothing extra. Onboarding is work you'd do anyway — *"you might as well slap a price on it and give it to them as a bonus. If you value it, they will too."*
- **Cross the format line**: physical bonuses on digital products, digital bonuses on physical ones. A hat with a digital membership; live-stream classes with a boxing gym membership. *"This often lowers the cost of getting a customer more than the cost of the bonus. And that's the point."*

## Anti-patterns
- **Unrelated bonuses**: "Don't advertise a free t-shirt to upsell tech services. Advertising a free t-shirt to upsell t-shirt printing makes sense." Wrong bonus, wrong customers.
- **Made-up bonus values**: "Some business owners make up ridiculous values. Don't. It won't anchor the customer and you'll lose trust." Use real prices from things you've actually sold.
- **Hiding the cancellation path**: "If customers have nowhere to complain inside your business, they will definitely complain outside of it. If there's no obvious way to cancel, more people vanish and complain. **Small businesses don't get rich by making stuff hard for their customers.**"

## Worked Example
**The trash man.** A neighbour in an Austin suburb — Ferrari in the garage, backwards hat, flip-flops:

> "I knew from my time working trash there wasn't much competition — big commercial places all went to the same place for their trash needs. So I had a truck, took my credit card, and I gambled. **I went to all the big apartments and said I'd do their trash for a whole year free if they contracted me for the next five years paid.** It worked good enough. They all made me their trash man before I knew it."
> — *"You fronted an entire year?"*
> "Uh-huh. Toughest thing I ever did. No one would invest, not even my family — they all called me crazy. But after that one-year mark passed, **the cash came flooding.** After a few years using that plan, I sold the whole shebang for a pretty penny."

*(Note this is "buy X get Y free" done continuity-style — 1 free : 5 paid, with the discount applied entirely upfront.)*

**The waived fee, from a high-ticket legend** who wanted to work as little as possible:

> "I tell customers they have two options. You can go month-to-month with a big setup fee — it covers the cost of getting you started, but you can leave whenever. **Or if you commit to a year, I'll waive the fee.** And I make the fee huge, so buyers commit to avoid it. I also have them initial that they understand they can quit early if they pay the fee I waived."

**Continuity bonus templates:**

| Business | One-time bonus | Continuity |
|---|---|---|
| Pet food | *"Get every dog toy we've ever made free — an $800 value"* | $59/month shipments, **plus a new dog toy every month as a member** |
| Service | Short-term accelerator that costs $1,000 standalone — **free** | $100/month membership; VIP members get first-in-line event access, longer support hours, better support reps |
| Digital | *"Get all my past 40 newsletters, valued at $880"* | $399/month after a 30-day free trial. **Pay today and lock in a lifetime discount to $299/month**, plus early digital access and a physical copy monthly |

**Bonus stacking script**: Anchor the bonus first → they react to the price → *"Do you want to know how you can get this for free?"* → *"Become a VIP member today and you'll get it all as a free gift for joining. Or you can just buy it for $X — which would you prefer?"* → then stack: *"On top of that, when you become a member you'll get amazing thing one, amazing thing two, amazing thing three"* — naming each individual dollar value.

**The titles bonus**: Give customers titles after 3, 6, or 12 months — silver, gold, diamond, double diamond. A friend of Hormozi's found **her customers cared more about the title than any other bonus** and introduced themselves to her by it. *"If you can't think of something to give them, at the very least you can call them something special."*

**Cancellation policy** — his preference: **make the cancellation fee equal to the discount they received.** Got $600 in discounts by committing? They can pay $600 whenever they want to cancel, which puts them back at the month-to-month rate. Simple to explain.
- **Light terms** → more signups, more leavers. **Harsh terms** → fewer signups, fewer leavers.
- **Always run an exit interview.** Incentivize it by waiving the cancellation fee. *"Some people like to vent. Let them. Get more angry about the problem than they are — they may try to calm you down. Sometimes they save themselves."* If they wanted higher-cost features, **rollover-upsell them into the higher tier.** *"I routinely save a third of customers that agree to exit interviews."*

## Key Takeaways
1. Run continuity last, then immediately upsell prepaid blocks to hit 30-day profit.
2. Advertise the bonus, never the membership.
3. Make bonuses out of things you already have and do, priced honestly.
4. Use the standalone-price ratio to dial what share of customers choose continuity.
5. Bill every four weeks, add a 3% processing fee, and trade the fee for a second payment method.
6. Extend terms rather than consuming them, and set a lifetime discount just past your churn point.
7. Make cancelling easy, price the cancellation at the discount received, and always run the exit interview.
