# Chapter 4: Attraction Offers — Free Giveaways

## Core Idea
Advertise a chance to win a big prize in exchange for contact information. **One person wins the grand prize — everyone else qualifies for a discounted version of it.** Many will enter; many will win.

> ⚠️ **Sweepstakes and giveaways are heavily regulated.** Done wrong they become illegal lotteries. Follow local advertising law and consult counsel. Hormozi is explicit that his description is no guarantee of lawfulness.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Six Steps to Run a Giveaway**:
  1. **Pick a grand prize** — make it the thing you want everyone to buy, and **assign it a monetary value to serve as a price anchor**
  2. **Pick your promotional offer** — your core offer enhanced with a discount, a bonus, or a minor change from the grand prize that ethically justifies a price reduction against the anchor
  3. **Ask for contact information** and other eligibility criteria
  4. **Pick qualifying actions** entrants must take
  5. **Put the giveaway on a deadline** to add urgency
  6. **Announce the grand prize winner** publicly, then contact everyone else privately
- **The Two Question Types** in the entry form:
  - **Eligibility** — are they a fit? *"Do you own a vet clinic?"* Or need-based: *"Why should you be selected?"*
  - **Qualifying actions** — things that increase commitment or spread the giveaway: attending a call or event, making a post, entering a group
- **The Discount Rule of Thumb**: Make your core offer discount equal to **10–30% of your gross margins.**
- **The Three Urgency Points**: urgency to **enter**, urgency to **claim**, urgency to **use**. Deadlines at every stage.
- **The Two-Prize Referral Mechanic**: Give away **two** grand prizes and tell everyone that **if someone they refer wins, they win one too.** This gives them endless entries by referring friends, gets people working together, and has a sneaky benefit — **referrers are invested in the success of their referrals**, which keeps quality high.

## Key Concepts
- **The anchoring math is the whole play.** Entrants know the value of the grand prize, not your retail price. When you present the discounted price against that anchor, it reads as enormous savings.
- **Participation trophy framing**: When you contact non-winners, the reason why can simply be *"I found your answer so compelling I felt obligated to give you something just for entering."*
- **Scarcity by capacity**: Limit by time (7 days), number of entries (5,000), or both. Hormozi matches entries allowed to **the number of people he can actually contact within 7 days.** "Anymore would be a waste."
- **The countdown is a mini product launch**: whisper, tease, shout. Update entrants daily across email, DM, text, and social — how long is left, plus real value: the benefits of the grand prize, why to be excited, and social proof.
- **Grand prizes only work if they're grand.** One portfolio company ran a giveaway with **event tickets** as the prize and barely got interest. They re-ran it with a **$50,000 bundle of equipment from a well-known industry supplier plus their core product for a year** — and it crushed. *"If nobody bites, give away something better, or at least better for the audience."*

## Mental Models
- **Everyone who entered showed interest in your thing.** "And if somebody shows interest in a thing you have to offer, **offer it to them.**" The giveaway is a qualification mechanism disguised as a prize.
- **More entry friction = fewer but better entrants.** The more work you make it to enter, the fewer will enter but the more qualified they'll be. Find your sweet spot.
- **A giveaway explains itself.** If nobody bites, the problem is almost never the mechanics — it's the prize.

## Anti-patterns
- **Not actually awarding the grand prize**: Somebody has to win. Make the prize and the qualifications clear in the rules, and make clear that more than one person can win a prize.
- **An unremarkable grand prize**: The most common failure mode.
- **No downsell on the claim call**: Some won't or can't buy the promotional offer even discounted.
- **Uncapped entries**: More leads than you can call in a week is waste, not success.

## Worked Example
**Where it came from.** A fitness certification business advertised a **full-ride scholarship** to their entire program. People applied with contact information and answered questions like *"Why should we pick you for the full ride?"*

The twist: they could only give away one full ride — **but as many partial scholarships as they wanted.** So they made a public spectacle of the full-ride winner, then **called everyone else** to tell them they'd received a partial scholarship. Callers were thrilled; **most joined on the spot.** They never knew the actual price of the program — only the value of the full ride — so the discounted price read as huge savings. The company had to **cap entries** to service the signups.

**The anchoring math, worked:**
- Grand prize advertised value: **$5,000**
- Normal retail price: **$2,000**
- Everyone else's price: **$1,800** (a 10% discount off retail)
- What they're told: *"$5,000 in value for $1,800"*
- **A 10% discount reads as a 64% difference in cost-to-value.**

**Templates across industries:**

| Business | Grand prize | Promotional offer |
|---|---|---|
| Dentist | Free set of invisible braces ($6,000 retail) | $2,000 gift card for braces |
| Physical products | Free year of organic dog food ($1,000 retail) | $300 gift card, usable only with a one-year subscription |
| Services | Free one-year package ($5,000 retail) | $2,000 voucher toward a one-year service agreement |
| Consulting | 16-week turnaround ($12,000 retail) | $6,000 partial scholarship |

**The claim call structure** (with a built-in downsell): Open by telling them they qualified for two prizes and you'll help them find which makes most sense. Present the **promotional offer first** — the discount on the grand-prize thing. If they take it, great. If not, **offer the same percentage discount on any other product** that fits them. If you have recurring revenue, apply their discount over the longest period they'll agree to, then set the subscription to bill automatically at normal rates once the discounted period ends.

## Key Takeaways
1. The grand prize should be the thing you want everyone to buy, with a stated value that anchors price.
2. Non-winners are the business model. The winner is the advertisement.
3. Set 10–30% of gross margin as the discount and present it against the anchored value.
4. Deadlines at three points: enter, claim, use.
5. Cap entries at the number of people you can personally contact within the claim window.
6. Two prizes plus a referral rule multiplies entries and raises lead quality.
7. If it flops, the prize wasn't grand enough. Fix that before anything else.
