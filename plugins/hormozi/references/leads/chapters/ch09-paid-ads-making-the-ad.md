# Chapter 9: Run Paid Ads I — Making the Ad

## Core Idea
Paid ads rent eyeballs. **Reach is guaranteed; getting your money back isn't** — so paid ads is a game of *efficiency*, not reach. You start with the whole world as a haystack and narrow until a high percentage of who sees the ad are needles.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Four Problems Paid Ads Create**: (1) knowing where to advertise, (2) getting the right audience to see it, (3) making the best ad for them to see, (4) getting permission to contact them.
- **The Four Platform Criteria** (step 1): You've **used it and gotten value from it** as a consumer · you can **target** people interested in your stuff · you know how to **format ads specific to the platform** · you have the **minimum spend** to place an ad.
  - *Where to start:* look at where competitors place ads. "If they can make it work, so can you. **Replicate before you iterate.**"
- **Two Targeting Methods** (step 2): (a) **lookalike audiences** — upload a list; bigger and higher-quality lists produce more responsive lookalikes. Build the list in quality order: current and previous customers → warm outreach list → cold outreach leads → later, people who engaged with your ads but didn't buy. (b) **Filters** on top — age, income, gender, interest, time, location.
- **The Three Chunks of Every Ad** (step 3): **Call out + Value + CTA.**
- **Verbal Call Outs** (four kinds):
  1. **Labels** — words putting people into a group: features, traits, titles, places. *Clark County moms · gym owners · remote workers.* The more local, the better: Americans → Texans → Dallas residents → Irving residents.
  2. **Yes questions** — answering "yes, that's me" qualifies them. *"Do you wake up to pee more than once a night?"*
  3. **If-then statements** — *"If you run over $100,000/month in ads, we can save you 20% or more."*
  4. **Ridiculous results** — bizarre, rare, out of the ordinary. *"Massage studio books out 2 years in advance. Clients furious."*
- **Non-Verbal Call Outs** (three kinds): **contrast** (bright over dull, attractive over plain, moving over still) · **likeness** (show the labels visually — *"quack like a duck": if you want plumbers, dress like a plumber, talk like a plumber, be in a plumbing environment*) · **the scene** (show the yes-question — a person tossing and turning calls out sleep trouble; a room stacked to the ceiling calls out too much junk).
- **The What / Who / When Framework** (step 3, value): Know **eight key things** about your offer — how it fulfils each of the four value-equation elements *and* how it helps avoid each of their opposites (dream outcome ↔ nightmare, perceived likelihood ↔ past failures, and so on). **What** = the eight elements. **Who** = whose perspective experiences them, especially status with people they know. **When** = past, present, future.
- **CTA** (step 3): Spell it out. Click this button. Call this number. Reply with yes. Scan this QR code. Make phone numbers easy, buttons obvious, websites short and memorable — `acquisition.com/training`, not `alexprivateequityfirm.com/free-book-course2782`.
- **Landing page** (step 4): Keep it simple so it's easy to test — focus on the words and the image. **Make the landing page match the ad**; you promised a benefit, so carry the same look and language over. "You want a continuous experience from click to close."

## Key Concepts
- **The cocktail party effect**: In a loud ballroom, one sound cuts through — your name. Call outs harness this. They range from hyper-specific (someone says your name → only you look) to non-specific (a dropped tray of dishes → everyone looks). Aim for *specific enough to get the right people, broad enough to get many of them.*
- **The first 5 seconds**: "My advertising became **20× more effective** when I focused the majority of my effort on the first 5 seconds." The purpose of each second of the ad is to sell the next second — and the headline is the first sale.
- **Consistency principle (Cialdini)**: Remind them of the action they just took, then show how the next action aligns. *"Now that you just did A, you need to do B to get the most out of A."*
- **Long vs. short ads**: The only difference is how many angles you have room to cover. Keep the call outs and CTAs identical; add or remove middle angles by platform.
- **Mascots**: They don't age, never ask for more money, never take days off — Mickey Mouse, the Geico Gecko, Tony the Tiger, the Michelin Man. Also: a spokesperson who isn't you makes the business less dependent on you and therefore more sellable.

## Mental Models
- **Puddle → pond → lake → ocean.** Advertise profitably in a small audience first; expand outward. Efficiency drops as the audience grows, but total profit rises — $1,000 → $10,000 (ratio 10:1) becomes $100,000 → $300,000 (ratio 3:1, profit $200,000). *"We want the audience as big as possible while still turning a profit."*
- **"If an ad isn't profitable, most of the time it's because the right people never saw it."**
- **Turn ads into a curriculum.** Hormozi doesn't skip or mute ads and buys no ad-free subscriptions — he watches for how each one calls out, presents value, and asks for action. *"My goal is that for the rest of your life, when you see an ad, you turn up the volume."*
- **You don't need to become a copywriting expert.** "Get good enough to grow. If you call out the right people and have an amazing offer, you barely need any copy to begin with."

## Anti-patterns
- **Over-filtering a local audience**: A local market is already ~0.1% of a nation. Be as specific as possible *but no further*, or you burn the list fast.
- **Frankenstein experiences**: Ad promises one thing, landing page looks and sounds different. People waste money until they remember this.
- **Perfecting copy and creative before knowing the numbers**: "Efficiency matters more than creativity."

## Worked Example
**The infinite-ads production system** — "one of the highest ROI tips I can give you":
- Record **~10 new ads every week**
- Record **30+ opening sentences or questions** — the 5-second call outs people consume before deciding to keep watching
- 30 call outs × 10 ads = **300 variations in a matter of hours**
- Once you know the best call out, apply it to all ads

**Building the ad from the What/Who/When framework** (weight loss):
- *"how fast* **(what — time delay)** *they fit into that suit* **(what)** *your wife loves* **(who — status with spouse)** *that didn't fit but does now* **(when — future)**"
- *"how their kids* **(who)** *got more interested in eating healthy and tagging along during workouts* **(what)** *month after month* **(when)**"
- *"how they catch a look at themselves in a reflection in the mall* **(who — self)** *in a few months* **(when)** *and realize stuff actually fits me in this store* **(what)**"

**The ad-library research method:** search "[platform] ad library." If an ad has been running a month or more, assume it's profitable. Take notes on the call outs, how they illustrate value elements, and their CTAs — the words used and how they're demonstrated. **Break down ~50 ads** and you have a massive head start.

**The paid ads daily checklist:**

| Field | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who | Yourself |
| What | Your offer |
| Where | Any platform / audience you can buy access to |
| To whom | Target audience or lookalike audience |
| When | Every day, 7 days/week |
| Why | Get engaged leads to sell |
| How | Call out + What/Who/When + CTA |
| How much | Learning budget, then reverse from sales goal |
| How many | 30+ call outs × 10 ads |
| How long | As long as it takes |

## Key Takeaways
1. Efficiency, not reach — the world will see your ad if you pay enough; the question is how well it works.
2. Pick a platform on four criteria and start where competitors already advertise.
3. Build lookalikes from your best lists first, then layer filters.
4. Every ad = call out + value + CTA. Spend most of your effort on the first 5 seconds.
5. Use verbal *and* non-verbal call outs together when the platform allows.
6. Build value with What/Who/When — especially status with people they know.
7. Match the landing page to the ad exactly; keep it simple so it's testable.
