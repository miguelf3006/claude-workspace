# Chapter 5: Warm Outreach

## Core Idea
One-to-one contact with people who already know you is **the cheapest and easiest way to find people interested in what you sell — and most businesses don't do it.** It is also the most reliable: "As certain as the sun rises and sets, it works."

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Warm Outreach in Ten Steps**:
  1. **Get your list** 2. **Pick a platform** 3. **Personalize your message** 4. **Reach out** 5. **Warm them up** 6. **Invite their friends** 7. **Make them the easiest offer in the world** 8. **Start at the top** 9. **Start charging** 10. **Keep your list warm**
- **The ACA Framework** (step 5 — what to say when they reply):
  - **A**cknowledge what they said, restated in your own words (shows active listening). *"Two kids and you're an accountant."*
  - **C**ompliment them, tied to a positive character trait if you can. *"Wow, so hardworking — managing a full-time career and two kids."*
  - **A**sk another question, steering toward your offer. *Therapy: "Do you get time for yourself?" Fitness: "Do you have time to get workouts in?" Cleaning: "Do you have anyone who helps keep the house tidy?"*
- **The "Do You Know Anybody" Offer** (step 7) — built directly on the value equation, and crucially **not asking them to buy**:
  > "By the way, do you know anybody who is *[their struggles]* looking to *[dream outcome]* in *[time delay]*? I'm taking on five case studies for free because that's all I can handle — I just want some testimonials. I help them *[dream outcome]* without *[effort and sacrifice]*. It works. I even guarantee people get *[dream outcome]* or I work with them until they do. I just had a girl named [X] *[dream outcome]*, even though she described the same struggle you have. I also had a guy who *[dream outcome]*, and it was his first time. I'd just like more testimonials to show it works across different scenarios. **Does anyone you like come to mind?**"
  > *(If no:)* "Huh. Well — does anyone you **hate** come to mind?" *(breaks the awkwardness)*
- **The Compressed Version** (for texts, DMs, short emails): *"I help [ideal customer] get [dream outcome] in [time period] without [effort and sacrifice]"* + a perceived-likelihood element.
- **The 11 Ways to Increase Perceived Likelihood of Achievement**: proof you've done it (your story) · proof of people just like them (testimonials) · sheer volume of happy reviews · certifications, degrees, legitimate third-party accreditation · numbers, stats, research · experts vouching · a new/unique characteristic they haven't failed with before · celebrity endorsement · a guarantee (skin in the game) · how well you describe their current pain ("he really gets me") · demonstrating the outcome live or on a recording.
- **The Increase-Every-Five Rule** (step 9): Once people start referring, stop working free. Swap "free" for **80% off for the next five**, then 60%, then 40%, and so on — raising 20% every five until you find your sweet spot. It doubles as honest urgency, because prices genuinely go up.
- **The Prepaid + Guarantee Twist**: Offer the guarantee **only** to people who pay upfront. *"People who invest upfront are more committed, and as a result we're able to guarantee their outcomes."* Or Dr. Cashey's wording: **"Would you rather pay less today, or get all your money back?"** — $2,000/month × 3 with no guarantee, vs. $6,000 upfront *with* one. Most take the upfront option.
- **The Nine-Word Email** (Dean Jackson, step 10): *"Are you still looking to [four-word desire]?"* No images, no preamble, no links — just the question. "This message is money for getting leads to engage. It's among the first things I do when I invest in a new business."

## Key Concepts
- **Everyone has a list**: Phone contacts + every email account you've ever used + every social profile's followers/connections. Add them up. For many people that's their first 1,000 leads.
- **Reach out to 100 people per day**, up to three times each — once a day for three days, or until they respond, whichever comes first (once per week for physical mail).
- **The Hinge Method for referrals**: Get a three-way introduction. Grab the customer's phone, take a photo of the two of you, text it to the referral *and* to your own number. Virtually: screenshot the video call and do the same. At minimum, get a three-way conversation going that *they* initiate.
- **Hidden costs**: The most expensive part of what you sell usually isn't the price — it's the time, effort, and sacrifice to get results (the bottom of the value equation). **If you can't give it away free, your free stuff is too expensive.**
- **Learning, not earning**: "The objective right now is to learn, not to earn." Your stuff isn't valuable yet.

## Mental Models
- **Rip off the band-aid.** The first reach-out is hardest and slowest; the second takes minutes; the third, seconds. *"Everything must be hard before it can be easy."*
- **"Yes gives me opportunity. No gives me feedback. Either way, I win."** Rack up failures fast to pay down your "no tax."
- **Free customers make money three ways**: they convert, they refer, and their testimonials bring in buyers.
- **You didn't ask them to buy.** Asking "do you know anybody" means you're never pushy — and of those who say yes, most say *they* are interested.

## Anti-patterns
- **Leading with the ask**: Personalize first. "You haven't asked for anything. You're just checking in and providing value. Relax."
- **Trying to look advanced when you're not**: "People aren't dumb. Just be honest and keep it simple."
- **Never raising prices**: Free forever means you never learn whether you're good. Referrals are the litmus test that you are.
- **Taking "no" without asking why**: Ask *"What would I have to do to make it worth it for you to continue?"* Their answer is either a sale or ammunition for the next person.

## Worked Example
**Hormozi's actual first business.** He quit his job after realizing a Harvard MBA — $150,000 in loans and two years without income — wouldn't help him start a business as much as *starting a business* would. He set up an LLC named **Impetus Group**; the first person he told said *"Impotence?"* He renamed it immediately.

Then, not knowing he was doing warm outreach, he called, texted, and messaged people he knew:

> "Hey, do you know anyone who's trying to get into shape? I'm training people for free for 12 weeks. On top of that, I'll make them a custom nutrition plan and grocery list. All they have to do is donate to a charity of their choice and let me use their testimonial."

**Six people said yes** — two high-school friends, one college friend, and three referrals. After the 12-week charity period he asked them to pay *him* instead. Nobody minded. He asked for referrals: **another five or six clients**, all paying directly. That business made **~$4,000/month** and replaced his job income.

His infrastructure: a tax ID, a bank account, a way to take payments, and a way to communicate with people. *"That last part is the most important. It's how you get leads."*

**The Buffett anecdote on hidden costs.** Before Warren Buffett became the greatest investor of our time, he offered to work for Benjamin Graham **for free**. Graham's response: **"You're overpriced."** The most expensive thing about hiring Buffett wasn't the paycheck — it was the time to train him. Graham would effectively be working for Buffett. Your early customers are in the same position: they're training you for free, so minimize that cost to them. *(Buffett eventually got Graham to take the free offer anyway.)*

**Why make the first five free on every new product or service:** (1) you get reps making offers and calm your nerves; (2) you probably suck for now, and people are far more forgiving when they haven't paid; (3) because you suck, you need to suck less — and you do that by doing more, with a few guinea pigs to work the kinks out.

## Key Takeaways
1. You already have a list. Total your phone, email, and social contacts before claiming otherwise.
2. 100 personalized reach-outs per day, up to three touches each.
3. Use ACA to make a real conversation, then transition to the offer at 3–4 exchanges.
4. Ask "do you know anybody" — never "do you want to buy."
5. Make the first five free; start charging the moment referrals begin, then raise 20% every five.
6. Offer the guarantee only to prepayers, and let them choose "pay less today or get all your money back."
7. Keep the list warm with regular value, then probe with the nine-word email.
