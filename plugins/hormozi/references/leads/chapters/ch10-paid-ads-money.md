# Chapter 10: Run Paid Ads II — Money Stuff

## Core Idea
All advertising works; the only difference is *how well*. Paid ads come down to one ratio — **LTGP:CAC** — and the target is **greater than 3:1**. Below that, businesses struggle to scale. Above it, they take off.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Three Phases of Scaling Ads**:
  1. **Track money** — set up accurate tracking *before* spending a dollar. Without it, "it would be like going to a casino and playing your favorite game for as long as you felt like it rather than for as long as you could afford it."
  2. **Lose money** — "I prefer to call it investing in a money-printing machine." You pay first, so the account goes down before it comes up. **Lose often but small; win rarely but huge** — because you know when to shut off and when to hit the gas.
  3. **Print money** — once you're making back more than you spend, spend as much as you can.
- **The Test Budget Rule**: Budget **2× the cash you collect from a customer in 30 days** (not LTGP) to test a new ad. If you make $100 in 30-day cash, let the ad spend to $200 before shutting it off — as long as it's producing leads. **If it produces no leads at all before 1× 30-day cash, shut it off.**
- **Reverse-Engineer the Budget from the Goal**: Once ads break even or better, stop asking "how much should I spend?" and ask **"how many customers do I want / can I handle?"** If you can handle 100 customers and they cost $100, spend $10,000 — then **pad 20%** because ads get less efficient as they scale → $12,000/30 days = **$400/day**. "If the number terrifies you, then you're doing it right. Trust the data."
- **LTGP:CAC** — the efficiency measure.
  - **LTGP (lifetime gross profit)** = all the money a customer ever spends on your stuff **minus** all the money it takes to deliver it. Buy at $15, cost $5 → $10 gross profit; 10 purchases → $150 revenue, $50 cost, **$100 LTGP**.
  - Gross profit matters because it's the actual money you use to acquire customers, pay rent, cover payroll, and run the business.
  - **Target: > 3:1.** "Every business I invest in that struggles to scale has at least one thing in common — their LTGP:CAC was less than 3:1."
- **Ads Problem or Business-Model Problem?** Use **industry average CAC** as the guide:
  - CAC **below 3× industry average** → your ads are fine. Work on the **business model** (raise LTGP).
  - CAC **above 3× industry average** → work on your **advertising**.
  - The surprising finding: *"The cost to acquire customers between competitors in the same industry is much closer than you'd think. The difference between winners and losers is how much they make off each customer."*
- **Client-Financed Acquisition**: "Figure out a way to get your customers to pay you back in the first 30 days so you can recycle your cash to get more customers." With that solved, "efficiency out of the way, creativity is your only limit."

## Key Concepts
- **Good enough is good enough**: "If you want a $100 million leads machine, you just need to get it good enough to scale."
- **Don't confuse a sales problem with an advertising problem**: If engaged leads have the problem you solve *and* the money to spend, and they're not buying — your ads work fine. You have a sales problem.
- **Your best free content makes your best paid ads**: Nine times out of ten, a free piece that generates sales or performs unusually well makes a great ad. Same for **UGC** — customer testimonials and reviews that perform as content often make killer ads, at no extra work.
- **"If you say you suck at something, you will probably suck at it."** Hormozi said "I'm not techy" for four years, then reversed it in four hours of concentrated effort.

## Mental Models
- **Think of paid ads as a casino where enough skill makes you the house.**
- **Measure returns over a long horizon, not next week.** One business took a year to get paid ads profitable — then made a year's worth of wasted money back in the following month. *"Can you think of anything more valuable than a machine that prints money? It would be unreasonable for it to be cheap or easy."*
- **Do paid ads last.** Skills from the other three transfer here, and paid ads cost money — money you'll have if you do the other three first.

## Anti-patterns
- **Spending before tracking is installed.**
- **Killing ads too early or letting them run too long**: He wasted money both ways before landing on the 2× 30-day-cash rule.
- **Giving up six inches from gold**: A portfolio company spent 12 weeks and $150,000, got the right leads on the phone, and the owner declared advertising didn't work. The ads were great; the *sales* were bad. The misdiagnosis cost an estimated **$30 million in enterprise value**.
- **Blaming ads for a bad business model**: "They often think they have crappy ads (high CAC), when in reality they have a crappy business model (low LTGP)."

## Worked Example
**The 10-ad portfolio math** — why you must scale winners hard:

| | |
|---|---|
| Spend $100 on each of 10 ads | $1,000 total |
| 9 lose everything | −$900 |
| 1 returns $500 on $100 | +$400 |
| **Net** | **−$500** |

Most people stop here and see a $500 loss. *"But not us. We see a winner."* Then you **100× down** on the winner: spend $10,000, make $50,000. You still lost nine times — the one win was enormous. **"You might lose 9 or 99 times in a row before you win big."**

**The LTGP:CAC diagnostic in two extremes** (why business model dominates):
- Make **$1 billion** per customer → you could spend $999 million to acquire one and still clear a million. No matter how crappy your ads, you'd win.
- Make **one cent** per customer → you must acquire for under a penny. Even with the best ads in the world, you'd fail.

## Key Takeaways
1. Track before you spend. Everything else depends on it.
2. Expect to lose money in phase two; lose small and often, win rarely and huge.
3. Test budget = 2× 30-day cash collected; kill at 1× if there are zero leads.
4. Once profitable, reverse the budget from your customer goal and pad 20%.
5. Target LTGP:CAC above 3:1. Below it, you won't scale.
6. Use industry-average CAC to decide whether to fix ads or fix the business model.
7. Aim for client-financed acquisition — customers paying you back within 30 days.
8. Run paid ads last, after the other three channels have built skill and cash.
