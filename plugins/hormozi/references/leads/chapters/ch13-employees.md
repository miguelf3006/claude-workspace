# Chapter 13: Lead Getters — Employees

## Core Idea
Getting other people to do the lead-getting is what converts a risky job into a **sellable asset**. And getting employees uses the exact same Core Four you already use to get customers — just aimed at a different audience.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Internal Core Four**: The methods for getting employees mirror the methods for getting customers, one for one.

| Getting customers | Getting employees |
|---|---|
| Warm outreach | Asking your network |
| Cold outreach | Recruiting |
| Post content | Posting job openings |
| Paid ads | Promoting job postings |
| Customer referrals | Employee referrals |
| Affiliates | Affiliations, guilds, list-servs |
| Agencies | Staffing firms |
| Employees | Employees (unchanged) |

- **The 3 Ds of Training — Document, Demonstrate, Duplicate**:
  1. **Document** — write the steps exactly as you do them. Have trusted observers watch and document, or record yourself doing it multiple ways across multiple shifts so you can watch as an observer rather than breaking flow. Then run your next work block **following only the checklist**. If you can do an A+ job on your own directions, that's draft one.
  2. **Demonstrate** — do it in front of them, walking through the checklist step by step. Any point where they stop you or slow down → adjust the checklist. That's draft two.
  3. **Duplicate** — they do it in front of you, following the same checklist. If the checklist is right, the outcome matches. If it's off, you find out fast.
- **The Employee Lead Math**: `total payroll ÷ total engaged leads = cost per engaged lead`. Then `cost per engaged lead × leads per customer = CAC`, and compare to LTGP.
- **The Single Diagnostic Question** (which employees to focus on): *"Do my engaged leads have the problem I solve and the money to spend?"*
  - **No** → they're not qualified → **advertising problem**
  - **Yes, and they're buying, but there aren't enough** → **advertising problem**
  - **Yes, qualified, but not buying** → **sales problem**
  - *"Don't fire your sales guy if you've got advertising problems. And don't fire your advertising employees if you've got a sales problem."*

## Key Concepts
- **The clarity standard**: *"If you vanish tomorrow, could a stranger get the results you get if they only followed your checklist?"*
- **If they get it wrong, we got it wrong.** If a step needs explaining, the step is too complicated — or, more likely, several steps got crammed into one.
- **Competence vs. performance**: They can know exactly what to do and not be good at it yet. If so, the instructions are fine and they need reps. *Slow, then smooth, then fast.*
- **Train direction-following over results**: If they follow directions and get the wrong result, you know it's the directions — and you have far more control over that.
- **You get rich from what you make. You become wealthy from what you own.**

## Mental Models
- **Better beliefs about talent** (replacing the old ones):
  - Old: *"If you want it done right, you've got to do it yourself." / "Nobody can replace me."*
  - New: **"If you want it done right, get someone to spend all their time doing it." · "If I can do it, someone else can do it better." · "Everyone is replaceable, especially me."**
- **"I could do anything better than any of my employees, but I couldn't do everything better than all my employees."**
- **Replace yourself as soon as you can, then make yourself useful somewhere else.**
- **Reward what you want more of.** "Learning a new skill is punishing enough. We don't need to add to it."

## Anti-patterns
- **Competing with your own employees**: "The more I tried to out-compete my employees, the more distracted I became and the worse my business got."
- **Blaming the team you hired and trained**: "It was my fault they sucked."
- **Taking over when they mess up**: Pause, step back, let them try again. Fast feedback cycles teach faster.
- **Multi-point feedback**: One piece of feedback at a time, practice until right, then move to the next step.
- **Punishing mistakes during training**: Avoid penalties of any type while they're learning.

## Worked Example
**The $10 million difference.** Two businesses, both $5M revenue and $2M profit:

| | Scenario 1 | Scenario 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Runs without you? | No | Yes |
| What it is | A risky job | A valuable asset |
| Your time | Consumed | Free to invest, buy other businesses, take a vacation |
| Value to a buyer | ~Zero | **$10M+** (especially if climbing) |

*"If you have an asset that makes millions of dollars without you, then somebody else could use it to make millions without them. In other words, your business is now a good investment."*

**Hormozi's own numbers at acquisition.com** (at time of writing): **~30,000 engaged leads/month**, with **no paid ads and no outreach**. The content team costs about **$100,000/month**, giving a cost of roughly **$3.33 per engaged lead**. They make far more than $3.33 per lead, so it's profitable.

**A generic worked calculation:**
- $100,000 payroll ÷ 1,000 leads = **$100 per engaged lead**
- 1 in 10 engaged leads converts → **$1,000 CAC**
- $4,000 LTGP ÷ $1,000 CAC = **4:1** ✓

**The three-week rule:** Most entry-level advertising jobs aren't complex — they take grit more than skill. If you train someone properly and they're still below expectations three weeks in, cut them. After training your first few people this way, you've worked the kinks out of that job and it's smooth from there.

**Retraining trigger:** Whenever there's a major dip from normal performance, retrain the team. Usually they stopped doing an important step because nobody told them it was important. Find the step, then reward people for following it going forward.

## Key Takeaways
1. Advertise for employees the same way you advertise for customers — the Core Four maps one-to-one.
2. Train with Document → Demonstrate → Duplicate, and fix the checklist rather than the person.
3. The standard: a stranger following your checklist gets your results.
4. Measure lead-getting payroll as cost per engaged lead, then CAC, then against LTGP.
5. Use the qualified/buying question to tell an advertising problem from a sales problem before firing anyone.
6. A business that runs without you is worth an order of magnitude more than one that doesn't.
7. It's not about brains, it's about guts — and guts is a skill anyone can learn.
