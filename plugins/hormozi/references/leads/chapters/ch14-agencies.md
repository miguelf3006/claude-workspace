# Chapter 14: Lead Getters — Agencies

## Core Idea
Don't hire an agency to do it forever. **Hire an agency to learn how they do it**, with a purpose and a deadline — then move to consulting once your team beats them.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Two Legitimate Uses for an Agency**: (1) **learning new methods**, (2) **learning new platforms**. "They've already made the big mistakes. So instead of wasting time figuring it out myself, I skip straight to the make-money part."
- **The Opening Script** (set the terms up front):
  > "I want to do what you do in my business, but I don't know how. I'd like to work with you for **6 months** so I can learn how you do it. Plus, **I'll pay extra for you to break down why you make the decisions you do** and the steps you take to make them. Then, after I get a good idea of how it all works, I'll start training my team on it. And once they can do it well enough, I'd like to change to a **lower-cost consulting arrangement** so you can still help if we run into problems. **Are you opposed to this?**"
  - Most agencies are not opposed. If it doesn't work for them, move on — but be willing to negotiate. "At some price, it's worth it for both of you."
- **The Two-Agency Ladder**: Hire one **good-enough** agency to learn the ropes of a new platform, then a **more elite** agency (Hormozi paid 4×) to learn how to maximize it. Once your videos beat their videos, drop to consulting only.
- **The Ten Signs of a Good Agency**:
  1. Somebody you know got good results working with them
  2. Prominent companies got good results with them
  3. **A waiting list** — demand exceeding supply is a signal
  4. A clear sales process that sets realistic expectations. No funny business
  5. **No short-term hacks** — they keep the talk on long-term strategy, with clear timelines for setup, scaling, and results
  6. They tell you exactly what they need from you, when, and how they'll use it
  7. They suggest a regular meeting schedule and offer several ways to update you
  8. Updates in simple terms with clear tracking, so you can compare costs to results
  9. **They make a good offer** — run the value equation on them: dream outcome (is it what you want?), perceived likelihood (how many people like you have they gotten there?), time delay (how long?), effort and sacrifice (what do they require of you, what will you give up, can you sustain it?)
  10. **They're expensive.** All good agencies are expensive, but not all expensive agencies are good.

## Key Concepts
- **The agency decay cycle** (what happens when you play their game):
  1. They get you excited about the leads → 2. Onboarding that feels valuable → 3. Their best senior rep is assigned → 4. You see results → 5. **The senior rep moves to the newest customer** → 6. A junior rep takes over → 7. Results suffer → 8. You complain → 9. The senior rep returns occasionally to make you feel better → 10. Results still suffer, you cancel → 11. You find another agency and repeat.
- **You only get a fraction of an agency's attention**, so your results get worse every time they sign new clients. Meanwhile your own team gets better because they're on you full time. **Compare your team's results to the agency's until you beat them.**
- **Talk to 5–10 agencies before buying.** At first you learn new things; over time the difference between the good and bad ones becomes obvious. If one doesn't fit but you like the people, **ask for a referral** — a good agency in one specialty will send you to good agencies in another. *"Those are some of my favorite referrals."*
- **No money → no agency.** Good agencies cost money. If you don't have it, learn by trial and error. Everyone starts that way.

## Mental Models
- **Buy the skill, not the service.** "Hiring an agency is all about investing in important skills you can't really learn anywhere else — unless you go through all the trial and error yourself. And if you did, you'd lose the time and attention you could have used to learn the other stuff that scales your business."
- **Be an informed customer.** Being informed helps both sides.
- **Some people never want to learn, and for those people agencies are great.** Hormozi's approach is for people who do.

## Anti-patterns
- **Believing you'll never have to learn this stuff**: The lie that starts the decay cycle.
- **Hiring on the basis of their ads**: "If you only know about an agency from their paid ads or cold outreach, they probably aren't as good as the ones who rely solely on word of mouth — and the best ones do."
- **Signing without a stated exit**: A relationship with no purpose and no deadline becomes a permanent expense.

## Worked Example
**The $750/hour lesson that started it all.** After a failing stretch, Hormozi asked an agency owner to simply show him how he'd run ads on his account.

> "No. My time's not for sale."
> — *"What sort of arrangement could we come to?"*
> "Fine. **$750 an hour.**"
> — *"And for $750 an hour, you'll sit down and show me how you'd run my ads? And I'd be the one doing everything — you'll walk me through what to do and look over my shoulder as I do it, then explain why you do it that way?"*
> "Yeah. I mean, if you want to pay me $750 an hour, we can do whatever you want. It's your money."

He paid **$3,000 for the first four hours up front**, met one hour a week, came with notes and questions, did the homework between calls, and **recorded and re-watched every call.**

| Calls | What happened |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | The agency owner drove; Hormozi watched |
| 3–4 | Hormozi in the driver's seat |
| 5–6 | "It clicked" — he understood how decisions were made and what data was tracked |
| 7–8 | He no longer needed the help |

*"I had learned how to run paid ads — at least on Facebook — like a pro. And if I had to guess, it was because I learned it from a pro."*

**The YouTube application of the same principle:** he hired **two** agencies — one good-enough agency to keep him committed to making videos while they did legwork on the platform, and a second at **4× the price** to teach the in-depth ideas behind making the best content possible. Once their own videos beat the agency's videos, they dropped to consulting only.

*(Author note: Hormozi still owns equity in agency software, so he isn't against agencies — this is simply how he's had success with them. Large companies do use large agencies; that's not who the book is for. For most people, $10,000–$100,000 on an agency is a significant cost.)*

## Key Takeaways
1. Use agencies to learn new methods and new platforms, not to outsource forever.
2. State the purpose and deadline up front, and pay extra for the *why* behind decisions.
3. Ladder up: good-enough agency for the basics, elite agency to maximize.
4. Score agencies against the ten signs before signing; expensive is necessary but not sufficient.
5. Talk to 5–10 agencies and ask non-fits for referrals.
6. Benchmark your team against the agency, and cancel once you win.
7. Both sides benefit — they get a customer, you get a money-making skill for life.
