# Patterns — $100M Leads

## Build a Lead Magnet (7 steps)
**When to use**: You're struggling to get leads, or your core offer is expensive enough that people want to know more before buying.
**How**: (1) pick a narrow, meaningful problem whose next problem your core offer solves; (2) choose a type — reveal the problem, sample/trial, or one step of a multi-step process; (3) choose a delivery vehicle — software, information, service, or physical product; (4) **test the name** (headline → images → sub-headline); (5) package in every format; (6) make it as good as your paid stuff; (7) clear CTA plus a reason to act now.
**Trade-offs**: 3 types × 4 vehicles = up to 12 versions of one lead magnet. Rotate them to keep advertising fresh and see which wins.

## Warm Outreach (10 steps)
**When to use**: First five clients for any new product or service; re-engagement; new product lines.
**How**: List every contact across phone, email, and social → pick the platform with the most → personalize the greeting → 100 reach-outs/day, up to 3 touches each → reply with **ACA** → after 3–4 exchanges, make the "do you know anybody" offer → make the first five free → work platforms in descending order → start charging once referrals begin (80% off, then 60%, then 40%) → keep the list warm and probe with the nine-word email.
**Trade-offs**: Lowest leads-per-hour of the Core Four, but zero cost and completely reliable.

## The Content Unit
**When to use**: Every piece of content, short or long.
**How**: **Hook** (topic from far past / recent past / present / trending / manufactured + a headline with ≥2 of the eight news components + platform-matched format) → **Retain** (lists, steps, stories — embed unresolved questions) → **Reward** (satisfy the promise completely).
**Trade-offs**: Long form is just more units chained together, which requires more skill. Volume beats polish early.

## Give Until They Ask
**When to use**: Monetizing any audience.
**How**: Give in public, ask in private. Model growing platforms (over-give, under-ask) rather than mature ones. Choose **integrated** asks for long-form or **intermittent** asks for short-form. One CTA at a time. Point asks at the lead magnet by default.
**Trade-offs**: Slower revenue now, faster growth and better customers later. Asking too often shrinks audiences.

## Cold Outreach (3 problems)
**When to use**: You want to grow faster than your warm audience allows, or you want predictable lead flow.
**How**: (1) build a list — software, then brokers, then manual; (2) personalize with 1–3 facts a friend would know, then lead with big fast value; (3) get volume through automated delivery, automated distribution, and more follow-ups in more ways. Re-run the list after 3–6 months.
**Trade-offs**: Labour-intensive and slow to ramp (expect ~a year), but private, reliable, compliance-light, and very hard to copy.

## Make an Ad (call out + value + CTA)
**When to use**: Every paid ad.
**How**: Pick a platform meeting four criteria → target with lookalikes built from your best lists, plus filters → **call out** verbally and non-verbally → build value with **what/who/when** → explicit CTA → simple landing page that matches the ad.
**Trade-offs**: Focus the majority of effort on the first 5 seconds. Record 30+ call outs × 10 ads = 300 variations.

## Scale Paid Ads (3 phases)
**When to use**: Any paid spend.
**How**: **Track** before spending → **lose** deliberately: budget 2× 30-day cash per test, kill at 1× if zero leads → **print**: reverse the budget from your customer goal and pad 20%.
**Trade-offs**: You'll lose more often than you win; the wins must be scaled hard. Judge over quarters, not weeks.

## Diagnose Ads vs. Business Model vs. Sales
**When to use**: CAC feels too high.
**How**: Compare CAC to industry average. Below 3× average → fix the business model (raise LTGP). Above 3× → fix advertising. Then ask: *do my engaged leads have the problem I solve and the money to spend?* No → advertising problem. Yes and buying but too few → advertising problem. Yes and not buying → **sales problem**.
**Trade-offs**: Prevents firing the wrong team. One misdiagnosis in the book cost ~$30M in enterprise value.

## More, Better, New
**When to use**: You're running the Core Four and want more.
**How**: Below $1M profit → more first (Rule of 100). Above → better first. "Better" means one test per week per platform, aimed at the **constraint** (biggest drop-off), logged, with a 4-try/1-month give-up rule. "New" when returns from more and better fall below what a new placement would give.
**Trade-offs**: Testing many things at once teaches you nothing.

## Engineer Referrals
**When to use**: Always, but especially before spending more on ads.
**How**: Build goodwill six ways (sell better customers · set lower expectations · get more people better results · deliver faster wins · keep improving the product · tell them what to buy next), then ask seven ways (one-sided · two-sided · at point of purchase · as a negotiation chip · referral events · ongoing programs · unlockable bonuses).
**Trade-offs**: Referrals are exponential rather than linear; nothing else in the book compounds like this.

## Train a Lead Getter (3 Ds)
**When to use**: Handing any repeatable lead-getting task to an employee.
**How**: **Document** (write it, then run a work block following only your own checklist) → **Demonstrate** (walk them through, amending as they slow down) → **Duplicate** (they do it while you observe; fix the checklist, not the person).
**Trade-offs**: Standard: a stranger following the checklist gets your results. Give three weeks, then cut if still below expectations.

## Use an Agency to Buy Skill
**When to use**: Learning a new method or a new platform, once you can afford it.
**How**: Open with the six-month learn-then-consult script and pay extra for the *why* behind decisions. Ladder from a good-enough agency to an elite one. Score candidates against the ten signs. Benchmark your team against theirs and cancel when you win.
**Trade-offs**: Costs real money; the payoff is a permanent skill instead of a permanent expense.

## Build an Affiliate Army (6 steps)
**When to use**: You want compounding lead flow, or your ticket is too low for cold outreach economics.
**How**: Find affiliates via the six categories → make them a money-making offer → qualify by making them a customer and/or a paid-certified expert → pay from maximum allowable CAC in three tiers (sign-up / activation / sustained performance) → get them advertising → keep them advertising.
**Trade-offs**: The most advanced lead getter, with three convincing problems to solve — but tiered payouts blend below max CAC, improving your ratio while you scale.
