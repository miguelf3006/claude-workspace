## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### The Core Four
Two audience types × two communication modes = the only four ways one person can let other people know about anything.

| | **One-to-one (private)** | **One-to-many (public)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Warm** (gave you permission) | **Warm outreach** | **Post free content** |
| **Cold** (didn't) | **Cold outreach** | **Run paid ads** |

If you aren't getting enough leads, you aren't doing the Core Four with enough **skill** or enough **volume**. Learn them in that order — skills and cash from the earlier methods fund the later ones.

### Lead vs. Engaged Lead
A **lead is a person you can contact.** An **engaged lead shows interest in the stuff you sell** — and engaged leads are the true output of advertising. Optimize for the second, always.

### The Lead Magnet
A **complete solution to a narrow problem**, free or low-cost, valuable enough that you could charge for it, which once solved **reveals the next problem your core offer solves**. Think salty pretzels at a bar.
- **Three types**: reveal their problem (diagnosis) · samples and trials · one step of a multi-step process
- **Four delivery vehicles**: software · information · services · physical products → **up to 12 versions** of one magnet
- **Test the name first**: headline → images → sub-headline. "When you have written your headline, you have spent 80 cents of your advertising dollar" (Ogilvy).
- **Even free stuff has a cost.** If your lead magnet isn't worth their time, it's overpriced.
- A $25 delivery budget on a *free* magnet routinely **cuts CAC by 3×** versus advertising the core offer directly.

### The Content Unit — Hook, Retain, Reward
The content isn't the compounding asset; **the audience is.**
- **Hook** with a topic (far past · recent past · present · trending · manufactured), a headline carrying ≥2 of the eight news components (recency, relevancy, celebrity, proximity, conflict, unusual, ongoing), and platform-matched format
- **Retain** with lists, steps, and stories — embed unresolved questions
- **Reward** by satisfying the promise completely
- Long-form content is just content units linked together. **"There's no such thing as too long, only too boring."**

### Give Until They Ask
Give in public, ask in private. Model growing platforms (over-give, under-ask), not mature ones (~3.5:1). If you give enough, people start asking *you* — and those are the best customers. **One CTA at a time.**

### Cold Outreach — Three Problems
Strangers can't be contacted, ignore you, and aren't interested. Solve in order: **build the list** (software → brokers → manual, and manual is freshest) → **personalize + big fast value** (1–3 facts a friend would know; give away something people *actually pay for*) → **volume** (automate delivery, automate distribution, follow up more ways more times; re-run the list after 3–6 months).
*Expect roughly a year to scale, and track every metric from day one.*

### Paid Ads — Efficiency, Not Reach
Reach is guaranteed; getting your money back isn't. Every ad is **call out + value + CTA**.
- **Call outs** verbal (labels · yes questions · if-then · ridiculous results) and non-verbal (contrast · likeness · the scene)
- **Value** via **what / who / when** — the eight value elements, whose perspective experiences them (especially status), and in what timeframe
- **Three phases**: track money → lose money → print money. Test at **2× 30-day cash**; kill at 1× if zero leads. Once profitable, reverse the budget from your customer goal and pad 20%.

### LTGP:CAC — The One Ratio
**LTGP** = all money a customer ever spends **minus** all money it takes to deliver.
**Target > 3:1.** Every struggling business Hormozi invests in shares one trait: LTGP:CAC below 3:1.
Use industry-average CAC as the referee: **CAC below 3× average → fix the business model. Above → fix the advertising.** *The cost to acquire is far closer between competitors than people think; the difference between winners and losers is how much they make per customer.*

### More, Better, New
- **More** — Rule of 100: 100 primary actions per day for 100 days straight
- **Better** — one test per week per platform, aimed at the **constraint** (the biggest drop-off step), logged, abandoned after 4 tries or 1 month
- **New** — a new placement or method, once more and better stop paying
*Below $1M/yr profit: more first. Above: better first.*
Reject the **size of the pie fallacy** — mistaking your advertising slice for the whole market.

### The Four Lead Getters
Other people who get leads for you: **customers (referrals) · employees · agencies · affiliates.**
- **Referrals** are the only exponential channel. `referrals in − churn out` decides whether you can grow with no advertising. Build **goodwill (value − price)** six ways, then ask seven ways. *Ask **who**, not **if**.*
- **Employees** — get them with the *Internal Core Four*; train with **Document, Demonstrate, Duplicate**. A business that runs without you is worth ~$10M more than the same business that doesn't.
- **Agencies** — hire to *learn a method or platform*, with a purpose and deadline, then drop to consulting once your team beats them.
- **Affiliates** — the most advanced and most compounding. Pay from **maximum allowable CAC** across three tiers: sign-up, activation, sustained performance.

### Open to Goal
Don't commit to doing something a set number of times — commit to working until you hit a set number of **outcomes**, no matter what. Then compress it into the **one-page advertising checklist**: who · what · where · to whom · when · why · how · how much · how many · how long. Five minutes to fill out, and it leaves no room for excuses.
