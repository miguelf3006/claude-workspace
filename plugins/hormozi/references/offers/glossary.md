# Glossary — $100M Offers

**Anti-guarantee** — "All sales are final," justified by a damaging admission about your own exposure. For things that can't be un-seen once shown. (Ch 14)

**Avatar** — The specific person your offer is for and, equally, who it is not for. The "A" in MAGIC. (Ch 4, Ch 15)

**Bonus stack** — The same offer broken into named, individually priced component parts and presented one at a time. (Ch 13)

**Bonus vault** — The library of checklists, recordings, tools, and templates you accumulate over years, kept in your back pocket to close deals. (Ch 13)

**Buying yourself a job** — Working 100 hours/week to avoid working 40, because the business model was never designed for profit maximization. (Ch 1)

**Category of one** — The state where your offer cannot be compared to anything, so the buying decision is your product vs. nothing. Also called *selling in a vacuum*. (Ch 2)

**Cohort cap** — Scarcity mechanism: only accepting X clients per class or cohort. (Ch 11)

**Cohort-based rolling urgency** — Urgency mechanism: regular start dates create honest deadlines. (Ch 12)

**Commodity** — A product available from many places, therefore purchased on price rather than value. (Ch 2, Ch 3)

**Conditional guarantee** — A guarantee with terms attached, ideally tied to the actions required for success. (Ch 14)

**Container word** — The bundling word in an offer name that signals a system: challenge, blueprint, intensive, accelerator, reset. The "C" in MAGIC. (Ch 15)

**Convergent thinking** — Many known variables, one right answer. What school teaches; not how offers get built. (Ch 7)

**Create flow, monetize flow, then add friction** — Generate demand first, get yeses, and only then reduce what you deliver or add friction. (Ch 9)

**Damaging admission** — Publicly disclosing a real vulnerability to make an anti-guarantee credible. (Ch 14)

**Delicate dance of desire** — Keeping supply and satisfaction deliberately below the demand you can generate. (Ch 10)

**Divergent thinking** — Multiple variables, dynamic conditions, many right answers with one far more right. The offer-building mode. (Ch 7)

**Dream outcome** — The feelings and experiences the prospect has envisioned; the gap between current reality and their dreams. Numerator of the value equation. (Ch 6)

**Effort and sacrifice** — All ancillary costs, tangible and intangible, a buyer incurs. Denominator of the value equation. (Ch 6)

**Exploding opportunity** — Urgency mechanism where the opportunity itself decays with time (arbitrage windows, exploding job offers). (Ch 12)

**Fast beats free** — The only thing that beats free is fast. Compete against free by selling speed. (Ch 6)

**Fractal 80/20 demand** — Roughly 1/5th of prospects will pay 5× the price or more. Demand for services is nonlinear. (Ch 10)

**Grand slam offer** — An offer that cannot be compared to anything else available: an attractive promotion, an unmatchable value proposition, a premium price, and an unbeatable guarantee, plus a money model that lets you get paid to acquire customers. (Ch 2)

**Gross profit** — Revenue minus the direct cost of servicing one additional customer. (Ch 2)

**Grow or die** — Maintenance is a myth; a flat business is declining in real terms against a ~9% market. (Ch 1, Ch 3)

**Growth rate cap** — Scarcity mechanism: only accepting X new clients per week. (Ch 11)

**Honest scarcity** — Define the capacity you can genuinely handle, then advertise it. The easiest and most ethical scarcity. (Ch 11)

**Hormozi's Law** — The longer you delay the ask, the bigger the ask you can make. (Ch 10)

**Implicit egotism effect** — We're drawn to things and people that resemble us; why avatar-naming converts. (Ch 15)

**Implied guarantee** — Performance-based structures — rev share, profit share, ratchets, triggers. If I don't perform, I don't get paid. (Ch 14)

**LTV / LTGP (lifetime value / lifetime gross profit)** — Gross profit accrued over a customer's lifetime = gross profit × number of purchases. Excludes indirect costs. (Ch 2)

**MAGIC formula** — Magnet reason why · Avatar · Goal · Interval · Container. Use 3–5 to name an offer. (Ch 15)

**Market efficiency** — The equilibrium where competition has driven margins to the minimum that keeps the lights on. (Ch 3)

**Niche slap** — What you deserve if you keep hopping niches instead of finding a grand slam offer for the one you picked. (Ch 4)

**Normal market** — Growing at roughly the marketplace rate, with unmet needs in health, wealth, or relationships. Fine to build in. (Ch 4)

**Pent-up demand** — Unsatisfied prospects whose desire compounds and who buy faster and higher next time. (Ch 10)

**Perceived likelihood of achievement** — How likely the prospect believes they'll get the result. People pay for certainty. Numerator of the value equation. (Ch 6)

**Price-to-value discrepancy** — The gap between what they pay and what they get. Widen it upward, never downward. (Ch 5)

**Sales-to-fulfilment continuum** — Doing less makes it harder to sell; doing more makes it easy to sell and hard to fulfil. Find the sweet spot. (Ch 9)

**Starving crowd** — A market with demand so far in excess of supply that a mediocre offer still sells out. The highest-order lever. (Ch 4)

**Three ways to grow** — More customers · higher average purchase value · more purchases. Nothing else. (Ch 3)

**Time delay** — Time between purchase and receiving the promised benefit. Denominator of the value equation. (Ch 6)

**Total business cap** — Scarcity mechanism: capping total clients served, creating a waiting list. (Ch 11)

**Trim and stack** — Cut delivery vehicles by cost/value, then bundle the survivors into a named, individually priced stack. (Ch 9)

**Unconditional guarantee** — Pay first, decide later, no questions asked. Strongest and riskiest. (Ch 14)

**Value equation** — (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice). (Ch 6)

**Virtuous cycle of price** — Higher prices → more client investment → better results → better clients → more margin → better delivery. The vicious version runs the other way. (Ch 5)

**10× / 1⁄10th test** — What would you provide at 10× the price? How would you still make them succeed at 1⁄10th? A creativity forcing function. (Ch 9)
