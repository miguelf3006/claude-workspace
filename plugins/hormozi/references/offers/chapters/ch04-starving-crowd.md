# Chapter 4: Pricing — Find the Right Market (A Starving Crowd)

## Core Idea
Market beats offer beats persuasion. A grand slam offer given to the wrong audience falls on deaf ears — you are not creating demand, you are **channeling** it.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Four Market Indicators**: Evaluate any market on (1) **Massive pain**, (2) **Purchasing power**, (3) **Easy to target**, (4) **Growing**.
  - When to use: Before building an offer, and any time a good offer inexplicably isn't converting.
  - How: Score each. Pain sets the price ceiling. Purchasing power gates whether they *can* pay. Targetability decides whether you can reach them at all. Growth is a tailwind or a headwind on every effort.
- **The Order of Importance (Three Levers on Success)**: `Starving crowd (market) > Offer strength > Persuasion skills`.
  - How to read it: A *great* rating on a higher-order lever overpowers everything below it. A *normal* rating passes the buck down. A *bad* rating stops the equation entirely — unless a *great* higher lever nullifies it.
- **The Three Eternal Markets**: Health, wealth, relationships. They always exist because lacking them always causes pain. Find a growing subgroup inside one of them.
- **Niche Slap / Commit to the Niche**: Once you pick a market, stay until you have run enough offers to know. Hopping niches restarts the learning from zero.
- **Niche Pricing Ladder (Dan Kennedy)**: The same content priced by specificity of audience.

## Key Concepts
- **Starving crowd**: The market condition where demand so exceeds supply that a mediocre offer and zero persuasion still sell out.
- **Normal market**: Growing at roughly the same rate as the marketplace, with common unmet needs in health, wealth, or relationships. This is where most readers are, and it is fine.
- **"The pain is the pitch"**: If you can articulate a prospect's pain accurately, they will almost always buy the solution.
- **TAM (total addressable market)**: The ceiling a business can grow into. Only relevant as a reason to broaden *after* ~$10M/yr.
- **Don't be romantic about your audience**: Serve people who can pay you what you're worth. Market choice is always a choice.

## Mental Models
- **Think of market as terrain, not weather.** Growing markets are a tailwind; declining ones a headwind. You choose the terrain — so choose before you start pushing.
- **Use "the pain is the pitch" over feature lists.** Good writing makes the reader *understand*; good persuasion makes the prospect *feel understood*.
- **Riches are in the niches — below $10M.** Under ~$10M/yr, narrowing almost always makes more money. Above it, broaden by going up-market, down-market, or into an adjacent market. If you're at $1M–$3M and think you've capped, you haven't — you just need to be better.
- **Depersonalize failure.** If your offer doesn't work, your offer sucks — not you. You only suck if you stop trying.

## Anti-patterns
- **Fighting a dying market with better execution**: Lloyd had a great product, a zero-risk rev-share offer, and natural sales ability — and still declined, because newspapers shrank 25%/year. Same operator, pivoted to mask manufacturing, hit millions/month in five months.
- **Great market, no purchasing power**: A resume-improvement service sold to unemployed people. Massive pain, easy to target, constantly replenishing — and nobody can pay.
- **Niche-hopping**: Switching from dentists to chiropractors when both are billion-dollar normal markets. You haven't found a grand slam offer yet; you've found an excuse.
- **Serving two masters**: Trying to keep both niches. Pick one.

## Worked Example
**The niche pricing ladder** — identical principles, escalating specificity:

| Product | Price |
|---|---|
| Time management | $19 |
| Time management for sales professionals | $99 |
| Time management for B2B outbound sales reps | $499 |
| Time management for B2B outbound **power tools and gardening** sales reps | $1,997 |

A 100× price change on near-identical content. The mechanism: at each level the buyer thinks *"this was made exactly for me,"* the value can be tied to a concrete outcome (one more deal at $500 commission justifies $499), and the sales message can speak directly to that avatar. Same logic: generic weight loss at $19 vs. a fitness program marketed only to shift nurses at $1,997 — both are "eat less, move more."

Hormozi's own version: Gym Launch targeted "a micro gym owner with 100 members, a signed lease, at least one employee, who wants to help clients lose weight." They turned away personal trainers and online coaches — not because they couldn't help them, but to keep product focus and high-converting messaging.

## Key Takeaways
1. Check the market before blaming the offer, the funnel, or yourself.
2. Score markets on pain, purchasing power, targetability, growth. A bad score on any one is disqualifying; a shrinking market is the most disguised killer.
3. You cannot create demand — only channel it.
4. Pain is proportional to the price you can charge.
5. Under $10M/yr, niche down. Specificity is a pricing lever, not just a positioning one.
6. Commit long enough to run many offers. "If you try 100 offers, I promise you will succeed."
