# Chapter 14: Enhancing the Offer — Guarantees

## Core Idea
**Risk is the single greatest objection to any product or service.** Reversing it is the number one way to increase conversion — Jason Fladlien reported seeing conversion move 2–4× purely from changing the quality of a guarantee.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Four Types of Guarantee**:
  1. **Unconditional** — the strongest. Effectively a trial: pay first, then decide if you like it. Gets many more buyers; you will get some refunds.
  2. **Conditional** — terms attached. The creative category. Make the key actions required for success *part of the guarantee*.
  3. **Anti-guarantee** — "All sales are final," with a compelling reason why.
  4. **Implied** — performance-based: rev share, profit share, triggers, ratchets, bonuses. If I don't perform, I don't get paid.
- **The Conditional Statement Structure**: *"If you do not get **X result** in **Y time period**, we will **Z**."* Without the "or what," a guarantee sounds weak and diluted — which is what most marketers produce.
  - Weak: "We will get you 20 clients guaranteed."
  - Strong: "You'll get 20 clients in your first 30 days **or we give you your money back plus your advertising dollars spent with us.**"
- **Stacking Guarantees**: Combine an unconditional 30-day no-questions-asked with a conditional triple-your-money-back 90-day. Or stack two conditionals around sequential outcomes: "You'll make $10,000 by 60 days and $30,000 by 90 days, as long as you do things 1, 2, and 3." This future-paces the prospect into an outcome they now believe is far more likely, and shifts the burden of risk from them to you.
- **Always hit your guarantee hard — even if you don't have one.** Say it boldly and give the reason why.

## Key Concepts
- **Guarantee math**: If you close 130% as many people and your refund rate doubles from 5% to 10%, you still make 1.23× the money — 23% more, straight to the bottom line. *100 sales – 5 refunds = 95 net. 130 sales – 13 refunds = 117 net. 117 ÷ 95 = 1.23.* For a guarantee to not be worth it, the absolute increase in refunds would have to fully offset the absolute increase in sales — usually requiring a doubling of refunds, which is unlikely. **Don't be emotional, just do the math.**
- **Ideal state**: 100% of your customers *qualify* for a conditional guarantee but have achieved their result and therefore don't want it. Given the choice between a refund and the promised outcome, the vast majority take the outcome.
- **Damaging admission**: The anti-guarantee's engine — show real exposure or vulnerability a consumer immediately understands. "Our product is so exclusive and powerful that once used it cannot be un-used."
- **Name your guarantee**: Not "30-day money-back satisfaction guarantee." Instead: *"In 30 days, if you wouldn't jump into shark-infested waters to get our product back, we'll return every dollar."*

## Mental Models
- **Match their investment psychologically.** If they're making an investment, the guarantee should carry equal or higher perceived commitment — which is why conditional guarantees often beat plain money-back.
- **Bigger, broader guarantees fit low-ticket B2C. Higher-ticket and more business-oriented → more specific, conditional guarantees** that may or may not include refunds.
- **High fulfilment cost → conditional or anti-guarantee.** You'd otherwise eat both the refund and the delivery cost.
- **Reverse their specific fear.** Identify the client's biggest fears, pain, and perceived obstacles — what they don't want to happen if they pay you — then reverse that into the guarantee. Include time, emotion, and outside costs.

## Anti-patterns
- **Guarantees as cover for a weak product or sales team**: It will backfire into lots of refunds. Guarantees are *enhancers* — they can magnify an offer's attraction but cannot make a business.
- **Guarantee-driven buyers**: People who buy *because of* the guarantee can become terrible customers — unwilling to do the work. Tying the guarantee to required actions helps all parties.
- **Piling on conditions**: The more conditions you add to an unconditional guarantee, the faster it loses its teeth.

## Worked Example
**Hormozi's weight-loss guarantee** — used with a best-case/worst-case close:

> "I'm not guaranteeing you'll hit this goal in 6 weeks — I can't eat the food for you. But I am guaranteeing you'll get $500 worth of value and service from us. If you don't feel we gave you that level of service, **I'll write you a check the day you tell me we suck.**
> Best case: you get the body of your dreams and we give you all your money towards staying with us to hit your long-term goal. Worst case: you tell me I suck, I write you a check, and you get 6 weeks of free training. Both options are risk-free — **the only thing guaranteed not to help you is walking out of here today.**"

Result: **two people took him up on it out of 4,000 sales over 3.5 years.**

**Fladlien's $25,000 guarantee.** On a $2,997 e-commerce course: *"If you buy this course, spend X on advertising your store using these methods, and don't make money, I will buy your store from you for $25,000, no questions asked."* It generated an additional **$3 million in sales**, and he paid out only **10** refunds — netting ~$2.75 million in extra sales.

**The guarantee menu** (pick by margin, ticket size, and what you can control):

| Guarantee | What the client gets | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Unconditional refund | Full / 50% / refund + ad spend / pay for a competitor's program / money back + $1,000 | Low-ticket B2C |
| Conditional outsized refund | 2–3× money back, or a payment far exceeding what they paid, on consumption conditions | High margin, many required actions |
| **Conditional service** | You keep working free of charge until X is achieved | *Hormozi's personal favorite* — guarantees outcome, removes time; never at risk of losing the money |
| Conditional modified service | Another period (≥2× the duration) of service free | A softer starting point than full service guarantee |
| Conditional credit | Money back as credit toward any service you offer | Upsells they're unsure about |
| Conditional personal service | *You* work one-on-one free until X | Strongest in existence — add heavy conditions |
| Conditional hotel & airfare | Reimburse product + hotel + airfare | Workshops and in-person events |
| Conditional wage payment | Pay their hourly rate if the session isn't valuable | Consults (nobody will produce a tax return to claim it) |
| Conditional release of service | Let them out of the contract free | Businesses with commitments or cancellation fees |
| Conditional delayed second payment | No second bill until their first outcome | When you have a systematized first result |
| Conditional first outcome | You keep paying their ad spend until first sale | Keeps everyone focused on first dollar over the bridge |
| **Anti-guarantee** | Access to something that can't be un-seen once shown | Code, scripts, proprietary funnels/metrics |
| **Implied / performance** | Pay per sale, per show, per pound lost; rev share; profit share; ratchets | Quantifiable outcomes with tracking |

**Anti-guarantee wording:** *"We're going to show you our proprietary process we're using right now to generate leads — our funnels, ads, and metrics. We're exposing the inner workings of our business. As a result, all sales are final."* Or the qualifying version: *"If you're the type of customer who needs a guarantee before taking a jump, you're not the type of person we want to work with. We want motivated self-starters."*

**Performance with a floor:** "We get the greater of $1,000 or 10% of revenue generated" — or "$1,000/month for the first 3 months, then 100% performance," ideal when a setup takes time to get going.

## Key Takeaways
1. Reversing risk is the highest-leverage conversion change available. Spend as much time on the guarantee as on the deliverables.
2. Every guarantee needs the "or what." No consequence, no teeth.
3. Do the math before fearing refunds — a 30% lift in closes survives a doubled refund rate.
4. Tie conditions to the actions that actually produce success. It protects you and helps them.
5. Match guarantee type to ticket size, margin, and fulfilment cost.
6. Name your guarantee vividly. Boring names waste the asset.
7. Start with service-based guarantees or performance partnerships — all sales final, no refund risk, and it keeps you honest.
