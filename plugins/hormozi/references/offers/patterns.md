# Patterns — $100M Offers

## The Five-Step Offer Build
**When to use**: Building any offer from scratch, or rebuilding one that isn't converting.
**How**:
1. Identify the **dream outcome** — sell the vacation, not the plane flight.
2. **List problems** — every friction point before, during, and after, in the customer's sequence, run against all four value drivers.
3. **List solutions** — prepend "how to" and reverse each problem.
4. **Create delivery vehicles** — divergently generate every way you *could* solve each, using the cheat codes.
5. **Trim and stack** — cut high-cost/low-value then low-cost/low-value; bundle, name, and price the survivors.
**Trade-offs**: Steps 2–4 feel excessive and are the whole point. Doing it once produces an asset that lasts years.

## The Product Delivery Cheat Codes
**When to use**: Step 4, when your imagination defaults to one obvious solution.
**How**: Vary along six axes — (1) personal attention: 1-on-1 / small group / one-to-many; (2) effort expected: DIY / DWY / DFY; (3) live medium: in person, phone, email, text, Zoom, chat; (4) recorded format: audio, video, written; (5) response speed and hours; (6) the 10× / 1⁄10th test.
**Trade-offs**: Generates far more options than you'll use — that's the intent. Solutions to one problem seed ideas for others.

## Trim by Cost-Value Quadrant
**When to use**: After generating delivery vehicles.
**How**: Remove **high-cost/low-value first**, then **low-cost/low-value**. Keep low-cost/high-value and high-cost/high-value. Judge "value" by asking whether the item raises financial worth, raises belief in success, lowers effort, or lowers time.
**Trade-offs**: Reserve high-cost items for big value-adds only. If a cheaper vehicle produces the same value, use it.

## Build the High-Value One-to-Many Asset
**When to use**: Whenever a solution recurs across every customer.
**How**: Absorb a large one-time creation cost to produce something with near-zero marginal cost — a calculator, a recorded tour, a template library, a book.
**Trade-offs**: Slow to build, compounding forever after. This is why software is valuable and why Hormozi's 100-hour meal-plan tool served 4,000+ gyms.

## Scarcity Mechanisms (pick one)
**When to use**: To raise price and speed decisions without changing the product.
**How**: Total business cap · growth rate cap · cohort cap · honest capacity disclosure. Always sell out, and always announce that you sold out.
**Trade-offs**: Caps real revenue in the short run to compound demand over the long run. Never fake it.

## Urgency Mechanisms (pick one)
**When to use**: To lower the action threshold.
**How**: Cohort-based rolling urgency · rolling seasonal urgency (re-dated, re-named promotions) · pricing/bonus-based urgency (deadline on the *promotion*, not the service) · exploding opportunity.
**Trade-offs**: Requires real deadlines. Expect 50–60% of a campaign's sales in the final four hours.

## The Bonus Presentation Sequence (one-on-one)
**When to use**: Every one-on-one sale.
**How**: Ask for the sale **first**. Yes → reveal bonuses after signing (wow experience, reinforces the decision). No → present the bonus that answers their specific stated obstacle, then ask again. Repeat.
**Trade-offs**: Feels repetitive; reciprocity makes it work. Never discount instead.

## Partner-Sourced Bonuses With Commission
**When to use**: When your own deliverables are already trimmed and you want more perceived value at zero cost.
**How**: Approach adjacent, non-competing businesses your customer will need next. Trade exposure for free services. Then negotiate a group discount *and* a referral commission back to you.
**Trade-offs**: Requires relationship work; turns bonuses into a second revenue stream on customers you already paid to acquire.

## The Conditional Guarantee
**When to use**: Higher-ticket, higher-fulfilment-cost, or business-oriented offers.
**How**: *"If you do not get X result in Y time, we will Z"* — where the conditions are the actions genuinely required for success.
**Trade-offs**: Weeds out guarantee-shoppers, improves client results, and protects you against fulfilment cost. Weaker headline punch than unconditional.

## Guarantee Stacking
**When to use**: When you want both breadth of appeal and outcome credibility.
**How**: Layer an unconditional short-window guarantee with a conditional longer-window one, or two conditionals around sequential outcomes ("$10,000 by 60 days, $30,000 by 90 days, provided you do 1, 2, 3").
**Trade-offs**: Future-paces the prospect into believing the outcome. Only credible if you can actually deliver.

## MAGIC Naming and the Fatigue Ladder
**When to use**: Naming any offer, and every time response rates decay.
**How**: Name with 3–5 of Magnet/Avatar/Goal/Interval/Container. When performance drops, change in order: creative → body copy → headline → duration → enhancer → monetization structure.
**Trade-offs**: The lower down the ladder, the more operational drag. Exhaust the light changes first; once an offer is monetized, rarely change the machine.
