## The Acquisition Equation

```
  Growth = (Offer strength) × (Volume of engaged leads) × (Speed of cash recovery)
            $100M Offers        $100M Leads                $100M Money Models
```

**Multiplicative, not additive.** A zero anywhere is a zero everywhere. Each book exists
because the previous one exposed the next constraint: *Offers* solves **not enough cash**,
*Leads* solves **not enough clients**, *Money Models* solves **cash arriving too slowly to
fund more clients**.

## The Master Diagnostic

Walk top-down. Stop at the first failure — that's the real constraint, and every level
inherits the failures above it. Fixing a lower level first is motion, not progress.

1. **Market** — pain · purchasing power · targetability · growth? *(any one failing is disqualifying — fix or leave)* → `references/offers/chapters/ch04-starving-crowd.md`
2. **Offer** — can a prospect price-compare you? *(if they ask "how are you different?", the offer failed)* → `references/offers/overview.md`
3. **Volume** — are they doing **100 primary actions per day**? *(if not, they don't have data, they have anecdotes)* → `references/leads/chapters/ch11-more-better-new.md`
4. **CAC vs. 3× industry average** — **below**: fix the business model (raise LTGP) · **above**: fix the advertising → `references/leads/chapters/ch10-paid-ads-money.md`
5. **Qualified & buying?** — qualified but not buying = **sales problem**. Stop spending on traffic.
6. **30-day recovery?** — no = **money model problem** → `references/money-models/overview.md`

Full version with the common wrong self-diagnoses: `references/system/chapters/ch02-the-build-order.md`.

## The Three Numbers

| Number | Target | Which section moves it |
|---|---|---|
| **LTGP:CAC** | **> 3:1** | Offers raises LTGP · Leads lowers CAC |
| **30-day cash** | **≥ CAC** | Money Models |
| **Referrals vs. churn** | referrals > churn | Leads ch12 — the only exponential channel |

Use **gross profit, never revenue.** Revenue-based LTV inflates the acquisition budget and
hides negative unit economics. If the user quotes an LTV, check which one they mean before
doing anything with it.

## The Value Equation — it runs all three books

```
              Dream Outcome  ×  Perceived Likelihood of Achievement
  Value  =  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
              Perceived Time Delay  ×  Perceived Effort & Sacrifice
```

It prices offers (*Offers* ch6), generates problem lists (ch8), judges lead magnets
(*Leads* ch3), structures ad copy as **what/who/when** (ch9), writes outreach scripts
(ch5), and recruits affiliates (ch15).

**Spend the hardest effort on the denominator** — claims are cheap to copy, immediacy and
effortlessness are not. And **every objection maps to a driver** — answer the driver, not
the sentence. Objection translator: `references/system/chapters/ch03-value-equation-everywhere.md`.

## The Guarantee Lifecycle — one idea, five revenue mechanisms

conversion tool (*Offers* ch14) → results mechanism with conditions set to the success
behaviours (*Leads* ch12) → **attraction offer**, win your money back (*Money Models* ch3)
→ **downsell**, trial with penalty (ch12) → **priceable feature**, removed to lower the
price (ch12).

One piece of customer research — *what did our best customers actually do?* — powers all five.

## The Mirror Pairs — build one, get two

- **Win your money back** ↔ **Trial with penalty** — refund if they comply / charge if they don't
- **Buy X get Y free** ↔ **Continuity discount** — one-time / recurring
- **Decoy offer** ↔ **Anchor upsell** — cheap-first / expensive-first contrast
- **Conditional guarantee** ↔ **Feature downsell** — add it / price its removal

## Sequencing Rules

- **Build**: offer → leads → money model
- **Channels**: warm outreach → content → cold outreach → paid ads *(each funds the next)*
- **Lead getters**: customers → employees → agencies → affiliates
- **Money model**: attraction → upsell → downsell → continuity
- **Amplify**: below $1M/yr profit **more, better, new** · above **better, more, new**
- **Money**: create flow → monetize flow → add friction

## Resolving Apparent Contradictions

Most dissolve once you ask **where in the sequence** the advice sits:

- *Premium pricing vs. free attraction offers* → discount at the **entry**, premium at the **core**. Never discount the core offer.
- *Give until they ask vs. 30-day recovery* → patient in **content**, impatient in **paid**.
- *Niche down vs. broaden* → niche under **$10M/yr**; "I feel capped at $3M" is not a TAM problem.
- *Overdeliver vs. margin* → overdeliver first, engineer efficiency with the cash flow it produces.

The six real tensions, where the books genuinely pull against each other:
`references/system/chapters/ch08-tensions-and-judgment.md`.

## Universal Rules

- **Add bonuses, never discount** the core offer. Offer something *different* for less — never the same thing for less.
- **Ask which, not whether.** Ask **who**, not **if**, for referrals — at the moment of purchase.
- **Every guarantee needs the "or what,"** and its conditions should be the actions that cause success.
- **Real scarcity and real deadlines only.** Credibility is what makes the whole stack work.
- **Refund anyone who asks.** Don't take blood money. Don't hard-sell. Obey advertising law.
- **Depersonalize failure**: the offer sucks, not the person. You only suck if you stop trying.
- **Never let effort be the explanation** for why something didn't work.

## Applying This Well

These frameworks are specific, and their value comes from being applied with the user's real
numbers rather than recited. A few things worth doing:

- **Get their numbers before prescribing.** LTGP, CAC, 30-day cash, and current volume change
  which advice is correct. Ask for what's missing rather than assuming a default business.
- **Name the constraint out loud.** The single most useful output is usually "your problem
  isn't X, it's Y, and here's how I know" — the diagnostic exists to prevent expensive work
  on the wrong level.
- **Don't stack every enhancer at once.** Scarcity, urgency, bonuses, and guarantees each
  cost credibility when faked. Pick what's true for their business.
- **Flag when it doesn't apply.** These books assume a business that can raise prices and
  control its offer. Regulated pricing, marketplace-set pricing, and enterprise procurement
  break several of the plays. Say so rather than forcing the framework.

## Scope & Limits

Built from **audiobook transcripts**, and the *$100M Money Models* transcript is an
**abridged narration** — it does not contain the client-financed-acquisition or LTGP:CAC
vocabulary of the print edition. That terminology appears in the *$100M Leads* source and is
captured in `references/leads/chapters/ch10-paid-ads-money.md`;
`references/system/chapters/ch04-the-cash-engine.md` stitches the two together and says so.

Where the print editions go further than the narrations, these files say nothing rather than
inventing it. If a framework the user expects isn't here, say it isn't covered rather than
reconstructing it — and suggest the print edition.

Not covered by any of the three books: sales conversations in depth (explicitly deferred by
Hormozi to a future book), operations and fulfilment at scale, hiring beyond entry-level, and
product quality itself beyond "make it good."
