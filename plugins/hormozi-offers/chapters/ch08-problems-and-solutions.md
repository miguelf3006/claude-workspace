# Chapter 8: Creating Your Grand Slam Offer, Part I — Problems & Solutions

## Core Idea
Enumerate every problem a prospect will hit — before, during, and after — then mechanically reverse each one into a solution. **You must solve every perceived problem**, because a single unresolved "but what about…" kills the sale.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Offer Creation Process, Steps 1–3**:
  1. **Identify the dream outcome** — sell the vacation, not the plane flight. State it as arrival at the destination and what they'll experience there.
  2. **List problems** — everything that happens immediately before and immediately after someone uses your product, in insane detail, in the sequence the customer experiences them.
  3. **List solutions** — turn each problem into a solution, then name it.
  - When to use: Once per product, and it can last years. High-value, high-leverage work.
- **The Four-Problem Grid (problems map to value drivers)**: Every problem lands in one of the four value-equation buckets:
  1. **Dream outcome** — "This will not be financially worth it."
  2. **Likelihood of achievement** — "It won't work for me specifically. I won't stick with it. External factors will get in my way." *(The most unique and service-specific bucket.)*
  3. **Effort & sacrifice** — "This will be too hard, confusing. I won't like it. I'll suck at it."
  4. **Time** — "This will take too much time. I'm too busy. It'll take too long to work. It won't be convenient."
  - How: List each core thing someone must *do*, then run all four buckets against it.
- **Problem → Solution Transform (copywriting 101)**: Add **"how to"** then reverse the problem into solution-oriented language. That's the whole move.

## Key Concepts
- **Sell the vacation, not the plane flight**: Nobody wants a membership; they want to lose weight.
- **Insane detail**: The more problems you list, the more valuable the offer, because you're pre-answering the next objection as it manifests.
- **Repetition is normal**: The same four drivers recur across every problem category. That's the framework working, not you being unoriginal.
- **The "one thing" that kills the sale**: A single missing solution can cause a no. "You would be amazed at the reasons people do not buy."

## Mental Models
- **Think of problems as opportunities for value, one-for-one.** "The more problems you think of, the more problems you get to solve." Overwhelm is the wrong reaction — 32–64 problems is the best news you'll get.
- **Don't get romantic about how you solve the problem.** Find *a* way to solve every problem the prospect presents.
- **Walk the customer's timeline, not your product's feature list.** What's the next thing they need help with?

## Anti-patterns
- **Insisting on your method**: Hormozi refused to let weight-loss clients eat out because it complicated results — and lost sale after sale over it.
- **Solving most problems**: You'll still sell some people, just far fewer than you could have. The goal is the most people, at the highest price, at the highest margin.
- **Selling the mechanism instead of the destination**: Boot camp membership vs. "lose 20 lbs in 6 weeks."

## Worked Example
**Step 1 — Dream outcome.** Competing gyms all sold $99/month boot camps, and prospects answered "LA Fitness is $29." Hormozi couldn't even give it away free — people wouldn't start something they wouldn't continue. So he stopped selling the membership and sold: **"Lose 20 lbs in 6 weeks."** Big dream outcome, compressed time delay.

**Step 2 — Problem list** (weight loss, sequenced by what the customer must do):

*Buying healthy food / grocery shopping* — hard, confusing, I won't like it · takes too much time · expensive · I can't do it forever · my family's needs get in the way · if I travel I won't know what to get.
*Cooking healthy food* — hard and confusing, I'll suck at it · takes too much time · expensive, not worth it · unsustainable · family gets in the way · undoable when travelling.
*Eating healthy food* — same four drivers, applied again.
*Exercising regularly* — same four drivers, applied again.

That's **16 core problems with 2–4 sub-problems each = 32–64 problems total.** "No wonder most people don't achieve their goals."

**Step 3 — Solutions list** (problem reversed, "how to" prepended):

| Problem | Solution |
|---|---|
| Buying food is hard/confusing | How to make buying healthy food easy and enjoyable so anyone can do it, especially busy moms |
| Takes too much time | How to buy healthy food quickly |
| It's expensive | How to buy healthy food for **less than your current grocery bill** |
| Unsustainable | How to make buying healthy food take **less effort than buying unhealthy food** |
| Family gets in the way | How to buy healthy food for you and your family at the same time |
| Undoable when travelling | How to get healthy food when travelling |
| Cooking takes too long | How to cook meals in under 5 minutes |
| Eating healthy is expensive | How eating healthy is actually **cheaper** than unhealthy food |

**A different domain, same process.** Brooke Castillo's 90-day relationship course. Dream outcome: *amazing loving relationship in 90 days.* Problems: no good options · not attractive · not available · boring · no chemistry · poor communication · not hot enough · sex isn't good · no intellectual stimulation · not enough effort · no time · insecurity · needs not met · unmet expectations · acting crazy/emotional · relationship is dull · want different things · too much pressure · moves too slow · fizzles out fast · kids involved · sexual incompatibility. Solutions: *how to get a list of prospective partners to invite into 90 days · how to be attracted to your chosen partner · how to create chemistry like you've never known · how to communicate in sexy, fun, meaningful ways · how to make time for hourly dopamine hits · how to overcome all insecurity within the 90 days…*

**The eating-out guide.** Facing rent, Hormozi took a sales call with an exec who ate lunch out daily. Instead of losing the sale on principle he said: *"I'll make you an eating-out guide for when you go to restaurants so you can eat out 100% of the time and still hit your goal. How does that sound?"* She bought. He built the guide — and from then on, whenever anyone raised eating out, the objection was pre-solved. He kept doing this with templates and trainings until there were no "one things" left.

## Key Takeaways
1. Dream outcome first, stated as arrival, not as mechanism.
2. Walk the customer's sequence and list every friction point in insane detail.
3. Use the four value drivers as prompts against every step they must take.
4. Convert problems to solutions with "how to" + reversal. It's mechanical.
5. Solve **every** perceived problem — each unresolved one is somebody's reason not to buy.
6. When a prospect raises a novel objection, build the asset that kills it permanently.
