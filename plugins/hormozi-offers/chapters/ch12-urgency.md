# Chapter 12: Enhancing the Offer — Urgency

## Core Idea
Urgency is a function of **time**. You limit *when* people can sign up rather than *how many* — and deadlines drive decisions.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Four Ethical Urgency Methods**:
  1. **Cohort-based rolling urgency** — start clients on a regular cadence. "If you sign up today I can get you in with our next group that kicks off Monday. Otherwise you wait until the next kickoff." Juice: "A client who signed up a few weeks ago just dropped, so I have an opening for Monday's cohort." Adds the operational benefit of a choreographed onboarding, which matters more as you scale. The less frequently you kick off, the more powerful it is.
  2. **Rolling seasonal urgency** — real countdowns and dated promotions on landing pages and in copy. The underlying promotion may be identical; renaming it by season creates a genuine start and finish.
  3. **Pricing or bonus-based urgency** — put the deadline on the *promotion*, not the service. Lets year-round businesses use urgency honestly. "Let's get you started today so you can take advantage of the discount you came in for. I'm not sure how long we'll run it — we change them every four weeks or so, and this is one of the better ones."
  4. **Exploding opportunity** — the opportunity itself decays with time. Arbitrage windows correct themselves; every second of delay costs disproportionate gains.
- **Clean Your Pipeline With Every Price Change**: If you're raising prices, tell people first. It shows strength and produces an influx of cash from fence-sitters. *Never raise prices without letting people know.*

## Key Concepts
- **Scarcity vs. urgency**: Quantity vs. time. Frequently used together; worth separating conceptually.
- **Action threshold**: The bar a prospect must clear to act. Urgency lowers it.
- **Rolling cohorts**: Regular start dates that create natural, non-phony deadlines.
- **Rapper on the same core service**: Local businesses must vary marketing more often than national advertisers — a new dated wrapper around the same service delivers urgency *and* novelty, and consistently outperforms unchanged campaigns.

## Mental Models
- **"Deadlines drive decisions."** You don't push people over the edge; the deadline lets them push themselves.
- **You always have the advantage, because you call the shots.** If a cohort just started, either offer speedy personalized onboarding as a sign-today bonus, or reframe the wait as an advantage — more time to review materials, consult employees (B2B) or family (B2C), plus a more extended payment plan available *only* because the start date is far out.
- **Make it real or don't do it.** Fake countdowns destroy credibility.

## Anti-patterns
- **Fear of losing sales by turning business away**: Universal and unfounded. Every experienced marketer will tell you the same.
- **Fake deadlines**: You'll lose credibility and look like every other wannabe marketer.
- **Silent price increases**: You forfeit a free cash influx and signal weakness.

## Worked Example
**The last-4-hours data.** On a week-long campaign or launch, **50–60% of sales happen in the final four hours of the last day.** The last ~3% of the time allotted produces over half the revenue. Completely illogical, unmistakably human. This is the argument against the fear of turning business away: you make far more from people who take action than you lose from people who "miss out" — and those people were never going to buy anyway. *They didn't buy when they had their feet to the fire; why would they without it?*

**The rolling seasonal calendar** — same promotion, different wrapper, every month:

| Month | Promotion name | Ends |
|---|---|---|
| January | New Year promotion | Jan 30 |
| February | Valentine's Lovers promo | Feb 28 |
| March | Sexy by Spring | Mar 31 |
| April | Fools in Love April promo | Apr 30 |

Five minutes of editing — new ad campaign, new landing page, new dates — and conversions go through the roof. Hormozi calls this his **number one strategy for local businesses**.

**Bonus-based urgency swaps** you can rotate around an unchanged core offer: a pricing promotion · a discount · free install · free onboarding · an extra workshop valued at $1,000 if they buy now.

## Key Takeaways
1. Urgency limits *when*, scarcity limits *how many*. Deploy both.
2. Put deadlines on cohorts, seasons, or promotions — three ways to be honest and still urgent.
3. Real deadlines only. Credibility is the asset that makes urgency work at all.
4. Expect the majority of sales in the final hours; don't panic mid-campaign.
5. Announce every price increase in advance to clear the pipeline.
6. For local businesses, re-wrap and re-date the same core service continuously.
