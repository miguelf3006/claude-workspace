## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### The Acquisition Equation
```
  Growth = (Offer strength) × (Volume of engaged leads) × (Speed of cash recovery)
            $100M Offers        $100M Leads                $100M Money Models
```
**Multiplicative, not additive.** A zero anywhere is a zero everywhere. Each book was written because the previous one exposed the next constraint: *Offers* solves **not enough cash**, *Leads* solves **not enough clients**, *Money Models* solves **the cash arrives too slowly to fund more clients.**

### The Master Diagnostic — walk top-down, stop at the first failure
1. **Market** — pain · purchasing power · targetability · growth? *(any one failing is disqualifying — fix or leave)*
2. **Offer** — can a prospect price-compare you? *(if they ask "how are you different?", the offer failed)*
3. **Volume** — are you doing **100 primary actions per day**? *(if not, you don't have data, you have anecdotes)*
4. **CAC vs. 3× industry average** — **below**: fix the business model (raise LTGP) · **above**: fix the advertising
5. **Qualified & buying?** — qualified but not buying = **sales problem**. Stop spending on traffic
6. **30-day recovery?** — no = **money model problem**

Every level inherits the failures above it. Fixing a lower level first is motion, not progress.

### The Three Numbers
| Number | Target | Which book moves it |
|---|---|---|
| **LTGP:CAC** | **> 3:1** | Offers raises LTGP · Leads lowers CAC |
| **30-day cash** | **≥ CAC** | Money Models |
| **Referrals vs. churn** | referrals > churn | Leads ch12 — the only exponential channel |

Use **gross profit, never revenue.** Revenue-based LTV inflates your acquisition budget and hides negative unit economics.

### The Value Equation Runs All Three Books
```
              Dream Outcome  ×  Perceived Likelihood of Achievement
  Value  =  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
              Perceived Time Delay  ×  Perceived Effort & Sacrifice
```
It prices offers (*Offers* ch6), generates problem lists (ch8), judges lead magnets (*Leads* ch3), structures ad copy as **what/who/when** (ch9), writes outreach scripts (ch5), and recruits affiliates (ch15). **Spend your hardest effort on the denominator** — claims are cheap to copy, immediacy and effortlessness are not. And **every objection maps to a driver** — answer the driver, not the sentence.

### The Guarantee Lifecycle (one idea across three books)
conversion tool (*Offers* ch14) → results mechanism, with conditions set to the success behaviours (*Leads* ch12) → **attraction offer**, win your money back (*Money Models* ch3) → **downsell**, trial with penalty (ch12) → **priceable feature**, removed to lower the price (ch12).
One piece of customer research — *what did our best customers actually do?* — powers **five** revenue mechanisms.

### The Mirror Pairs — build one, get two
- **Win your money back** ↔ **Trial with penalty** — refund if they comply / charge if they don't
- **Buy X get Y free** ↔ **Continuity discount** — one-time / recurring
- **Decoy offer** ↔ **Anchor upsell** — cheap-first / expensive-first contrast
- **Conditional guarantee** ↔ **Feature downsell** — add it / price its removal

### Sequencing Rules
- **Build**: offer → leads → money model
- **Channels**: warm outreach → content → cold outreach → paid ads *(each funds the next)*
- **Lead getters**: customers → employees → agencies → affiliates
- **Money model**: attraction → upsell → downsell → continuity
- **Amplify**: below $1M/yr profit **more, better, new** · above **better, more, new**
- **Money**: create flow → monetize flow → add friction

### Resolving Apparent Contradictions
Most dissolve once you ask **where in the sequence** the advice sits:
- *Premium pricing vs. free attraction offers* → discount at the **entry**, premium at the **core**. Never discount the core offer
- *Give until they ask vs. 30-day recovery* → patient in **content**, impatient in **paid**
- *Niche down vs. broaden* → niche under **$10M/yr**; "I feel capped at $3M" is not a TAM problem
- *Overdeliver vs. margin* → overdeliver first, engineer efficiency with the cash flow it produces

### Universal Rules
- **Add bonuses, never discount** the core offer. Offer something *different* for less — never the same thing for less
- **Ask which, not whether.** Ask **who**, not **if**, for referrals — at the moment of purchase
- **Every guarantee needs the "or what,"** and its conditions should be the actions that cause success
- **Real scarcity and real deadlines only.** Credibility is what makes the whole stack work
- **Refund anyone who asks.** Don't take blood money. Don't hard-sell. Obey advertising law
- **Depersonalize failure**: your offer sucks, not you. You only suck if you stop trying
- **Never let effort be the explanation** for why something didn't work

## Scope & Limits

This skill synthesizes the three books; it does **not** replace them. For any single-book framework in depth, load the companion skill — the chapter files there carry the worked examples, scripts, and full mechanics.

**Source note:** all four skills were built from **audiobook transcripts**, and the *$100M Money Models* transcript is an **abridged narration**. It does not contain the client-financed-acquisition or LTGP:CAC vocabulary associated with the print edition; that terminology appears in the *$100M Leads* source and is captured in `hormozi-leads` ch10. Ch04 of this skill stitches the two together deliberately and says so. Where the print editions go further than the narrations, these skills say nothing rather than inventing it.

Not covered by any of the three books: sales conversations in depth (explicitly deferred by Hormozi to a future book), operations and fulfilment at scale, hiring beyond entry-level, and product quality itself beyond "make it good."
