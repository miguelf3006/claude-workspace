# Chapter 4: The Cash Engine — LTGP, CAC, and 30-Day Recovery Unified

## Core Idea
Three numbers govern whether a business compounds: **how much a customer is worth (LTGP)**, **what they cost (CAC)**, and **how fast the money arrives (30-day recovery)**. Each book moves a different one, and only together do they produce a machine.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Three Numbers and Their Owners**:

| Number | Definition | Which book moves it | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LTGP** | All money a customer ever spends **minus** all money to deliver | *Offers* + *Money Models* | Premium pricing, differentiation, upsells, continuity |
| **CAC** | Total cost to acquire one customer | *Leads* | Better call outs, better channels, referrals, affiliates |
| **30-day cash** | Cash collected in the first 30 days | *Money Models* | Attraction offer design, upsell sequencing, prepaid blocks |

- **The Two Thresholds**:
  - **LTGP:CAC > 3:1** (*Leads* ch10) — *"Every business I invest in that struggles to scale has at least one thing in common: their LTGP:CAC was less than 3:1. As soon as I get above 3:1, they take off."*
  - **30-day cash ≥ CAC** (*Money Models* ch1) — recover acquisition cost inside the interest-free credit-card window so the same money funds the next customer.
- **The Referee Rule** (*Leads* ch10) — which number to work on:
  - CAC **below** 3× industry average → your ads are fine. **Raise LTGP.**
  - CAC **above** 3× industry average → **fix advertising.**
  - *"The cost to acquire customers between competitors in the same industry is much closer than you'd think. The difference between winners and losers is how much they make off each customer."*
- **The Compounding Identity** (*Money Models* ch1): 2× value × 2× volume × 2× speed = **8× growth.** 3× each = **27×.**

## Key Concepts
- **Use gross profit, never revenue.** *Offers* ch2 is explicit: indirect costs (admin, software, rent) are excluded from LTGP, so don't confuse LTGP with what you can afford to spend. Revenue-based LTV inflates your acquisition budget and hides negative unit economics.
- **The two levers on the ratio**: lower CAC (more efficient ads, referrals, affiliates) or raise LTGP (better business model). *"For maximum money, I prefer to do both."*
- **Referrals break the linearity.** Every other channel is linear — double the inputs, double the leads. Referrals compound: one brings two, two bring four. An LTGP:CAC of 4:1 where each customer brings two more becomes **12:1** (*Leads* ch12).
- **Speed is a third axis most operators ignore.** Two businesses with identical LTGP:CAC grow at completely different rates if one collects in 30 days and the other in two years.

## Mental Models
- **Think of 30-day cash as your reload speed, and LTGP:CAC as your damage per shot.** High ratio with slow reload is a good business that grows slowly. Both together is a machine.
- **"If you made a billion per customer, you could spend $999 million to acquire one and still clear a million — no matter how crappy your ads. If you made one cent per customer, even the best ads in the world would fail."** (*Leads* ch10) The business model dominates the ads.
- **Every offer in the money model is an LTGP contribution with a timestamp.** Upsells add to LTGP *and* pull it forward. Continuity adds to LTGP but pushes it back — which is why it runs last and gets paired with prepaid blocks.

## Anti-patterns
- **Blaming ads for a business-model problem**: *"They often think they have crappy ads (high CAC) when in reality they have a crappy business model (low LTGP)."*
- **Confusing a sales problem with either**: One portfolio company spent 12 weeks and $150,000, got the right leads on the phone, and declared advertising broken. The ads were fine; sales were bad. **~$30M in enterprise value lost to the misdiagnosis.**
- **Collecting cash you can't deliver against**: *Money Models* ch6 is blunt — if you collect a year of payments in a month, budget the servicing money separately.

## Worked Example
**The same business, three interventions, compounding.** Baseline: $10,000 LTGP, $3,000 CAC, cash collected over 12 months.

| Step | Intervention | Source | LTGP | CAC | 30-day cash | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Baseline | — | $10,000 | $3,000 | $800 | 3.3:1 |
| 1 | Advertise a **free lead magnet** instead of the core offer ($25 delivery, 10× engagement) | *Leads* ch3 | $10,000 | **$1,000** | $800 | **10:1** |
| 2 | Add a **menu upsell** at point of sale | *Money Models* ch9 | **$12,500** | $1,000 | **$2,200** | 12.5:1 |
| 3 | **Downsell a trial with penalty** to the noes (3 of 10 → 6 of 10 buyers) | *Money Models* ch12 | $12,500 | **~$600** | $2,200 | **~20:1** |
| 4 | **Continuity + prepaid blocks** ("buy 5, get 1 free") | *Money Models* ch13 | **$18,000** | $600 | **$3,900** | 30:1 |
| 5 | **Referrals** — ask at point of purchase | *Leads* ch12 | $18,000 | **~$460** | $3,900 | ~39:1 |

By step 4, **30-day cash exceeds CAC** — the business is now client-financed and the ad budget stops being a constraint. Step 5 is the only one that keeps improving on its own.

*(The numbers are illustrative; the shape is the point. Each intervention comes from a different book, and each compounds on the previous one rather than replacing it.)*

**The billing tweaks that cost nothing** (*Money Models* ch13) — worth running before any of the above, because they're wording changes:
- Bill **every 4 weeks** instead of monthly → 13 cycles vs. 12 → **+8.3% revenue**, which at 20% margins is **+41% profit**
- Add a **3% processing fee** → at 10% margins, **+30% profit**
- Trade the fee for a **second payment method** → recovers the revenue lost to expired cards

## Key Takeaways
1. Three numbers: LTGP, CAC, 30-day cash. Different books move different ones.
2. Target LTGP:CAC above 3:1 and 30-day cash above CAC.
3. Use industry-average CAC as the referee for which one to work on.
4. Gross profit, never revenue. Revenue-based LTV lies about your acquisition budget.
5. Referrals are the only channel that breaks linearity — build them before buying more traffic.
6. Speed of collection is a third axis, and it's the one most operators never measure.
7. Run the free billing tweaks first; they're the highest return per unit of effort in all three books.
