# Chapter 2: The Build Order — Diagnosing Your Actual Constraint

## Core Idea
Almost every business owner is working on the wrong problem. There is a **single diagnostic path** across all three books that tells you which one you're actually on — and it runs top-down, never bottom-up.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Master Diagnostic** (assembled from *Offers* ch4, *Leads* ch10 and ch13):

```
1. MARKET      Is it growing, with pain, purchasing power, and targetability?
               → Any one failing is disqualifying. Fix or leave. (Offers ch4)
                                    ↓
2. OFFER       Can prospects price-compare you to anything?
               → If they ask "how are you different?", the offer failed. (Offers ch2–3)
                                    ↓
3. LEAD FLOW   Are you doing 100 primary actions per day?
               → If not, you don't have a lead problem, you have a volume problem. (Leads ch11/16)
                                    ↓
4. ADS vs MODEL  Is CAC below 3× your industry average?
               → Below: fix the business model (raise LTGP)
               → Above: fix the advertising. (Leads ch10)
                                    ↓
5. ADS vs SALES  Do engaged leads have the problem you solve AND the money?
               → No → advertising problem
               → Yes, buying, too few → advertising problem
               → Yes, not buying → SALES problem. (Leads ch13)
                                    ↓
6. CASH SPEED  Do you recover acquisition cost within 30 days?
               → No → money model problem. (Money Models ch1)
```

- **The Order of Importance** (*Offers* ch4) as the meta-rule: `Market > Offer > Persuasion`. A *great* rating higher up overpowers everything below. A *bad* rating stops the equation entirely.
- **The Amplification Order** (*Leads* ch11): below $1M/yr profit → **more, better, new.** Above → **better, more, new.** Percentage improvements don't matter until volume is real.

## Key Concepts
- **Self-diagnoses that are almost always wrong**:
  - *"I've saturated my market"* → the **size of the pie fallacy** (*Leads* ch11). Check platforms used, content produced, outreach done.
  - *"My ads don't work"* → check whether qualified leads are taking calls. If they are, it's sales.
  - *"Everyone loves our stuff, we just need to get the word out"* → the product isn't remarkable (*Leads* ch12). If it were, you'd have more business than you could handle.
  - *"My CAC is too high"* → often a **low LTGP**, not high CAC. Competitors' acquisition costs are far closer than people think.
  - *"I need more leads"* → check the offer and the market first.
- **Failure is depersonalized at every level**: "If your offer doesn't work, it doesn't mean you suck. It means your offer sucks. Big difference." (*Offers* ch4)

## Mental Models
- **Diagnose top-down, always.** Every level below inherits the failure of the level above. Fixing ad creative on a commodity offer in a shrinking market is motion, not progress.
- **Effort is never allowed to be the explanation.** *"I would never let effort be the reason anything didn't work for me. It could be something else — the offer, the copy, the image, the targeting, the media, the platform, the position of the moon. But not my effort."* (*Leads* ch16)
- **The bottleneck moves.** Solving one constraint promotes the next one. That's success, not failure — and it's why the book order exists.

## Anti-patterns
- **Optimizing a non-constraint**: A +5-point improvement on a 50% step adds 10%; the same +5 on a 5% step adds 100% (*Leads* ch11). Same work, tenfold difference.
- **Skipping to paid ads**: The most expensive, most skill-intensive channel, run before the offer or the cheaper channels are proven.
- **Firing the wrong team**: "Don't fire your sales guy if you've got advertising problems, and don't fire your advertising employees if you've got a sales problem." (*Leads* ch13)

## Worked Example
**Three businesses, same symptom, three different answers.**

*"We're not growing and leads are expensive."*

| | Business A | Business B | Business C |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Market check** | Newspapers, shrinking 25%/yr | Chiropractors, $15.1B, growing | Gyms, normal, growing |
| **Offer check** | Great — zero-risk rev share | Fine | *"$99/month boot camp"* — directly comparable to a $29 competitor |
| **Volume check** | n/a | **$30k/month, one platform, no content, no outreach** | 100+ actions/day |
| **CAC vs industry** | n/a | n/a | Within 3× |
| **Qualified & buying?** | n/a | n/a | Qualified, not buying |
| **30-day recovery** | n/a | n/a | No |
| **Actual constraint** | **Market** — no tactic can win here | **Volume** — size-of-the-pie fallacy | **Offer**, then **sales**, then **cash speed** |
| **Correct action** | Change markets. *(Lloyd pivoted to mask manufacturing and hit millions/month in five months with the same skills)* | More, before better. Add platforms, content, outreach | Rebuild the offer (*Offers*), then fix sales, then sequence offers (*Money Models*) |

Each of these owners would have described their problem identically. Only the top-down walk separates them.

**The five-minute version** — answer in order, stop at the first no:
1. Is my market growing, painful, solvent, and reachable?
2. Can a prospect price-compare me?
3. Am I doing 100 primary actions a day?
4. Is my CAC within 3× industry average?
5. Do qualified leads buy when they talk to us?
6. Do I get my acquisition cost back within 30 days?

## Key Takeaways
1. Walk the diagnostic top-down and stop at the first failure. That's your only project.
2. Market failures cannot be out-executed. Leave.
3. Comparability, not price, is what makes you cheap.
4. Below 100 actions/day, you don't have data — you have anecdotes.
5. Use industry-average CAC to separate an ads problem from a business-model problem.
6. Qualified-but-not-buying is a sales problem. Don't spend another dollar on traffic.
7. Solving a constraint promotes the next one. Expect the target to move.
