# Patterns — The Hormozi Acquisition System

*Cross-book workflows. For single-book plays, see that skill's patterns file.*

## Run the Master Diagnostic
**When to use**: Before doing anything else, and any time growth stalls.
**How**: Walk top-down, stop at the first failure — (1) market: pain, purchasing power, targetability, growth; (2) offer: can they price-compare you? (3) volume: 100 primary actions/day? (4) CAC vs. 3× industry average → ads or business model; (5) qualified leads buying? → ads or sales; (6) 30-day recovery? → money model.
**Trade-offs**: Every level inherits the failures above it. Fixing a lower level first is motion without progress.

## Translate an Objection Into a Value Driver
**When to use**: Any stall in a sales conversation, or any underperforming ad or page.
**How**: Map what they said to one of four — "not worth it" (dream outcome), "won't work for me" (perceived likelihood), "too hard" (effort), "too long" (time delay). Answer *that* driver with its matching tool: outcome → tie to a number they believe; likelihood → mechanism + matched testimonial + guarantee; effort → move DIY→DWY→DFY; time → engineer a fast win.
**Trade-offs**: Answering the wrong driver reads as not listening. Bigger promises never fix "this seems hard."

## Convert Success Research Into Five Revenue Mechanisms
**When to use**: Once you know what your best customers actually did differently.
**How**: Take those criteria and deploy them as (1) a conditional guarantee, (2) a win-your-money-back attraction offer, (3) a trial-with-penalty downsell, (4) the fast win engineered into fulfilment, (5) a feature downsell that prices the guarantee.
**Trade-offs**: Requires the *Leads* ch12 research first — survey best customers, interview them, find common actions.

## Build the First Money-Model Layer
**When to use**: You have a working offer and any customer flow at all, before buying more traffic.
**How**: Add one **classic upsell** at point of sale, then one **downsell** for the noes (payment plan for one-time, trial with penalty for recurring). Then ask for a **referral** at the moment of purchase.
**Trade-offs**: Three moves, no new traffic, and each roughly doubles some part of the economics. Cheaper than any ad.

## Free the Billing Margin
**When to use**: Any recurring business, immediately, before any other optimization.
**How**: Bill every four weeks instead of monthly (13 cycles vs. 12) · add a 3% processing fee · offer to waive the fee for a second payment method, ideally ACH.
**Trade-offs**: Essentially none — these are wording changes with no delivery cost. At 20% margins the billing change alone is ~+41% annual profit.

## Climb the Channel Ladder
**When to use**: Deciding which lead source to add next.
**How**: Warm outreach (needs nothing) → free content (needs skill) → cold outreach (needs volume and systems) → paid ads (needs skill *and* money). Each funds the next. Rule of 100 on whichever you're on.
**Trade-offs**: Skipping ahead means paying to learn what a cheaper channel would have taught free.

## Build Lead Getters, Then Repeat the Plan
**When to use**: Once you can afford to pay someone to do a daily action.
**How**: Referrals → employees (Internal Core Four + Document/Demonstrate/Duplicate) → agencies (learn-then-consult, with a deadline) → affiliates (three-tier payouts from max allowable CAC). Then return to step 1 of the one-page plan with employees as the new target lead type.
**Trade-offs**: This is the step that converts a job into a sellable asset — roughly a $10M swing on a $2M-profit business.

## Check Preconditions Before Running a Play
**When to use**: Every time, especially for the aggressive plays.
**How**: Ask what has to be true — does the customer do work? is it recurring? are the actions trackable? do you know the success behaviours? can you deliver? does the back end fit the front end? will you waive fees for non-starters?
**Trade-offs**: An unchecked box means run something else or fix that first. Most play failures are precondition failures, not execution failures.

## Resolve an Apparent Contradiction by Position
**When to use**: Whenever two pieces of Hormozi advice seem to conflict.
**How**: Ask *where in the sequence* each belongs. Discount at the entry, premium at the core. Patient in content, impatient in paid. Overdeliver first, engineer efficiency second. Niche under $10M, broaden above.
**Trade-offs**: A few tensions are real rather than positional — notably scarcity's deliberate revenue cap, which requires believing a compounding claim you can't verify until the next promotion.
