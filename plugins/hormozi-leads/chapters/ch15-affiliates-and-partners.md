# Chapter 15: Lead Getters — Affiliates & Partners

## Core Idea
An affiliate is another business with a warm audience full of people like your customers. Building an **affiliate army** turns the same amount of work into compounding, multi-level lead flow — the highest-leverage lead getter in the book, and the most advanced.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Six Steps to Build an Affiliate Army**:
  1. **Find your ideal affiliates** 2. **Make them an offer** 3. **Qualify them** 4. **Figure out what to pay them** 5. **Get them advertising** 6. **Keep them advertising**
- **The "Who's Got My Leads?" Questions** (step 1): What do my best customers buy? Who provides that stuff? Where do they go? What businesses are in those surrounding areas? What do they like to do? Who provides those services? *(Direct-to-consumer: the **employers** of your consumers can make great affiliates — what types of businesses do they work for, what jobs do they have?)*
  - **The six affiliate categories**: softwares · products · equipment · services · groups they belong to · events they attend.
  - **Signal**: A business that falls into *multiple* categories probably has lots of good leads for you and would make a great affiliate.
- **The Affiliate Offer Structure** (step 2 — it's just an offer, aimed at a different customer):
  - *Dream outcome*: "Make more money from your current customers and get more leads than your current offer."
  - *Perceived likelihood*: "…with a high chance of working, since your customers already want the product."
  - *Effort and sacrifice*: "…without needing to build, deliver, or provide customer support for the product yourself."
  - *Time delay*: "…so you can start selling it tomorrow."
- **Two Ways to Qualify Affiliates** (step 3 — get them invested so they win fast):
  1. **Make them a customer** — they buy and preferably use the product to keep affiliate status. "If they don't believe in your stuff enough to buy it, they probably shouldn't sell it." *(Bulk purchase variant: "So do you want anything extra, or just go with the minimum order?" — presenting a minimum means they'll at least buy that, and more often than you'd think, more.)*
  2. **Make them an expert** — they pay for onboarding and training that certifies them as a product expert. **Charge 10–20% of what the average active affiliate makes in their first 12 months.** (Average affiliate makes $40,000/year → charge $4,000–$8,000.) Too low and they're not invested; too high and you won't get enough affiliates.
  - *"Nine times out of ten, if they pay, they'll pay attention."*
- **The Three-Tier Payout Structure** (step 4): Base payouts on your **maximum allowable CAC**.
  - Example: $200 product, $40 to fulfil → $160 available. For a 3:1 LTGP:CAC, three parts ($120) to the business and one part (**$40**) is your max allowable CAC.

| Tier | Payout | Trigger |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 25% CAC = **$10** | Agreeing to initial terms — signing up and buying products or certification |
| 2 | 50% CAC = **$20** | **Activation** — finishing the certification, doing a set number of posts and reach-outs, doing a launch |
| 3 | 100% CAC = **$40** | **Sustained performance** — e.g. maintaining five customers/month on subscription |

  - **The hidden profitable side effect**: your *blended* payout is well below max CAC. If 20% of sales come from tier 1, 20% from tier 2, and 60% from tier 3, the blend is **$30 instead of $40** — improving LTGP:CAC from 3:1 to **4:1**. Cutting marketing costs ~33% often translates to 10–20% more net profit. Use the surplus for contests, recruiting more affiliates, or incentivizing rising stars.
  - **What to pay for**: new customers and repeat customers. As tracking improves, you can pay for earlier steps — lead magnet downloaded, appointment set, or anything that reliably turns into sales.

## Key Concepts
- **Affiliates convert low-ticket economics into high-ticket economics**: One affiliate sale can be worth thousands of customers, which is how you make cold outreach work for a direct-to-consumer business whose average ticket is too small.
- **Three problems affiliates present**: convince them to advertise someone else's stuff at all → convince them to advertise *yours* → keep them advertising so they're a long-term source.
- **Raise the commitment until it fits**: If not enough people start, lower the commitment. If not enough follow through, raise it. (Same mechanic as raising prices every five signups in warm outreach.)
- **Track record**: Hormozi built two companies with affiliates — Allan and Prestige Labs — together doing **$75M+ in revenue from 5,000+ affiliates**.

## Mental Models
- **Same work, more money.** Selling 10 customers a month vs. selling 10 *affiliates* a month who each bring you a customer every month — the second compounds while the first resets.
- **Affiliates are just customers you're advertising to.** Call out, value elements, CTA. Nothing new to invent.
- **The more money an affiliate invests in your product, the more money they make.** Investment precedes effort.

## Worked Example
**Allan's three-level affiliate structure** — the clearest illustration of compounding:

| Level | Who | Brings |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Agency **super affiliates** | agency leads |
| 2 | Agencies | local business leads |
| 3 | Local businesses | end-consumer leads |

One super affiliate added **10 agencies/month**. Those 10 agencies brought a combined **~50 local businesses/month**. Those local businesses brought a combined **2,500 leads/month**, which Allan worked at about **$5 a pop** — **$12,500/month.**

And it didn't stop: each super affiliate kept bringing more agencies, who kept bringing more local businesses, every month after.

| Month | Revenue from one super affiliate |
|---|---|
| 1 | $12,500 |
| 2 | $25,000 |
| 3 | $37,500 |
| … | … |

**With only a few agency super affiliates, Allan scaled to $1,700,000/month within 6 months of launching.** *"That's why you want an affiliate army."*

**The leverage comparison:** sell 10 customers a month at $10,000 each, and you're doing the same work every month for the same result. Sell **10 affiliates** a month, and each brings you one $10,000 customer every month — **$100,000 extra revenue every month**, compounding to **$7.8 million over 12 months** and growing thereafter. Same effort.

**Building the hit list — the actual method.** When Hormozi started Allan, agency owners were the ideal affiliate. He made a list of **200 products and services for agencies** and the businesses delivering them, then noticed they fell neatly into the six categories. *"It wasn't fancy, so don't overthink it."* If you struggle to fill the sheet, call your customers and ask them.

**Call-out variations for recruiting affiliates:**
- Aimed at the owners: *"Attention spa owners…"*
- Aimed at the affiliate's customers: *"Do you work with busy professionals who spend all day in meetings?"*
- Aimed at the affiliate's promise: *"To the heroes who heal the stress of others…"*
- Aimed at what they deliver: *"If you sell lotions or scented oils, this is for you."*
- Aimed at your own customers: *"Do you know anyone who owns a spa?"*

## Key Takeaways
1. Ideal affiliates are businesses with a warm audience full of people like your customers.
2. Build the hit list from six categories: softwares, products, equipment, services, groups, events.
3. Recruit affiliates with an offer — call out, value elements, CTA — not a pitch deck.
4. Qualify by making them a customer, an expert, or both. Paying means paying attention.
5. Set payouts from maximum allowable CAC and tier them across sign-up, activation, and sustained performance.
6. The tiered blend lands below max CAC, improving your ratio and funding contests and recruiting.
7. Multi-level affiliate structures compound — the same monthly effort adds a new permanent revenue layer.
