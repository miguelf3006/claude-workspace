# Chapter 7: Post Free Content II — Monetize Your Audience

## Core Idea
**Give, give, give, give, give — until they ask.** Give in public, ask in private. The moment you start asking for money is the moment you decide to slow your growth, so the more patient you are, the more you get when you finally ask.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Give:Ask Ratio** (benchmarks from mature platforms):
  - Television: 13 minutes of ads per 60 minutes → **~3.5:1** give-to-ask
  - Facebook feed: roughly 4 content posts per 1 ad → **~4:1**
  - These are *mature* platforms optimizing for monetization, not growth. **Growing** platforms show lots of content and almost no ads — they dramatically over-give and under-ask. **If you want to grow, model the growing platforms, not the mature ones.**
- **Give Until They Ask** (the recommended strategy): If you give enough, people start asking *you*. It makes people uncomfortable to keep receiving without giving back. They'll go to your website, DM you, email you. Benefits: you get the **best customers** (the biggest givers, who feel they get the better end of the deal even as paying customers), and **your growth never slows**.
- **Two Ways to Weave in Asks**:
  - **Integrated** — advertise inside every piece, keeping the ratio high. An hour-long podcast with 3 × 30-second ads = 58.5 minutes giving to 1.5 minutes asking, well above 3:1.
  - **Intermittent** — many pure-give pieces, then an occasional ask piece (10 gives, then promote on the 11th).
  - **Rule of thumb**: short-form platforms → intermittent dominates. Long-form platforms → integrations are usually best.
- **Two Scaling Strategies**:
  - **Depth then width** — maximize one platform (short form: up to ~10×/day; long form: up to ~5 days/week), then add the next while maintaining. *Advantages*: maximize return on one skill set, audiences compound, fewer resources. *Disadvantages*: less low-hanging fruit, no omnipresence, and **single-channel risk** — platforms change and sometimes ban you for no reason.
  - **Width then depth** — get on every relevant platform early, maintain, then maximize all at once. *Advantages*: broader reach faster, heavy content repurposing (one short video formats across all short-video platforms cheaply). *Disadvantages*: more labor, attention, and time; people often end up with lots of bad content everywhere.
- **The Seven Lessons**:
  1. **Switch "how to" → "how I."** "I make my oatmeal this way" beats "you should make your oatmeal this way." When you talk about experience, no one can question you. This makes you bulletproof.
  2. **We need to be reminded more than we need to be taught.** He posts about his book daily; surveyed, **1 in 5** who saw the post didn't know he had one. You'll get bored of your content long before your audience has seen it.
  3. **Puddles, ponds, lakes, oceans.** Narrow the focus. Become king of the puddle (plumbing in one town), then expand to the local-business pond, the brick-and-mortar-chain lake, and eventually the ocean of general business.
  4. **Content creates tools for salespeople.** Keep a master list of greatest hits, labelled by the problem each solves and the benefit it provides, so sales can send them before or after calls.
  5. **Free content retains paying customers.** Buyers are *more* likely to consume your free content, and they fold it into how they calculate ROI on the paid thing.
  6. **People don't have shorter attention spans — they have higher standards.** Biology hasn't changed; circumstances have. They have more rewarding options.
  7. **Avoid pre-scheduling posts.** Manually posted content performs better — the close feedback loop makes you try harder. Someone should press submit.

## Key Concepts
- **Goodwill as a bank account**: Rewarding content deposits it; offers withdraw from it. Deposits make the audience pay more attention *and* make them more likely to do what you ask.
- **Asks are commercials you run on yourself**: Free in money, paid for in trust, growth rate, and the time it took to build the audience.
- **The PS statement**: One of the most-read parts of anything written, because it summarizes the main action the author wants. One of Hormozi's favourite places to put an ask.
- **One CTA at a time**: "A confused mind doesn't buy." Don't ask people to share, like, subscribe, *and* comment — they'll do none. Pick one. *"Make up your mind so they don't have to."*
- **Ask target**: When you ask, advertise either your **core offer** or your **lead magnet**. Nothing else. If unsure, use the lead magnet — it's lower risk.

## Mental Models
- **Don't kill your golden goose.** A friend's podcast blew up, he monetized eagerly, asked too often — and it didn't just stop growing, **it shrank**.
- **Under-ask deliberately.** "I try to under-ask my audience and build as much goodwill as possible."
- **The best day to start posting content was the day you were born. The second best day is today.**

## Anti-patterns
- **Asking too frequently after early success**: The single most common way audiences die.
- **Multi-part CTAs**: share + like + subscribe + comment = nothing.
- **Making general content for a local business**: The audience will listen to people with better track records than you. Narrow first.
- **Pre-scheduling everything**: Removes the pressure that makes content good.

## Worked Example
**The paid-ads mystery, January 2020.** Hormozi called every major department into a room to answer why paid advertising had stopped working like it used to. The team debated creative, copy, offer, pages, sales process, price. Leila asked a different question: **"What did we stop doing in the months before the decline?"** Unanimous answer: *Alex stopped making gym content and started talking about general business.*

He surveyed gym owners, asking whether they'd consumed his content before booking a call. **78% of all clients had consumed at least two long-form pieces before booking.** He had given paid ads all the credit; free content had been nurturing the demand the whole time.

**The takeaway he draws:** free content gives strangers the chance to find, get value from, and share your stuff — *and it warms the fence-sitters who go to and come from your cold-audience methods.* Even when it's hard to measure, it improves the return on every other advertising method.

**His actual production process** (he is not a full-time creator; he runs businesses):
1. Find topics using the five topic categories — **about an hour**.
2. Sit down **twice a month** and record ~30 short clips from those topics.
3. Same day, record **2–4 longer videos** unpacking tweets that had more story or examples.

*"If this sounds simplistic, it's because it is. Just start. You can add volume over time."*

**A lead-magnet ask, integrated:** after a post about getting more leads — *"I have 11 more tips that have helped me do this. Go to my site to grab a pretty visual of them."* The thank-you page after the opt-in then displays the paid offer with a video explaining how it works.

**A core-offer ask, direct:** *"I'm looking for five [avatar] to help achieve [dream outcome] in [time delay]. The best part is you don't have to [effort and sacrifice]. And if you don't get [dream outcome], I'll do two things: hand you your money back, and work with you until you do. I do this because I want everyone to have an amazing experience with us, and because I'm confident I can deliver on my promise. If that sounds fair — DM me / book a call / comment below."* Then get back to providing value.

## Key Takeaways
1. Give until they ask. It produces the best customers and never slows growth.
2. Model growing platforms (over-give, under-ask), not mature ones (3.5:1).
3. Choose integrated asks for long-form, intermittent asks for short-form.
4. Ask for exactly one thing per CTA.
5. Advertise only the core offer or the lead magnet — default to the lead magnet.
6. Pick depth-then-width or width-then-depth deliberately; single-channel dependence is a real business risk.
7. Free content raises the return on every other channel, even when attribution can't see it.
