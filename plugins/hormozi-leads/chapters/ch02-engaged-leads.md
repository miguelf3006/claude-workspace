# Chapter 2: Leads Alone Aren't Enough — Engaged Leads

## Core Idea
**A lead is a person you can contact. That's all.** But leads alone aren't enough — you want **engaged leads**: people who show interest in the stuff you sell. Engaged leads are the true output of advertising.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Lead (definition)**: A person you can contact. A bought email list, contacts scraped from a database, the numbers in your phone, people on the street — if you can contact them, they're leads.
- **Engaged Lead (definition)**: A lead who shows interest in the stuff you sell. Someone who gives their contact info on your website, follows you where you can contact them, or replies to your email campaign.
  - Why the distinction matters: "Words matter because they affect how we think. How we think affects what we do. And if words have us thinking the wrong way, then we will probably do the wrong stuff."
- **Test the Offer, Not the Product**: "Spend your time testing the offer rather than perfecting an untested product."

## Key Concepts
- **A raised hand is where this book ends**: Getting strangers to *show interest*, not to buy. Buying is sales — a different book.
- **The subtitle test result**: "How to get **strangers** to want to buy your stuff," not "customers" — because these people don't know you yet.
- **Give people something they want**: The whole discovery, stated plainly. Not what you want to make.

## Mental Models
- **Think "engaged leads" every time you're tempted to count raw leads.** A list of 10,000 unengaged contacts is not better than 50 engaged ones.
- **Advertising's output metric is engagement, not impressions or list size.**
- **Simple beats polished when you're testing.** The winning asset was a 13-minute screen recording with no slides and nobody presenting.

## Anti-patterns
- **Perfecting an untested product**: Eight Sundays building a webinar nobody watched.
- **Copying the mechanism rather than the principle**: The webinar worked for someone else in the same group in the same industry. It failed for Hormozi because he lacked the skill *at the time*, not because webinars don't work. (He didn't try another for years — the scar tissue was itself a cost.)
- **Measuring your progress against someone else's forum posts**: The comparison spiral that preceded the breakthrough.

## Worked Example
**The webinar failure and the case-study breakthrough.** Hormozi paid $25,000 to join a group where everyone told him to run a webinar. He spent eight Sundays assembling landing pages, thank-you pages, follow-up emails, cart-close emails, payment processing, and ad creative. He launched at $150/day. **Three days, $450 spent, 80 leads, zero sales.** Nobody even watched.

Then he saw an ad in his feed: *"Free case study on how I spent $1 and made $123,000 in a weekend."* He entered his email and got a video of a guy walking through a campaign — no slides, no presenter, just explanation. *"This I can do."*

He fired up a screen recorder and made his own in about 13 minutes:

> "Here's the ad account of a gym we just launched. Here are the ads we ran. This is how much we spent. We sent them to this page with this offer. You can see how many leads we got here. They got this many people scheduled. This many showed. This is how many they sold. This is how much the gym owner made. This is everything we did. If you want help setting something like this up, we'll do the whole thing for free — and we only get paid off the sales you make. If that sounds fair, book a call."

He swapped the webinar for the video and changed the headline to *"Free case study: how we added 213 members and $112,000 in revenue to a small gym in San Diego."* Next page: book a call. He set up the campaign and went to bed.

The next morning, Leila: *"Strangers have booked my calendar solid for like the next week."*

**The lesson he draws:** "They didn't want my webinar. They wanted my case study. You have to give people something they want."

## Key Takeaways
1. Lead = a person you can contact. Engaged lead = one showing interest. Optimize for the second.
2. Engaged leads are the true output of advertising — make that your reported number.
3. Test offers, don't perfect untested products.
4. The winning asset is often far simpler and faster to make than the one you're avoiding.
5. A method failing for you doesn't mean the method fails — often it means you lacked the skill at that moment.
