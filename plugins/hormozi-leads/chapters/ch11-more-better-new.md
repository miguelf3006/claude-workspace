# Chapter 11: Core Four on Steroids — More, Better, New

## Core Idea
When you're doing the Core Four and still want more leads, there are exactly three moves: **do more** of what you're doing, do it **better**, or do it somewhere **new**. Most businesses plateau because they mistake their tiny advertising slice for the whole market.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **More, Better, New**: (1) more of what you're currently doing, (2) the same thing done better, (3) the same thing somewhere new.
  - **Default order below $1M/year profit: More → Better → New.** You haven't done enough volume for percentage improvements to matter.
  - **Above $1M/year profit: flip to Better → More → New.** At that scale, improvement becomes the lowest-cost, highest-return move.
- **The Rule of 100**: **100 primary actions every day for 100 days in a row.**

| Core Four method | The 100 |
|---|---|
| Warm outreach | 100 reach-outs/day — email, text, DM, calls |
| Post free content | 100 minutes/day making content; release ≥1/day, more as you improve |
| Cold outreach | 100 reach-outs/day — email, text, DM, cold call, flyers (expect lower response; use automation) |
| Paid ads | 100 minutes/day making ads; run them 100 days straight on the reverse-engineered budget |

  > "If you do 100 primary actions per day and you do it for 100 days straight, you will get more engaged leads. Commit to the Rule of 100 and you will never go hungry again."
- **Constraints (for "better")**: The step where the most leads drop off. "Constraints are the points where the smallest improvements create the biggest boost in results."
- **The Testing Discipline**: **One test per week per platform.** Four reasons: (1) testing multiple things at once means you never learn what worked; (2) steps affect each other — more opt-ins but fewer applications is a loss you'd never see; (3) it forces prioritization, since tests are infinite and time isn't — don't waste a weekly test on red vs. bright red; (4) it runs long enough to produce real data without wasting time you could spend on the next constraint.
  - **The Monday ritual**: pick winners for each platform test → **write results in a log of all tests** so next time you start "a zillion improvements later, not at square one" → design the next test to beat the current best. **If you can't beat the current version in 4 tries or 1 month, move to the next constraint.**
- **The Size of the Pie Fallacy**: A small business uses one of the Core Four, on one platform, in one specific way, to one targeted audience — sees a handful of competitors doing the same — and concludes that slice is the entire market. *"This is why most businesses stay small. For many, saying 'I'm as big as I can get' is much easier than saying 'I'm not as good at advertising as I thought.'"*
- **When to do New**: When the returns from more and better fall below what a new placement or new way of advertising would give you.

## Key Concepts
- **Front vs. back**: The lowest-percentage steps usually sit at the front (1% click an ad, then 30% give contact info), so the front is usually the constraint — call out, value elements, offer, CTA, landing page headline, sub-headline, image.
- **New placements exist inside platforms you already use**: Instagram has stories, messenger ads, and posts; YouTube has sidebar, mid-stream, and pre-roll.
- **Volume of creative is a documented winner**: Facebook reviewed all advertiser accounts and found the **top 0.1% of advertisers test 11× more creative than everyone else.** *"It's not that you can't scale an ad profitably. You just can't scale a mediocre ad profitably. And the only way to find the exceptional ads is to make more of them."*

## Mental Models
- **"Do more until it breaks, then make it better."** More raises CAC until it's unsustainable; that's the signal to start testing.
- **Improve the constraint, not everything.** A uniform 5-point improvement gives wildly unequal results — improving the bottleneck wins by a mile.
- **Success leaves clues.** Do what the 0.1% do and get what the 0.1% get.

## Anti-patterns
- **Declaring market saturation**: The most common and most expensive self-diagnosis in the book.
- **Testing many things simultaneously**: You learn nothing and can't attribute the result.
- **Not logging tests**: You restart from square one every time someone new touches the account.
- **Optimizing a non-constraint**: Effort spent where drop-off is small returns almost nothing.

## Worked Example
**The saturation conversation** (Q&A after a lead-gen talk, ~50 entrepreneurs in the room):

> "I just feel like I've saturated the market. I don't think we can get any bigger in the chiropractor niche."
> — *"What are you doing in revenue?"* "$2 million a year."
> — *"How much do you spend on advertising?"* "About $30,000 a month on Facebook."
> — *"What's your conversion rate from click to close?"* "I don't know."
> — *"So you don't track overall throughput?"* "I guess not."
> — *"What other platforms do you advertise on?"* "None."
> — *"How much content do you make for chiropractors?"* "None."
> — *"How much cold outreach do you do?"* "None."
> — *"And $30,000 on one platform for a $2 million business saturated the **$15.1 billion** chiropractor industry. Does that sound reasonable?"*

Before he could answer, a second owner spoke up: *"If it helps, I'm in the chiropractor niche too, and I spent **$30,000 across four platforms last week.**"*

A parallel case: an entrepreneur doing ~$3M/year in weight loss worried that going past **$40,000/month** would saturate a platform with **over 1 billion daily active users**, selling into a **$60 billion** US industry.

**Constraint math** — why you improve the bottleneck, not everything:

| Step | Current | +5 points | Relative lift |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opt-in | 30% | 35% | **1.16×** (+16%) |
| Apply | 5% ← **constraint** | 10% | **2.0×** (+100%) |
| Schedule | 50% | 55% | **1.1×** (+10%) |

Identical absolute improvement, wildly different outcomes. The constraint wins by a mile — *and* it's the step where the most leads were dropping off in the first place.

## Key Takeaways
1. Three moves only: more, better, new. Below $1M profit, start with more.
2. Run the Rule of 100 — 100 primary actions daily for 100 days.
3. Do more until CAC breaks, then start testing.
4. Find the constraint by locating the biggest drop-off; test there.
5. One test per week per platform, logged, with a 4-try or 1-month give-up rule.
6. Test far more creative than feels necessary — the top 0.1% test 11× more.
7. Reject the size-of-the-pie fallacy. The market is almost always vastly bigger than your slice of it.
