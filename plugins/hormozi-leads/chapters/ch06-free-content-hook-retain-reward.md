# Chapter 6: Post Free Content I — Hook, Retain, Reward

## Core Idea
**The content isn't the compounding asset. The audience is.** Content disappears; the audience keeps growing. And all audience-growing content is made of the same basic unit: **hook → retain → reward.**

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Content Unit**: The smallest amount of material that hooks, retains, and rewards attention. It can be an image, a meme, or a single sentence — which is how a short tweet or a jingle goes viral: it does all three at once.
  - **Hook** — give them a reason to redirect attention toward you. Measured by the *percentage of people who start consuming*.
  - **Retain** — get them to keep consuming.
  - **Reward** — satisfy the reason they consumed it to begin with.
  - **Long-form content is just content units linked together.** More links = more skill required.
- **The Five Topic Categories** (for hooks):
  1. **Far past** — important life lessons connected to your product. *"Give them the story without the scar."*
  2. **Recent past** — do stuff, then talk about what happened. Mine last week's calendar, meetings, social interactions, and warm-outreach conversations. Testimonials and case studies live here.
  3. **Present** — record ideas the moment they arrive. Keep capture within arm's reach; pause meetings to note them. Post ideas publicly as they happen and let performance tell you what's interesting.
  4. **Trending** — go where the attention is; apply your own experience to it.
  5. **Manufactured** — create the experience, then make content about it. *"I lived on $100 for a month. Here's how."* Highest cost, biggest potential payout.
- **The Eight Headline Components** (from a meta-analysis of news — **include at least two**):
  - **Recency** — as recent as possible
  - **Relevancy** — personally meaningful (nurses care about nurse things)
  - **Celebrity** — prominent people, authorities (nobody cares what a human eats for breakfast; everyone cares what Bezos eats)
  - **Proximity** — geographically close to home (a house fire across the country vs. your neighbor's)
  - **Conflict** — opposing ideas, people, or nature (pineapple vs. no pineapple; freedom vs. security)
  - **Unusual** — odd, rare, bizarre (the six-fingered man at the circus)
  - **Ongoing** — stories still in progress, dynamic, with plot twists (someone in labor: updates every 10 minutes)
- **The Three Retention Devices** — embed unresolved questions:
  - **Lists** — things, facts, tips, opinions presented one after another under a theme. Stating the number up front retains longer. Flexible, less explicit reward.
  - **Steps** — actions in a required order that accomplish a goal. Less flexible, more explicit reward.
  - **Stories** — events real or imaginary, with a lesson. Drive curiosity because people want to know what happens next.
  - Interweave freely: lists within steps, a story per list item, ongoing storylines.

## Key Concepts
- **Value per second**: How good your content is = how often it rewards, per unit of consumption time.
- **"There's no such thing as too long, only too boring."** The same person who bails 3 seconds into a 10-second video will binge a 900-page book or 8 hours of television.
- **Format matching**: People consume content because it resembles things they've liked before. Match the platform's popular format first, then tweak to hook your ideal audience. *"Better-looking content will hook them before yours even has a chance."*
- **Trade-offs of content**: harder to personalize (fewer respond) · you compete with everyone else posting · if you stand out, people copy you, so you must keep innovating.

## Mental Models
- **Think of the audience as the asset, the content as the payment.** This is what Hormozi's ego blocked him from learning for a full year.
- **Curiosity is the best retention driver** because "if done correctly, people will wait years."
- **The audience decides, not you.** "I've had tons of content I thought would smash records, but the audience smashed the next button instead."
- **Growth is the scoreboard.** "If it's not growing, your stuff isn't that good. Practice and you'll get better."
- **Documenting beats manufacturing for most people** — lower cost keeps volume high and is more sustainable over a lifetime. *"I don't want to be filling my living room with sand when I'm 50."*

## Anti-patterns
- **Blaming content quality when the issue is volume**: the entire lesson of the influencer call below.
- **Failing the reward promise**: hook says seven ways to make up with your spouse, and you give four — or seven they've heard — or you're addressing a room of single guys. They won't watch again and certainly won't share.
- **Optimizing the hook while ignoring format**: no matter how good the content is, mismatched formatting kills it before it starts.

## Worked Example
**The $120,000 phone calls.** Leila bought Hormozi four calls with a big influencer whose audience he wanted. On call one, the advice was: **post regularly on every platform.** He did. Twelve months later his audience had grown by **200,000+ people**.

On call two he asked for the blueprint. The response:

> "Bro, anyone telling you there's some secret is trying to sell you something. We just put out as much as we possibly can. Pull up your Instagram and pull up mine. Look — you've posted once today. I posted three times. Pull up your LinkedIn. You posted once this week. I posted five times today."

He went platform by platform. Over the next six months Hormozi put out **10× the content** — and added **1.2 million** people. *"Volume works. Content works. A growing audience is the result."*

**A complete content unit in three sentences** (his own example, on the belief "I don't have enough time"):
1. **Hook** — "I complained to a friend that I didn't have enough time to do something, while glued to my phone."
2. **Retain** — "They yanked it out of my hands and looked at the usage. It showed I spent 3 hours a day on social media."
3. **Reward** — "They looked back at me and said: *Hey, I found you some time.*"

Relatable, connects his domain (growing businesses) to a struggle many people have, and gives the epiphany away.

**Retention devices in headline form:**

| Device | Example |
|---|---|
| List | "7 ways I invested $1,000 in my 20s that paid off big" / "28 ways to stay poor" |
| Steps | "3 steps to creating a great hook" / "How I create a headline in 7 steps" |
| Story | "My journey from sleeping on the bottom floor of a gym to the top floor of a five-star hotel" |

## Key Takeaways
1. Build the audience, not the content library. The audience compounds.
2. Every piece of content must hook, retain, and reward — long form just links more units.
3. Source topics from your own life across five time horizons. Nobody else can say what you can say.
4. Put at least two of the eight news components in every headline.
5. Retain with lists, steps, and stories; embed unresolved questions.
6. Match the platform's format before optimizing anything else.
7. Volume works. When in doubt, post more.
