# Chapter 16: Get Started — Open to Goal & The One-Page Plan

## Core Idea
Advertising is an **inputs and outputs game**. Most people dramatically underestimate the volume required — not by half or a third, but by orders of magnitude. The fix is the Rule of 100, then **open to goal**, then a **one-page plan** that leaves no room for excuses.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Open to Goal**: You don't commit to doing something a specific number of times — **you commit to working until you hit a specific number of outcomes, no matter what.** It might mean doing something 50 times, or 5,000 times a day for years. "The Rule of 100 for the big kids."
  - Origin: a successful gym chain let sales managers set their own schedules with one catch — **sign up five new members per day, no matter what**. Done by lunch? Cut out early. Takes 18 hours? So be it.
  - *"Give up the idea of doing your best. Instead, do what is required — and sometimes that means your best just needs to get better."*
- **The One-Page Advertising Plan** (five steps):
  1. Pick the type of engaged lead to get: **customers, affiliates, employees, or agencies**
  2. Pick **Rule of 100** or **open to goal** — commit to your daily advertising actions
  3. Fill out the **advertising daily checklist** for that action
  4. Do that daily action until you can afford to pay someone else to do it
  5. When you can, **go back to step 1 — make employees your new target lead type** — and repeat until you have the help you need. Then scale again.
- **The Advertising Daily Checklist**:

| Field | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Who** | You |
| **What** | Your offer or lead magnet |
| **Where** | Platform |
| **To whom** | Audience / list |
| **When** | First 8 hours |
| **Why** | Get X engaged leads or lead getters |
| **How** | Warm/cold outreach · content · ads |
| **How much** | 100, or until you hit your goal |
| **How many** | Number of follow-ups / times to retarget |
| **How long** | 100 days, or until you hit your goal |

- **The High-ROI Habit Stack** (Hormozi's three habits):
  1. **Wake up early** (4–5 a.m. — *which actually means going to bed early*)
  2. **Get right to work** — no rituals, no routines. Coffee, then work
  3. **No meetings until noon** — no interruptions, fully focused work time
  - *"I don't think there's any magic to waking up early. But there is magic in a long stretch of uninterrupted work immediately after a long stretch of uninterrupted sleep."* Only after the block does he put out fires, talk to humans, and handle day-to-day. **This book was written exclusively in that open-to-goal time block.**

## Key Concepts
- **The right action in the wrong amount still fails.**
- **"Success comes down to doing the obvious thing for an uncommonly long period of time without convincing yourself you're smarter than you are."** (Neil Strauss)
- **"Do more than they do and you will have more than they have."**
- **The correction to the classic complaint**: *"I reached out to 100 people over the last 6 weeks and only got one customer — it doesn't work."* → **You did 1/42nd of the required work.** It was 100 **per day**, not 100 over time.
- **Reading doesn't get people interested in the stuff you sell. Advertising does.**

## Mental Models
- **Never let effort be the reason something didn't work.** "It could be something else — the offer, the copy, the image, the targeting, the media, the platform, the position of the moon. But not my effort."
- **One page, not one hundred.** "Many skip planning, or worse, write a 100-page plan that never gets used. Harness the power of laying out your action steps on a single page. It leaves little room for excuses, distractions, and delusions. **You either did the stuff or you didn't.**" It takes about five minutes to fill out.
- **Employees are the compounding step.** As soon as you can afford to hand off a daily action, the next lead type you advertise for is talent.

## Anti-patterns
- **Undersized tests**: Concluding a channel doesn't work from a sample far too small to produce a readable signal.
- **Confusing "my best" with "what's required"**: Open to goal exists precisely because those are different numbers.
- **Writing the long plan**: "Skip the atrocious waste of time that is writing pages of baloney."

## Worked Example
**The 300 flyers.** Hormozi's paid ads at his first gym were getting more expensive, so a mentor who ran a chain of tanning salons suggested flyers. They printed **300** and put them on cars near the gym.

Day one: nothing. Day two: the phone rang. *"Hey, you put a flyer on my car."* His heart raced — it worked! *"Yes, yes I did, how can I—"* *"Yeah, you scratched my Mercedes. You're going to need to pay for—"* He panicked and hung up. That was the only call. **No leads. Nothing.**

Weeks later he called the mentor to complain:

> "How many leads did you get from them?" — *"None."*
> "Hm, that's odd. What was your test size?" — *"I put out 300."*
> "Shoot. You only put out **300**? Hard to know if anything works with such a small number. **I test with 5,000. Then when we find a winner, we put out 5,000 per day, every day, for a month.**"
> "If we get half a percent, that's decent. If we get 1%, that's a winner. With 300 flyers, half a percent would be like one and a half people. That makes it pretty hard to know if you've got a winner."

The mentor tested with **almost 17× more than the entire campaign — in a single day.** Hormozi's own summary: *"I was doing 1/500th the level of effort required to make a flyer campaign work. I just didn't know it. I felt like the person who says exercise doesn't work after going to the gym one time."*

The promise he made himself afterwards: **"I would never let effort be the reason anything didn't work for me."**

## Key Takeaways
1. Advertising is inputs → outputs. Low-effort inputs produce low, unreliable output.
2. Test at a size large enough to read a signal — 0.5% is decent, 1% is a winner, so 300 tells you nothing.
3. Graduate from Rule of 100 (a set number of actions) to open to goal (a set number of outcomes).
4. Build the day around one long uninterrupted block placed immediately after sleep.
5. Fill out the one-page checklist in five minutes; it removes every excuse.
6. As soon as you can afford it, advertise for employees and repeat the whole loop.
7. Reading doesn't generate leads. Doing does.
