# Chapter 7: Attraction Offers — Pay Less Now or Pay More Later

## Core Idea
Give people a choice: **pay full price later with a conditional guarantee**, or **pay a discounted price now with bonuses**. The pay-later option removes risk to get them in; the pay-now option collects cash today.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Two-Option Structure**:
  - **Pay later** — put a card down for $0 today and get billed later. If the promised result doesn't happen, cancel before the deadline. **But it's conditional** — they must do the qualifying thing (attend, show up, submit data) to be eligible to cancel.
  - **Pay now** — a **20–50% discount** plus bonuses, in exchange for paying today. They give up the guarantee.
- **The Sequence**: Offer **pay later first**. Once they accept, *then* present the pay-now option. If they take it, they get the discount and bonuses instead of the guarantee.
- **The Promise Requirements**: Make the promise a **clear yes/no result**, make sure you can **deliver it inside your timeframe**, and make it **easy to track and difficult to refute**. Example: promising to reduce shoulder pain → have them rate pain 1–10 before, then after. If it went down, you succeeded and can sell them something else.
- **The Conditional Satisfaction Guarantee**: People can only cancel if they qualify. *"They can't say you suck if they never try it."* Track the conditions — attendance, showing up to an appointment, turning in data — and **make the criteria the things people do to get the most value out of the product.** Win-win.
- **The Optimization Dials**:
  - Too many taking **pay later** → discount the pay-now option more, add better bonuses, or both
  - Too many taking **pay now** → do the opposite
  - **More than 10% of pay-later people cancelling** → you promised too much, the guarantee conditions are too low, or the price is too high

## Key Concepts
- **Some cancellations are unavoidable.** "No matter how well you deliver, some people will cancel their payment. That's okay. Factor it into your costs of doing business and live your life."
- **It works for recurring revenue too**: give them the option to pay a higher ongoing rate starting 30 days later, or pay less today and **keep the lower rate for good.**
- **Hint at your next offer early** — one of the most transferable tactics in the book. If the speed-reading guru had said *"Everyone wants to know when my next reading intensive starts because they sell out so fast — I'll get to it at the end, but please pay attention, I want to deliver on my promise first,"* he would have sold more of it.

## Mental Models
- **The guarantee is the price of the pay-later option.** They're buying certainty; when they buy the discount instead, they're selling that certainty back to you.
- **Align guarantee conditions with success behaviours.** Every condition you attach should be something that makes the product work better. Then qualifying for the guarantee and getting the result are the same activity.
- **Extra attention to would-be cancellers.** "Give extra attention to those who claim they haven't received what was promised, *before* the cancellation deadline."

## Anti-patterns
- **A vague or unmeasurable promise**: Produces unnecessary cancellations and disputes. Keep it simple, clear, and measurable.
- **Conditions set too low**: Anyone can qualify to cancel, so the guarantee costs you without filtering anyone.
- **Waiting until the end to mention the next offer**: You forfeit the priming that makes it sell.

## Worked Example
**Where he encountered it.** A headline: *"Double your reading speed in 3 hours or it's free."* The registration page offered:

> **Option A** — Put your credit card down for **$0** and get billed $97 tomorrow. If your reading speed doesn't double, email us before then and we'll cancel the charge. **But you must attend in order to be eligible.**
> **Option B** — Pay **$97 right now** and as a free bonus get the recordings, which won't be for sale anywhere else.

He took option A — he wanted to see whether his reading speed doubled first. Note both halves of the design: the guarantee is **conditional on attendance** (the behaviour that makes the product work), and the pay-now option trades the guarantee for a **bonus that's genuinely unavailable elsewhere.**

**The accidental discovery that became a tactic.** Hormozi ran nutrition consultations where people constantly interrupted to ask about supplements. One tired day he snapped: *"Everyone wants to know what supplements to buy. We'll get there, I promise — but please pay attention to the nutrition section, it matters more."* By accident he had **implied everyone bought supplements without offering them.** The head nods confirmed the demand, and far more people bought when they finally got to ask. *"A happy mistake I made sure to repeat."*

**Templates across business types:**

| Business | Pay later | Pay now | Upsell that follows |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Education / workshop** | $0 today | **$299** for 3-day workshop + recordings + 1-on-1 call with a certified expert + printed materials delivered at the workshop | **$30,000** to take you through every step of closing your first deal within 6 months, plus legal templates, an advisor to vet the investment, inspection checklist |
| **Local service (lawn)** | $0 now — lawn cut + hedges, then **$599 after** | **$369** — lawn cut + hedges + lawn treatment | ~$100/month lawn care. *The rep makes the estimate at the house, offers both options, then upsells after the work is done* |
| **Physical product (clothing)** | $0 now, billed **$149 in 14 days** | **$97** of clothing plus an accessory that goes with it | Monthly subscription to more clothes like this. *Customers must return the product in like-new condition before billing to qualify* |

## Key Takeaways
1. Two doors: risk-free later, or cheaper-with-bonuses now. Present later first.
2. The guarantee must be conditional, and the conditions must be the success behaviours.
3. Make the promise a binary, measurable, in-timeframe result.
4. Discount the pay-now option 20–50% and load it with bonuses.
5. Tune the split by adjusting the pay-now discount and bonuses in either direction.
6. Over 10% cancelling is a signal — over-promise, weak conditions, or wrong price.
7. Hint at the next offer early in any event, workshop, or presentation.
