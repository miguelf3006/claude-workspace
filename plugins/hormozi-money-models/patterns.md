# Patterns — $100M Money Models

## Win Your Money Back
**When to use**: You want the steepest possible discount (100%) without giving product away, and you sell something people start and quit.
**How**: Set a goal and the path to it. They get money back — cash or store credit — for hitting **results**, taking **actions**, or **both**. Make every condition trackable and make the actions the ones that cause success. Required check-ins become upsell slots.
**Trade-offs**: ~10% will ask for money back. You must have an upsell ready for winners to apply credit toward. Store credit converts as well as cash.

## Free Giveaway
**When to use**: You want many qualified leads fast and have something genuinely grand to give away.
**How**: Grand prize (with a stated value that anchors) → promotional offer for everyone else → contact info + eligibility questions + qualifying actions → deadline → announce winner publicly, contact everyone else privately. Discount = 10–30% of gross margins.
**Trade-offs**: Heavily regulated — consult counsel. Cap entries at what you can call in 7 days. If it flops, the prize wasn't grand enough.

## Decoy Offer
**When to use**: Leads are expensive and you need a cheap front door to a premium sale.
**How**: Advertise the stripped version (fewer components, no personalization, **no guarantee**). In the room, ask *"Are you here for free stuff or lasting results?"*, present both, and let them name which reaches the goal faster.
**Trade-offs**: Free gets more leads; discount gets better show-up rates. If it isn't making money fast, the contrast is too small.

## Buy X, Get Y Free
**When to use**: You sell something people reasonably buy more of, or want longer access to.
**How**: Raise prices permanently first. Then reframe: three for $30 becomes *one for $30, get two free.* **Always give more free items than paid ones.** Free items may differ from paid ones.
**Trade-offs**: Generates large cash inflows against long delivery obligations. Budget the servicing money separately.

## Pay Less Now or Pay More Later
**When to use**: One-time products or services where you can prove a binary result quickly.
**How**: Present **pay later** first ($0 today, billed later, cancellable only if they meet conditions). Once accepted, present **pay now** — 20–50% off plus bonuses, forgoing the guarantee.
**Trade-offs**: >10% cancelling means you over-promised, set conditions too low, or priced too high.

## Classic Upsell
**When to use**: Immediately after any purchase, when the next problem becomes visible.
**How**: Know their next problem better than they do, then solve it for money. Direction: more (quantity), better (quality), or different (complementary).
**Trade-offs**: Timing dominates. Offering before the problem is felt reads as a pitch.

## Menu Upsell
**When to use**: You have multiple offers available and a face-to-face or live moment.
**How**: **Unsell** what they don't need → **prescribe** what they do, in personalized detail → **A/B** their preference → **card on file.** Add a nudge to every A/B.
**Trade-offs**: Requires real product knowledge and staff willing to talk customers out of things. Sold out? Take payment and set a delivery expectation.

## Anchor Upsell
**When to use**: High-ticket sales where the customer's budget is a guess.
**How**: Present the expensive thing → watch for the gasp → supply a face-saving reason (*"Do you care about the designer much?"*) → descend keeping the primary feature → sell accessories last.
**Trade-offs**: An anchor without a graceful exit embarrasses the customer out the door.

## Rollover Upsell
**When to use**: Lapsed customers, upset customers, competitors' upset customers, or current customers.
**How**: Credit past spending toward a next offer priced at **≥4× the credit** (so a full rollover is ≤25% off). Apply upfront or spread over time. Make it one-time-only.
**Trade-offs**: Always try this before issuing a refund. Negative reviews are a prospecting list.

## Payment Plan Downsell
**When to use**: They want it and genuinely can't pay in full today.
**How**: Same product, split across an upfront portion and scheduled payments. Ask what they *can* do rather than assuming the split.
**Trade-offs**: Makes money one way, loses it two — cancellations before profit, and full-pay customers taking a plan and cancelling early.

## Trial with Penalty
**When to use**: Recurring products or services where the customer must do work to get results.
**How**: Offer last → **always get a card** → sell staying and paying → **explain fees after the card** → make check-ins required. Reuse your win-your-money-back criteria as the terms.
**Trade-offs**: Nobody takes it → lower requirements. They take it but don't follow through → emphasize how fees help and make sales meetings mandatory. Just call it a free trial.

## Feature Downsell
**When to use**: A price objection on a high-ticket item where you refuse to discount.
**How**: Remove a feature to justify a lower price. Guarantees are features. *"If you don't want the option to get your money back, you can pay less — or keep the guarantee. Which would you prefer?"*
**Trade-offs**: Customers discover the feature's value in its removal, which is why more people also buy the main thing.

## Continuity Bonus
**When to use**: Converting buyers to recurring revenue.
**How**: Advertise the **bonus**, not the membership. Anchor its value, then: *"Do you want to know how you can get this for free?"* Stack additional bonuses with individual dollar values. Use the standalone-price ratio to control what share picks continuity.
**Trade-offs**: Keep bonuses related to the core offer or you attract the wrong customers. Use real, defensible values.

## Continuity Discount
**When to use**: Long-cycle services where a commitment is normal (internet, cleaning, gyms, landscaping, rentals).
**How**: Free time in exchange for a term. Apply the discount upfront, at the end, evenly spread, or **after the first one or two payments** (preferred — collects cash and validates the card). Extend the term rather than consuming it.
**Trade-offs**: Front-loading converts more but churns more; back-loading converts fewer but churns less.

## Waived Fee
**When to use**: Any continuity business where you want term commitments without hard-selling them.
**How**: Setup fee of **3–5× the monthly rate** for month-to-month, waived entirely for a term. Payable if they break the term. Have them initial that they understand.
**Trade-offs**: Fees get them to start *and* to stick — because leaving costs more than staying.

## Continuity Operating Tweaks
**When to use**: Any recurring business, immediately.
**How**: Bill **every four weeks** (13 cycles vs. 12 months = +8.3%/yr) · add a **3% processing fee** · trade the fee for a **second payment method**, ideally ACH · set a **lifetime discount** just past your average churn month · offer **bulk prepaid blocks** (1 in 8 taking "buy 5 get 1 free" lifts 30-day profit ~50%) · price cancellation at the discount received · always run an **exit interview** (saves ~1/3).
**Trade-offs**: Almost none — these are wording and mechanics changes with no extra delivery cost.
