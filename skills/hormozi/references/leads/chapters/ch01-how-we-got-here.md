# Chapter 1: How We Got Here — The Lead-Getting Machine

## Core Idea
There are only two ways to grow: **get more customers** or **make them worth more**. This book is entirely about the first. And "more customers" decomposes into four things: **more leads, better leads, cheaper leads, reliably** — from lots of places.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Two Ways to Grow**: (1) get more customers, (2) make them worth more. That's it. $100M Leads solves #1; $100M Money Models solves #2.
- **The Four Lead Qualities**: more · better · cheaper · reliable (from many sources).
  - When to use: Diagnosing a lead problem. Say which of the four is actually broken before choosing a tactic.
  - Why it matters: "All else being equal, when you double your leads, you double your business."
- **Book Progression Logic**: The sequence runs from *zero clients, zero leads, zero advertising, zero money, zero skills* → *max everything*. Skills come first because more skill means more leads per hour spent; the most complex methods come last because they require skill and money, and both take time.

## Key Concepts
- **When leads flow, it's hard not to make money** — the analogy to cash flow is deliberate.
- **Masters never don't do the basics.** "Respecting the tried-and-true methods that got you to your current level will probably get you to the next one."
- **The five sections**: (1) start here, (2) what makes advertising really work, (3) advertising's Core Four, (4) getting other people to do it for you, (5) the one-page advertising plan.

## Mental Models
- **Think of leads as flow, not stock.** A pile of contacts isn't a business; a repeatable flow is.
- **Diagnose which of the four is broken.** "Not enough leads" and "leads too expensive" and "leads unreliable" are different problems with different fixes.
- **Skills compound faster than budget.** More skill → more leads per unit of time → which is why the book saves paid ads for last, not first.

## Anti-patterns
- **Skipping the basics once you have money and skill**: Hormozi includes this order partly to remind advanced operators "of the basics we stopped doing."
- **Reaching for the most complex channel first**: Paid ads take the most skill and money. Warm outreach takes neither.
- **Confusing revenue growth with lead growth**: If customers aren't worth more, more leads is the only lever — know which one you're pulling.

## Worked Example
**The portfolio results Hormozi uses as proof of the model.** Within the first 12 months, the average Acquisition.com portfolio company achieves **1.8× revenue and 3.01× profit**. Companies partnered 12–24 months average **2.3× revenue and 4.7× profit**. His invitation: plug your own numbers in.

**The business model behind the free book** (and the reason it's a lead magnet — see ch03): (1) provide better free products than the marketplace's paid products; (2) earn the trust of entrepreneurs making over $1M/year in profit; (3) invest in those entrepreneurs to fast-track growth; (4) help everyone else free, for good. *Note the threshold change from the previous book: the minimum moved from $3M in revenue to $1M in profit, and the industry focus widened beyond education and services.*

## Key Takeaways
1. Two growth levers only. Know which one you're working on.
2. "More customers" is four separate problems: more, better, cheaper, reliable.
3. Double your leads, double your business — all else equal.
4. Sequence channels by skill required: warm outreach → content → cold outreach → paid ads.
5. Advanced operators should re-audit the basics before adding complexity.
