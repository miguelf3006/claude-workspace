# Chapter 4: Advertising Simplified — The Core Four

## Core Idea
There are only **four ways** one person can let other people know about anything. Two audience types (warm, cold) × two communication modes (one-to-one, one-to-many) = the **Core Four**. Everything else is a variation.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Core Four**:

| | **One-to-one (private)** | **One-to-many (public)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Warm audience** | **Warm outreach** | **Post free content** |
| **Cold audience** | **Cold outreach** | **Run paid ads** |

  - When to use: Any time you need leads. "If you aren't getting as many leads as you want, you're not doing the Core Four with enough skill or with enough volume."
  - Why it matters: This is the "get out of jail free card you carry around forever." It reduces the overly confused world of advertising to four actions.
- **Warm vs. Cold Audience**:
  - **Warm** = people who gave you permission to contact them — friends, family, followers, current and previous customers, contacts.
  - **Cold** = people who have not — strangers, other people's audiences, purchased or built contact lists, platforms you pay for access to.
- **One-to-One vs. One-to-Many**:
  - **Private**: one person gets a message at a time (phone call, email).
  - **Public**: many get it simultaneously (social post, billboard, podcast).
  - **Automation doesn't change the nature.** Emailing a 10,000-person list is still one-to-one, just executed very fast by a machine.

## Key Concepts
- **The advertising output is engaged leads** — every Core Four method is judged on that, not on impressions.
- **Skill × volume** are the only two dials. Not enough leads means one of the two is too low.
- **The skill ladder built into the ordering**: warm outreach needs no money and no audience; posting content needs skill; cold outreach needs volume and systems; paid ads need money *and* skill.

## Mental Models
- **Think of the Core Four as a 2×2 you can always return to.** When someone pitches you a "new channel," locate it in the grid — it's a variation of one of the four.
- **Every method takes one step closer to overflowing leads.** They're additive, not alternatives.
- **"Either do them and get as many leads as you darn well please, or get crushed by those who do."**

## Anti-patterns
- **Channel shopping**: Hunting for a fifth way instead of raising skill or volume on one of the four.
- **Confusing automation with a different method**: Automated email is one-to-one at machine speed, and behaves like one-to-one — including in how personal it must feel.
- **Starting with paid ads** because they feel like "real" advertising. They require the most skill and money, which is why they come last in the book.

## Worked Example
**Locating common tactics in the grid** (the practical use of the framework):

| Tactic | Audience | Mode | Core Four method |
|---|---|---|---|
| Texting past customers about a new service | Warm | 1-to-1 | Warm outreach |
| Instagram post to your followers | Warm | 1-to-many | Post free content |
| Cold email sequence to a scraped list | Cold | 1-to-1 | Cold outreach |
| Meta ads to a lookalike audience | Cold | 1-to-many | Paid ads |
| Automated email blast to your list | Warm | 1-to-1 (machine-fast) | Warm outreach |
| Podcast guest appearance | Cold | 1-to-many | (Other people's audience — see lead getters) |
| DMs to strangers who liked a competitor's post | Cold | 1-to-1 | Cold outreach |

The diagnostic use: if lead flow is short, name which of the four you're actually doing, then ask whether the deficit is **skill** or **volume**. Those have different fixes — skill means study and reps; volume means more actions per day or more people doing them (Section IV).

## Key Takeaways
1. Four methods, no more: warm outreach, post free content, cold outreach, paid ads.
2. Classify by audience permission (warm/cold) and mode (one-to-one/one-to-many).
3. Automation changes speed, not the nature of the communication.
4. Insufficient leads = insufficient skill or insufficient volume. Diagnose which.
5. Learn all four. Master one and you can feed yourself for life.
