# Chapter 8: Cold Outreach

## Core Idea
Cold outreach is warm outreach's advanced cousin, no longer limited by who you know. Strangers create **three problems** — you can't contact them, they ignore you, and even when they listen they're not interested — and the entire method is built to solve those three in order.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Three Problems (and the order to solve them)**:
  1. **Get a way to contact them** → build a targeted list
  2. **Figure out what to say** → personalize, then give big fast value
  3. **Contact them until they're ready and able to listen** → volume, automation, follow-up
- **Three Ways to Build a List** (work from most accessible to least):
  1. **Software** — subscribe to as many scraping tools as you can, search by your criteria, test a representative sample (a few hundred) from each. If the contact info is current, the leads are responsive, and they're who the software claims — take everything it will give.
  2. **Brokers** — ask multiple list brokers to build to your criteria, test their samples, stick with whoever performs.
  3. **Elbow grease** — join groups and communities containing your audience, check directories for contact routes, prefer contacting outside the group so you don't look like you're milking it.
  - **The key insight**: *If you can search the database, so can everyone else.* A list you assemble yourself is the freshest, because those people haven't been hit by everyone. Downside: it takes the most time.
- **Personalization = make cold look warm**: Find **1–3 pieces of information a friend might know**, compliment them on it, and ideally show how it benefited *you*. "People like people who like them." Even strangers will give you more time if you know something about them — and they appreciate the research.
- **Big Fast Value**: Strangers give you far less time to prove worth and need far more incentive to move. *"We're not trying to tickle their interest. We're trying to blow their minds in under 30 seconds."*
  - **Give away something for free that people normally pay for.** Note the precision: not "so good they *should* pay for it" — stuff they **actually pay for**. Big difference.
- **Three Ways to Get Volume**: (1) automate delivery as far as possible, (2) automate distribution as far as possible, (3) follow up more times in more ways.
- **The Timing Re-run**: After multiple attempts across multiple channels, **wait 3–6 months and do it again**. Nothing about your offer changed — their circumstances did. Same list, entirely new set of engaged leads.

## Key Concepts
- **Cold outreach is a numbers game**: Once you know how much outreach it takes to engage a lead, there's only one thing left to do — *more*.
- **Reading level**: Hormozi rewrote their cold outreach template below a **third-grade reading level** and got **50% more responses**. Run every script through a free reading-level checker.
- **Interest groups are the warmest cold audience** — concentrated pools of people already looking for a solution. One of his favourite places to fish.
- **Multi-step for expensive things**: Sell expensive stuff in multiple steps, not on first contact. Priority one is getting interest; then schedule a time to sell.
- **Script length**: Phone and chat scripts are never more than a page or two; cold emails rarely more than half a page. *"There are no awards for prettiest script."* Get your first 100 conversations or 10,000 emails out before tweaking.
- **Replicate before you iterate**: New to an outreach team? Shadow the best person, then **double their inputs**. 200 calls → make 400. "Volume negates luck. When you do twice as many, you'll get good in half the time."
- **Rep lead allocation**: Give each rep an explicit weekly lead count and *under*-allocate. If someone can work 100 leads at full capacity, give them ~70 — so they can squeeze everything out of what they have. Equal allocation lets you set absolute quotas: "I give you 70 leads, you give me back 7 appointments." **No leads left behind.**

## Mental Models
- **Think of cold outreach as a machine with a solvable equation.** "For every X people contacted, you get Y customers. Then you simply solve for X."
- **The low-ticket workaround**: If your average ticket is too low to fund an outreach team, use cold outreach to recruit **affiliates** instead of customers — either selling them product in bulk upfront, or recruiting them to sell on commission. One affiliate sale can be worth thousands of customers.
- **Count in 100s**: Don't set a daily goal below 100, and don't stop for 100 days minimum.

## Anti-patterns
- **Expecting fast results**: Veterans told him it would take a year. He assumed 12 weeks. *"I was wrong. It took almost a year."*
- **Running cold outreach without metrics discipline**: The two failed attempts were both with people who never tracked metrics well. The third tracked everything and succeeded.
- **A mediocre lead magnet**: "If it's not big and fast, you'll blend in with the ocean of people trying to get their attention, and they'll treat you the same. They'll ignore you."
- **Opening with the pitch**: *"Hey man, want to buy some marketing services?"* → hangup.

## Worked Example
**The 9-month ramp.** A rejected sales applicant told Hormozi his previous employer — a gym software company Hormozi had never heard of — did **$10M/month from 100% cold outreach with a team of 30**, selling a retention offer that didn't even make gyms more money. Hormozi funded him to build the same machine, with the honest terms: *"You'll be expected to figure all this out. The software, the lists, everything. I'll front you the time, but we can't support you much beyond that."*

| Month | Sales | Revenue |
|---|---|---|
| September | 0 | $0 |
| October | 2 | $32,000 |
| December | 4 | $64,000 |
| January | 6 | $96,000 |
| February | 10 | $160,000 |
| March | 14 | $224,000 |
| April | 20 | $320,000 |
| May | 30 | $480,000 |

*The team asked him to pull the plug twice.* Today cold outreach generates millions per month.

**The personalized cold call** (why personalization is the whole game):

> *"[Name]?"* — "Yeah, who's this?"
> *"It's Alex. I watched a few of your videos and read that recent blog post you wrote on dog training. It was killer — really helped me out with my Doberman, she's a beast. That peanut butter trick really helped. Thanks for that."*
> *"Oh yeah, sorry, I got ahead of myself. I work for a company that helps dog trainers fill up their books. We like to partner with the best in the area, so I'm always on the lookout. We work with someone about an hour north of you, John's Doggy Daycare — heard of them?"*
> *"We ended up getting them 100 appointments in 30 days using a combination of text, email, and some ads. Do you offer similar services to them?"* — "Yes."
> *"Perfect. Then we'd be able to use that same campaign in your market and drive leads over to you. If you got a boatload of high-paying new dog training customers, you wouldn't be upset with me, would you?"*
> *"Great. Tell you what — I can walk you through the entire thing, soup to nuts, later today. Will you be around at 4:00?"*

**The lead magnet swap that 3×'d take rates.** The first four months "felt like torture." Their lead magnet was a *game planning session* — code for a sales call. Some gyms took it; most didn't. They swapped to **giving away as much free service as they could possibly afford**. Take rates tripled and cold outreach became a monster channel.

**Benchmarks by channel:**

| Channel | Volume | Step 1 | Step 2 | Engaged leads |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Phone | 100 calls/day | 20% pickup | 25% take the lead magnet | 4/day (≈1/hour) |
| Email | 100 personalized/day | 30% open | 10% reply | 3/day — **aim for 3% of list** |
| DM / voice memo | 100 personal videos or voice memos | — | 20% reply | 20 |

**Unit economics** (cold call team): reps at $15/hour + $50 per shown appointment; leads $0.10; $3,600 profit per sale. One rep calls 200 leads/day → ~2 shows. Eight hours = $120 labour + $100 commissions + $20 leads = **$240 for 2 shows = $120/show.** Close 33% → **$360 to acquire a client** against $3,600 profit = **10:1.**
*Minimum bar: make at least **3× the lifetime profit** of a customer versus what it costs to get them. The portfolio company referenced gets over **30:1**.*

**The scale ceiling.** For one portfolio company they automated scraping, crafting, and sending: **one virtual assistant sends 2,000 emails/day** (~60,000/month) using multiple pieces of software → **40 engaged leads/day** (response rate dropped, because personalization was removed) → 10% sold → **4 new customers/day**, past the 100-customers-per-month barrier. That business makes $20,000 per customer and started at Hormozi's then-minimum of $250,000/month.

## Key Takeaways
1. Solve the three stranger problems in order: list → message → volume.
2. Self-assembled lists are freshest; searchable databases are already saturated.
3. Personalize with 1–3 facts a friend would know, then compliment and connect.
4. Give away something people actually pay for. Mediocre lead magnets get ignored.
5. Write below a third-grade reading level.
6. Under-allocate leads per rep so nothing is wasted, and set absolute quotas.
7. Re-run the same list after 3–6 months — circumstances change even when your offer doesn't.
8. Expect ~a year to scale, and track every metric from day one.

**The seven benefits of cold outreach:** one message to perfect (no ad fatigue, no banner blindness) · competitors can't see what you're doing · extremely reliable and reverse-engineerable · almost no platform rule changes · far less compliance pain · no spokesperson, which makes the business sellable (it's how Gym Launch was sold) · very hard to copy, because most steps are invisible.
