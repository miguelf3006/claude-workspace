# Glossary — $100M Leads

**ACA framework** — Acknowledge, Compliment, Ask. How to reply to a warm reach-out without sounding icky. (Ch 5)

**Advertising daily checklist** — Who/what/where/to whom/when/why/how/how much/how many/how long, filled out in five minutes. (Ch 16)

**Affiliate** — Another business with a warm audience full of people like your customers, who advertises your stuff for a commission. (Ch 15)

**Big fast value** — The cold-outreach requirement: blow their mind in under 30 seconds by giving away something people actually pay for. (Ch 8)

**Call out** — Whatever you do to get an audience's attention, verbal (labels, yes questions, if-then statements, ridiculous results) or non-verbal (contrast, likeness, the scene). (Ch 9)

**Client-financed acquisition** — Getting customers to pay you back within the first 30 days so you can recycle the cash into acquiring more. (Ch 10)

**Cocktail party effect** — In a loud room one sound cuts through: your name. The psychological basis for call outs. (Ch 9)

**Cold audience** — People who have not given you permission to contact them. (Ch 4)

**Constraint** — The step in your funnel where the most leads drop off; where the smallest improvement creates the biggest gain. (Ch 11)

**Content unit** — The smallest amount of material that hooks, retains, and rewards attention. Long-form content is units linked together. (Ch 6)

**Core Four** — Warm outreach · post free content · cold outreach · paid ads. The only four ways to let anyone know about anything. (Ch 4)

**Engaged lead** — A lead who shows interest in the stuff you sell. The true output of advertising. (Ch 2)

**Give until they ask** — Give in public, ask in private; give enough that people start asking you to sell to them. (Ch 7)

**Give:ask ratio** — Benchmark ~3.5:1 (TV) to 4:1 (Facebook feed). Growing platforms over-give far more; model them, not mature platforms. (Ch 7)

**Goodwill** — Value minus price. The surplus that produces word of mouth and referrals. (Ch 12)

**Hidden costs** — The time, effort, and sacrifice required to get results — the bottom of the value equation. "If you can't give it away free, your free stuff is too expensive." (Ch 5)

**Hinge method** — Getting a genuine three-way introduction for a referral: photo, text to both, or a conversation the customer initiates. (Ch 5, Ch 12)

**Hook, retain, reward** — The three jobs of every piece of content. (Ch 6)

**Internal Core Four** — The Core Four aimed at recruiting employees instead of customers. (Ch 13)

**Lead** — A person you can contact. That's all. (Ch 2)

**Lead getter** — Someone other than you who gets leads for you: customers, employees, agencies, affiliates. (Ch 12–15)

**Lead magnet** — A complete solution to a narrow problem, free or low-cost, which once solved reveals the problem your core offer solves. (Ch 3)

**LTGP (lifetime gross profit)** — All money a customer ever spends minus all money it takes to deliver. The correct numerator for acquisition math. (Ch 10)

**LTGP:CAC** — The efficiency ratio. Target above 3:1; below it, businesses struggle to scale. (Ch 10)

**Maximum allowable CAC** — The most you can pay to acquire a customer while holding your target LTGP:CAC. The basis for affiliate payouts. (Ch 15)

**More, better, new** — The three ways to amplify any Core Four method. Below $1M profit: more first. Above: better first. (Ch 11)

**Nine-word email** — "Are you still looking to [four-word desire]?" No images, no links, nothing else. (Ch 5)

**Open to goal** — Working until you hit a set number of outcomes, no matter how long it takes. The Rule of 100 for the big kids. (Ch 16)

**Problem–solution cycle** — Every problem has a solution; every solution reveals more problems. How you choose a lead magnet's target problem. (Ch 3)

**Referral growth equation** — Referrals in minus churned customers out. Above zero, you grow without advertising. (Ch 12)

**Rule of 100** — 100 primary actions every day for 100 days straight. (Ch 11)

**Size of the pie fallacy** — Mistaking the tiny slice you advertise into for the entire available market. (Ch 11)

**Three Ds** — Document, Demonstrate, Duplicate. The training model. (Ch 13)

**Three phases of scaling ads** — Track money, lose money, print money. (Ch 10)

**Three types of lead magnet** — Reveal their problem · samples and trials · one step of a multi-step process. (Ch 3)

**Warm audience** — People who gave you permission to contact them. (Ch 4)

**What / who / when** — The framework for building ad value: the eight value elements, whose perspective experiences them, and in what timeframe. (Ch 9)
