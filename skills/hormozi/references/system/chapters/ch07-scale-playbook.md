# Chapter 7: The Scale Playbook — $1M to $10M and Beyond

## Core Idea
Past roughly $1M/year in profit, the constraint changes. **You stop being the lead getter and start building lead getters** — and the amplification order flips from *more, better, new* to **better, more, new.**

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Order Flip** (*Leads* ch11): Below $1M/yr profit → **more** first, because you haven't done enough volume for percentage changes to matter. Above → **better** first, because improvement becomes the lowest-cost, highest-return move available.
- **The Four Lead Getters, in build order** (*Leads* ch12–15):
  1. **Customers (referrals)** — the only exponential channel. Build goodwill six ways, then ask seven ways
  2. **Employees** — get them with the *Internal Core Four*, train with **Document, Demonstrate, Duplicate**
  3. **Agencies** — hire to *learn a method or platform*, with a purpose and a deadline, then drop to consulting
  4. **Affiliates** — the most advanced and most compounding; pay from maximum allowable CAC across three tiers
- **The Scaling Loop** (*Leads* ch16, step 5): run a daily action until you can afford to pay someone to do it → then **make employees your new target lead type** and repeat the whole plan → then scale again.
- **The Money Model Maturation** (*Money Models* ch14): early businesses have an attraction offer and maybe an upsell. Scaling businesses fill all four categories and then optimize the *timing* within each.
- **The Broadening Decision** (*Offers* ch4): under ~$10M/yr, niching down almost always makes more money. Above it, TAM may force you up-market, down-market, or into an adjacent market. *"If you're at $1M or $3M thinking you have capped and must expand, you are wrong. You just need to be better."*

## Key Concepts
- **The $10M difference** (*Leads* ch13): Two businesses at $5M revenue and $2M profit. One runs without you and is worth $10M+ to a buyer; one doesn't and is worth roughly nothing. *"You get rich from what you make. You become wealthy from what you own."*
- **The testing discipline that defines "better"** (*Leads* ch11): one test per week per platform, aimed at the **constraint** (biggest drop-off), **logged** so you start "a zillion improvements later, not at square one," abandoned after **4 tries or 1 month**.
- **Creative volume is the documented differentiator**: the top **0.1% of advertisers test 11× more creative** than everyone else. *"You can't scale a mediocre ad profitably, and the only way to find exceptional ads is to make more of them."*
- **Free content raises the return on everything else** — even when attribution can't see it. **78% of Gym Launch clients had consumed at least two long-form pieces before booking a call**, during a period when the team believed paid ads deserved all the credit.
- **The billing tweaks scale linearly with size** (*Money Models* ch13): four-week billing (+8.3% revenue) and the 3% processing fee are pure margin, and their absolute value grows with revenue while the effort stays at zero.

## Mental Models
- **Replace yourself as soon as you can, then make yourself useful somewhere else.** *"I could do anything better than any of my employees, but I couldn't do everything better than all my employees."*
- **If they get it wrong, we got it wrong.** The training standard: *"If you vanish tomorrow, could a stranger get the results you get if they only followed your checklist?"*
- **Buy skill, not service.** Agencies are for learning a platform with a stated exit, not for permanent outsourcing.
- **The bottleneck becomes fulfilment.** *Money Models* ch1 promises it explicitly: once 30-day cash covers acquisition, *"you'll be forced to work on everything else in your business just to keep up."*

## Anti-patterns
- **The size-of-the-pie fallacy at scale**: A $2M business spending $30k/month on one platform declaring a $15.1B industry saturated. *"For many, saying 'I'm as big as I can get' is much easier than saying 'I'm not as good at advertising as I thought.'"*
- **Competing with your own employees**: *"The more I tried to out-compete my employees, the more distracted I became and the worse my business got."*
- **The agency decay cycle**: senior rep assigned → results appear → senior rep moves to the newest client → junior rep takes over → results suffer → you cancel → you find another agency and repeat.
- **Broadening at $3M because you feel capped**: Many companies expand past $30M/year serving a single niche — chiropractors, gyms, plumbers, solar, roofers, salon owners.

## Worked Example
**The scale sequence, with the numbers each move produces.**

| Move | Source | Documented effect |
|---|---|---|
| Switch amplification order to **better first** | *Leads* ch11 | Percentage gains now exceed volume gains |
| Weekly constraint testing, logged | *Leads* ch11 | +5 points on a 5% step = **2× leads**; the same +5 on a 50% step = +10% |
| **11× the creative volume** | *Leads* ch11 | What the top 0.1% of advertisers actually do |
| **Referral ask at point of purchase** | *Leads* ch12 | **+25–30% signups**, often covering the entire ad spend |
| **Find what best customers did, make it mandatory** | *Leads* ch12 | Gym Launch: ads live + first sale within 7 days → **LTGP tripled** |
| **Hire and train with the 3 Ds** | *Leads* ch13 | acquisition.com: **~30,000 engaged leads/month** at **$3.33 per lead**, with no ads and no outreach |
| **Agency ladder** — good-enough, then elite, then consulting | *Leads* ch14 | Eight $750/hour calls replaced years of trial and error |
| **Affiliate army** | *Leads* ch15 | Allan: a few super affiliates → **$1.7M/month within 6 months** |
| **Fill the money model's empty categories** | *Money Models* ch14 | Downselling noes takes 3 of 10 buyers to 6 of 10 |
| **Prepaid blocks + four-week billing + 3% fee** | *Money Models* ch13 | 1 in 8 taking "buy 5 get 1" = **+50% 30-day profit**; billing tweak = **+41% annual profit** at 20% margins |
| **Win-back campaign to lapsed customers** | *Money Models* ch11 | 200 personalized videos, $4,000 credit each, ~20% take rate → **$1.9M annual revenue from one day of recording** |

**The single highest-leverage question at this stage**: *"Which daily action am I still personally doing that a trained person following a checklist could do?"* Every answer is a hire, and every hire returns you to step 1 of the one-page plan with a new target lead type.

## Key Takeaways
1. Past ~$1M profit, flip to better → more → new.
2. Build lead getters in order: referrals, employees, agencies, affiliates.
3. One logged test per week per platform, aimed at the constraint, abandoned after 4 tries.
4. Test far more creative than feels reasonable. The 0.1% test 11× more.
5. A business that runs without you is worth an order of magnitude more than one that doesn't.
6. Fill the empty money-model categories before buying more traffic.
7. Run the free billing tweaks — their absolute value grows with your revenue.
8. Don't broaden because you feel capped. Broaden because TAM genuinely forces it.
