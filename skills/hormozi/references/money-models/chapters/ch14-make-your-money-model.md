# Chapter 14: Make Your Money Model

## Core Idea
Assemble the four offer types into a **sequence** that recovers your acquisition cost fast enough to recycle the cash — then keep adding offers, because **every solved problem reveals the next one.**

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Assembly Order**:
  1. **Attraction offer** — turn strangers into customers and get cash upfront. *Ideally enough cash to cover the cost of the customer and the cost to deliver, multiple times over — so you can pay yourself back and get the next customer.*
  2. **Upsell offers** — maximize 30-day profit from the customers you just got
  3. **Downsell offers** — recover the people who said no
  4. **Continuity offers** — convert the whole thing into recurring revenue, then upsell prepaid blocks
- **The Five Attraction Offers**: win your money back · giveaways · decoy offers · buy X get Y free · pay less now or pay more later
- **The Four Upsell Offers**: classic · menu · anchor · rollover
- **The Three Downsell Processes**: payment plan · trial with penalty · feature downsell
- **The Three Continuity Plays**: continuity bonus · continuity discount · waived fee
- **The Design Constraint**: cover the cost of getting a customer **within 30 days**, so the same credit line funds the next customer indefinitely.

## Key Concepts
- **Offers can be reused across positions.** The same play works as an attraction offer in one model and a downsell in another — *pay less now or pay more later* is an attraction offer here and a downsell elsewhere; *trial with penalty* is a downsell for Hormozi but was an attraction offer for the HR company that sold Leila.
- **The mirror pairs worth memorizing**:
  - *Win your money back* ↔ *Trial with penalty* — one refunds if they comply, one charges if they don't. **Same criteria, opposite incentive.** Build one and you've built the other.
  - *Buy X get Y free* ↔ *Continuity discount* — the same structure, one-time versus recurring.
  - *Decoy offer* ↔ *Anchor upsell* — the same contrast mechanic, cheap-first versus expensive-first.
- **Upsells often carry the profit.** The thing you sell most is rarely the thing you profit most on.
- **Downsells can double your customer count.** 3 of 10 on the main offer plus 4 more on a trial = 6 customers from the same 10 conversations.

## Mental Models
- **"Anytime you offer something next, you upsell."** There is no ceiling on the number of offers as long as each solves a real problem. *"You lose nothing by offering to solve someone's problem."*
- **Design for the customer's problem sequence, not your product catalogue.** The rental car agent's five offers each named a problem first.
- **Make it work rather than deciding whether it will.** Swap *"this won't work for my business"* for **"how will I make this work for my business?"**
- **Solve rich people problems. They pay better.**

## Anti-patterns
- **Copying a model wholesale**: "If you just try to copy what they do, you'll be disappointed. To make it work for your business, you have to design your own."
- **Collecting cash you can't deliver against**: Especially with buy-X-get-Y-free and prepaid blocks. Budget the correct amount to service customers for the full duration.
- **Hard selling and price-dropping**: Both trade long-term trust for a short-term close.
- **Building the model but never measuring 30-day cash**: The whole design target is the 30-day recovery. If you're not tracking it, you're guessing.

## Worked Example
**A complete model, assembled from the plays in this book** (illustrative — the point is the *shape*, not the numbers):

| Stage | Play | What the customer sees | What it does for the model |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Attract** | Decoy offer | Free 21-day transformation advertised; premium tier presented in the room at $399 | Generates leads cheaply, converts ~8 in 10 to premium |
| **Upsell 1** | Classic upsell | *"You can't do the program without X"* — the immediate next problem | Adds cash the same day |
| **Upsell 2** | Menu upsell | Unsell → prescribe → A/B → card on file | Adds cash without a yes/no question |
| **Downsell** | Trial with penalty | *"How about we get you started for free?"* Card taken, fees explained after, three required check-ins | Recovers the noes; check-ins are scheduled upsell slots |
| **Continuity** | Continuity bonus | *"Get [$1,000 thing] free when you join at $X/month"* — billed every 4 weeks + 3% processing fee | Converts to recurring; second payment method captured |
| **Continuity boost** | Bulk prepaid | *"Buy five months, get one free"* | 1 in 8 taking it lifts 30-day profit ~50% |
| **Recovery** | Rollover upsell | Win-back credit to lapsed customers; rollover instead of refund | Reclaims churned revenue |

**The self-audit** — the fastest way to use this book:
1. Write down every offer you currently make, in the order a customer encounters them
2. Label each: attraction, upsell, downsell, or continuity
3. **Empty categories are your build list**, in that order
4. Calculate what percentage of your customer acquisition cost you recover in 30 days
5. Pick the single play from the empty category closest to the front and build it first

**The results Hormozi claims for these plays**: attraction offers alone *"turned $1,000 into $10,000,000 in 10 months, because when I got returns, I kept doubling down."* Win-your-money-back has *"generated over $1 billion in sales industry-wide."* One day recording win-back videos for 200 past customers produced *$1,900,000 of annual revenue.*

## Key Takeaways
1. Build in order: attraction → upsell → downsell → continuity. Cash constrains everything upstream.
2. Target full acquisition-cost recovery inside 30 days so the same money recycles.
3. Reuse plays across positions; the mirror pairs mean building one gives you two.
4. Every solved problem is the next offer. There's no cap on how many you make.
5. Audit your existing sequence by labelling each offer; empty categories are the roadmap.
6. Design your own model — inspiration transfers, copies don't.
7. Deliver on what you collect, obey advertising law, and never trade trust for a sale.
