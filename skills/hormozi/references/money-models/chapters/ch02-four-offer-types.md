# Chapter 2: The Four Types of Offers

## Core Idea
Every money model is assembled from four offer types: **attraction, upsell, downsell, and continuity.** Each solves a different problem, they work alone, and together they make a business unstoppable.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Four Offer Types**:
  1. **Attraction offers** — turn strangers into customers
  2. **Upsell offers** — get people to spend more cash
  3. **Downsell offers** — get people to say yes when they would have said no
  4. **Continuity offers** — keep people buying
- **The Logic of the Sequence** (why all four, in this order):
  - No offer for *getting* customers → you won't get as many
  - Only that one thing to offer → you won't make nearly as much as you could
  - Add an **upsell** → you finally get some cash, but lots of people still say no
  - Add **downsells** → turn those noes into yeses
  - Add **continuity** → that extra cash is now guaranteed to arrive month after month
- **The Chapter Format** (how each play is documented, and a decent template for your own notes): a doodle · the story of how he first learned it · a description of how it works · examples across industries · important notes and tactics · a summary with extra profit thoughts.

## Key Concepts
- **All businesses have money models** — "it makes a business a business." You're either designing yours or inheriting one.
- **Any offer can be used on its own, at any time, in any order.** Most offers in the book could keep a business profitable by themselves. Used in the right sequence at the right time, they compound.
- **Attraction offers come first because cash is the constraint.** Most businesses spend too much to get customers and make too little from them; cash is what buys more customers.

## Mental Models
- **Think of the four types as roles, not products.** The same product can be an attraction offer in one position and an upsell in another. What changes is the job it does in the sequence.
- **Make offers available at the moment your customer has the problem** and you'll be ahead of the competition. If they don't want it, no sweat — find somebody who does. It's a numbers game.
- **"Some money models work better in some businesses than others. They're just different ways to offer stuff."**

## Anti-patterns
- **Lying or inflating to make an offer compelling**: "State the facts. And if the facts aren't compelling, **change reality to make them compelling**, or learn to frame them in a way that is. Don't lie. Unlike credit card debt, you can't file bankruptcy to erase a bad reputation."
- **Ignoring advertising law**: These plays were learned in different situations, on different platforms, in different times, under different rules. Advertising laws change constantly and tend only to get tighter — *especially around the word "free."* Check with counsel.
- **Treating this as a copy-paste catalogue**: It's inspiration for designing your own.

## Worked Example
**Mapping the four types onto a single customer journey** (using the rental-car sequence from ch01 as the spine):

| Offer type | Job | Rental car equivalent | Gym equivalent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Attraction** | Turn a stranger into a customer | The advertised $19/day rate | Free 6-week challenge |
| **Upsell** | Get more cash from a buyer | Vehicle upgrade, late return, premium insurance | Personal training package |
| **Downsell** | Convert a no into a yes | Minimum insurance after declining premium | Payment plan or group option |
| **Continuity** | Keep them buying | Loyalty membership | Monthly membership |

**The diagnostic use.** Take your own business and fill in the four rows. Empty rows are the money you're leaving on the table, and the order tells you what to build first:
- No attraction offer → build that first; nothing else matters without customers
- Attraction only → you're capped at one transaction per customer
- Attraction + upsell → you're losing everyone who said no
- All three but no continuity → your revenue restarts from zero every month

## Key Takeaways
1. Four types: attraction, upsell, downsell, continuity. Learn what each is *for*.
2. Build in that order — attraction first, because cash constrains everything else.
3. Any single play can carry a business; sequencing is what makes it a machine.
4. Offer at the moment of the problem, not at the moment convenient for you.
5. Never manufacture facts to make an offer compelling. Change the reality or reframe it honestly.
6. Audit your own model by filling in the four rows and building the empty ones.
