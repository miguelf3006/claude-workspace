# Glossary — $100M Money Models

**A/B upsell** — Asking which product they prefer rather than whether they want one. Either answer is a sale. (Ch 9)

**Anchor upsell** — Present the very expensive thing first so everything after reads as cheap. (Ch 10)

**Attraction offer** — An offer that turns strangers into customers, usually by offering something free or discounted. (Ch 2–7)

**Buy X get Y free** — Reframing a discount as free stuff by selling more than one item at once. (Ch 6)

**Card on file** — *"Do you want to just use the card on file?"* Referring to a payment method they already have instead of asking whether they want to pay. (Ch 9)

**Classic upsell** — Solving the next problem your own offer just revealed, the moment it appears. The "you can't have X without Y" structure. (Ch 8)

**Continuity bonus** — Give a high-value bonus free if they sign up today; the bonus is usually worth more than the first payment. (Ch 13)

**Continuity discount** — Give products or services free in exchange for a longer commitment. (Ch 13)

**Continuity offer** — An offer that keeps people buying month after month. (Ch 13)

**Decoy offer** — Advertise a stripped-down version, then present it beside the premium version so the contrast sells the premium. (Ch 5)

**Downsell** — Any offer made after someone says no. Changes how they pay or what they get — never the same thing for less. (Ch 12)

**Economist play** — Three options where the bundle is priced identically to the more expensive single option, making the bundle obvious. (Ch 9)

**Feature downsell** — Lowering the price by removing a feature (often the guarantee) rather than discounting. (Ch 12)

**Free giveaway** — Advertise a chance to win a big prize; one person wins, everyone else qualifies for a discounted version. (Ch 4)

**Gift card play** — Sell gift cards at 90%+ off, usable only on other people, then roll them over into a much larger offer. (Ch 11)

**Lifetime discount** — A lower rate customers earn by staying past your average churn month. (Ch 13)

**Menu upsell** — Unsell, prescribe, A/B, card on file — four tactics that never ask a yes/no question. (Ch 9)

**Money model** — A sequence of offers. (Ch 1)

**Nudge** — A one-line preference cue added to an A/B offer: *"This is my favourite," "X is usually a safe bet."* (Ch 9)

**Pay less now or pay more later** — A fork between a discounted immediate payment with bonuses and a delayed payment with a conditional guarantee. (Ch 7)

**Payment plan downsell** — Same product, cost spread across an upfront portion and scheduled payments. (Ch 12)

**Prescription upsell** — Telling customers exactly what they need and how to use it, in detail, as if they already own it. (Ch 9)

**Rollover upsell** — Crediting some or all of a previous purchase — yours or a competitor's — toward the next offer. (Ch 11)

**30-day cost recovery** — Covering the cost of acquiring a customer within 30 days so the same credit line funds the next one. (Ch 1)

**Trial with penalty** — Free access conditional on terms; customers only pay if they *don't* meet them. The mirror of win your money back. (Ch 12)

**Unselling** — Telling customers what they *don't* need, to build goodwill and emphasize what they do. (Ch 9)

**Waived fee offer** — A large setup fee for month-to-month, waived entirely for a term commitment, payable if they break the term. (Ch 13)

**Win your money back** — Customers pay, then earn their money back (cash or store credit) by hitting a goal, taking actions, or both. (Ch 3)
