# Cheatsheet — $100M Offers

## Order of Importance (diagnose top-down)
`Starving crowd (market) > Offer strength > Persuasion skills`
- **Great** at a higher lever overpowers everything below it.
- **Normal** passes the buck to the next lever.
- **Bad** stops the equation — unless a *great* higher lever nullifies it.
- Diagnosis rule: if a good offer isn't converting, check the market *before* blaming copy, funnel, or yourself.

## Market Go / No-Go
| Indicator | Pass condition | If it fails |
|---|---|---|
| Massive pain | They *need*, not want | No price ceiling — walk |
| Purchasing power | Can afford your price | Fatal (the unemployed-resume trap) |
| Easy to target | Lists, groups, channels, associations exist | You can't advertise — walk |
| Growing | ≥ marketplace rate | Fatal and disguised (the newspaper trap) |

## Value Equation Triage
`Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)`
- Weak on **dream outcome** → reframe as *status gained in others' eyes*.
- Weak on **likelihood** → add proof, specificity, and a guarantee.
- Weak on **time delay** → engineer a fast win in week one; sell speed.
- Weak on **effort** → move DIY → DWY → DFY.
- **Default bias**: spend effort on the bottom half. The top half is cheap to copy.

## Pricing Decision Rules
- Never discount the core offer → **add bonuses instead**. Discounting teaches negotiability.
- Raise value *first*, then price. They must still get a lopsided deal.
- No strategic benefit to being second-cheapest; there is one to being most expensive.
- Don't compete on price unless you can cut cost to **1⁄10th** of competitors.
- Under **$10M/yr** → niche down. Above → consider up-market, down-market, or adjacent.
- Announce every price increase in advance — it cleans the pipeline and reads as strength.

## Guarantee Selector
| Situation | Use |
|---|---|
| Low ticket, B2C, broad | Unconditional refund |
| High margin, many required client actions | Conditional outsized refund (2–3×) |
| High fulfilment cost | Conditional service or anti-guarantee |
| Outcome measurable, you control the levers | Implied / performance (rev share, ratchets) |
| Product can't be un-seen once shown | Anti-guarantee + damaging admission |
| Systematized first result exists | Conditional delayed second payment |
| Starting out, want zero refund risk | Service-based guarantee or performance partnership |

**Guarantee math**: +30% closes with refunds doubling 5%→10% still nets **1.23×**. Don't be emotional, do the math.

## Scarcity vs. Urgency
| | Scarcity | Urgency |
|---|---|---|
| Function of | Quantity | Time |
| Mechanisms | Total cap · growth-rate cap · cohort cap · honest capacity | Rolling cohorts · seasonal re-dating · promo/bonus deadline · exploding opportunity |
| Rule | Always sell out, always announce it | Deadlines must be real |
| Cadence | ~monthly; compounds over repetition | Continuous; re-wrap for local markets |

## Thresholds & Tells
- **50–60%** of a launch's sales land in the **last 4 hours**. Don't panic mid-campaign.
- **~1/5** of prospects will pay **5×** more (fractal 80/20). Skim the top before selling the pyramid.
- **~9%/yr** is flat in real terms; growing markets may demand 20–30%.
- **$20 ≈ 1,000 people reached** locally → a 200k-person radius costs ~$10k to reach once.
- **3–5** MAGIC components per offer name. Shorter and punchier wins.
- **Tell**: prospect asks "how are you different?" → the offer failed; differentiation must be structural.
- **Tell**: you're defending price to keep a client → you've been commoditized; change the offer, not the number.
- **Tell**: you're mentally reframing a lost sale as "bad lead" → check the four market indicators first.

## Offer Fatigue Ladder (change in this order)
1. Creative (images/video) → 2. Body copy → 3. Headline/wrapper → 4. Duration → 5. Enhancer (free/discount) → 6. Monetization structure
*Operational drag increases as you descend. Once monetized, rarely change the machine.*
