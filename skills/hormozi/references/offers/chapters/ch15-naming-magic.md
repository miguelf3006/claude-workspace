# Chapter 15: Enhancing the Offer — Naming (MAGIC)

## Core Idea
Change the **wrapping paper, not the offer**. The MAGIC formula generates unlimited names for the same unchanged value stack — which is the key to evergreen lead generation in a market of any size.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The MAGIC Naming Formula** (use **3–5 components**, not necessarily all, not necessarily in order):
  - **M — Make a Magnetic Reason Why**: The reason you're running this promotion. Answers "why are they making this great offer?" and "what's in it for me?" *Think like a fraternity party planner — we once threw a party because a guy got his wisdom teeth out.* Examples: free, 88% off, giveaway, spring, summer, back to school, grand opening, new management, new building, anniversary, Halloween, new year.
  - **A — Announce Your Avatar**: Who you're for and who you're not for. As specific as possible, but no more. **Locally, go sub-market**: not Baltimore but Towson, Maryland; not Chicago but Hinsdale. Examples: BK dentists, Rolling Hills moms, salon owners, retired athletes, Brooklyn busy executives.
  - **G — Give Them a Goal**: The dream outcome — an event, a feeling, an experience, an outcome. More specific and tangible is better. Examples: pain-free, celebrity smile, first place, never out of breath, little black dress, double your profit, first client, 7 figure, $100,000.
  - **I — Indicate a Time Interval**: The duration to expect. Minutes, hours, days, weeks, months. *Compliance warning:* most platforms won't approve a quantifiable claim (income or weight loss) paired with a stated duration, because it implies a guarantee. "$10,000 in 10 days" is a problem; "make a first sale in 10 days" is not.
  - **C — Complete With a Container Word**: Signals a bundled system that can't be compared to a commodity. Examples: challenge, blueprint, boot camp, intensive, incubator, masterclass, program, detox, experience, summit, accelerator, fast track, shortcut, sprint, launch, slingshot, catapult, explosion, system, getaway, meetup, transformation, mastermind, game plan, deep dive, workshop, comeback, rebirth, reset, solution, hack, cheat code, liftoff.
  - *(Marketing theory mapping: Magnet = attention, Avatar = discrimination, Goal = purpose, Interval = timeline/method, Container = container.)*
- **The Offer Fatigue Ladder** — change things in this order, top first, because the lower you go the more operationally heavy it gets:
  1. Change the **creative** (images/videos)
  2. Change the **body copy**
  3. Change the **headline / wrapper** (e.g. "Free 6-Week Lean Challenge" → "Free 6-Week Tone Challenge"; "Holiday Hangover" → "New Year New You")
  4. Change the **duration** (6 weeks → 28 days → 8 weeks)
  5. Change the **enhancer** (the free or discount component)
  6. Change the **monetization structure** (the series of offers and price points) — *last resort*
- **Rhyme and Alliteration (optional)**: "Six-Pack Fast Track," "5-Day Bookprint Sprint," "Marriage Thrive Deep Dive," "12-Month No-Debt Reset" / "Make Money Masterclass," "Change Your Life Challenge," "Debt Detox," "Real Estate Reset," "Life Coach Liftoff." *Don't force it.*

## Key Concepts
- **The wrapper vs. the machine**: The work you do, services you provide, and products you offer remain unchanged as the name shifts.
- **Reaching once ≠ fatigue**: Most people don't notice an offer on the first mention. Fatigue happens over *years*, not months — provided you keep refreshing creative, hooks, stories, and copy.
- **Local fatigue math**: You can reach ~1,000 people for about $20. In a 200,000-person addressable area, reaching everyone once costs ~$10,000. Small TAM → faster fatigue.
- **Control and test**: Run 2–3 of your best names, note the winner, then use it as the control to test new names against.
- **Implicit egotism effect**: We're drawn to things and people that resemble us — which is why avatar-naming converts.

## Mental Models
- **Think "wrapping paper," not "product."** Entrepreneurs love change; change below the wrapper level usually just creates inefficiency and operational drag. Spend your creativity on the wrapper.
- **A great offer nobody hears about earns nothing.** "Like the tree that falls in the forest that no one hears."
- **Don't be emotional about which names win.** "I honestly have no idea why some names win and others do not." Keep trying, keep striking out, try more.
- **Name the sub-items too.** Run MAGIC on every item in the stack and every bonus — it raises perceived value automatically.

## Anti-patterns
- **Lazy naming**: "Half-assed naming your product or offering can ruin conversions."
- **Changing the machine when the creative is what's tired**: When ads fatigue, don't change your business — change the video.
- **Overlong names**: The shorter and punchier the better. Balance brevity against specificity; three to five components usually lands right.
- **Changing a monetized offer**: Once an offer is monetized, rarely change it. Rinse and repeat.

## Worked Example
**Same thing, different name:** *"Free 6-Week Stress Release Challenge"* vs. *"a float tank center session."* Same service. You're far more likely to respond to the first.

**Named offers across industries** (note how each mixes 3–5 MAGIC components):

| Named offer | Components used |
|---|---|
| Venice Free 6-Week Lean by Halloween Challenge | Avatar + Magnet + Interval + Goal + Container |
| 88% Off 12-Week Bikini Blueprint | Magnet + Interval + Goal + Container |
| Free 21-Day Mommy Makeover | Magnet + Interval + Avatar/Goal |
| 60-Minute Make Your Friends Jealous Model Hair System | Interval + Goal + Container |
| Free Bend-Over Pain-Free in 42 Days Healing Fast Track | Magnet + Goal + Interval + Container |
| Lakeway Moms $1,500 Off Your Kids' Braces | Avatar + Magnet + Goal |
| Back to School Free Braces Giveaway | Magnet + Goal |
| 90-Day Rapid Healing Intensive 81% Off | Interval + Goal + Container + Magnet |
| 7F Agency 12-Week Intensive | Avatar/Goal + Interval + Container |
| Fill Your Gym in 30 Days Free | Goal + Interval + Magnet |

**The fatigue ladder in practice:** a massage center running "6-Week Stress Release Challenge" that starts to tire doesn't rebuild the business. It becomes "42-Day Relaxing Holidays Challenge" — same core offer, different wrapper. Then, if needed, the duration shifts from 6 weeks to 28 days.

**The local paradox:** local marketing is easier to get working (trust in the familiar means higher in-person conversion at higher prices) and harder to keep working (a brick-and-mortar's TAM is its immediate radius, so offers fatigue fast). Learning to rapidly vary offers, headlines, and creative in local markets is what made Hormozi's expansion to national advertising straightforward.

## Key Takeaways
1. Use 3–5 MAGIC components; order by what sounds punchier, not by the acronym.
2. Go hyper-local on the avatar when advertising locally.
3. Watch platform compliance on quantifiable claim + duration.
4. Exhaust the light changes (creative → copy → headline) before the heavy ones (duration → enhancer → monetization).
5. Test 2–3 names, keep the winner as the control, keep challenging it.
6. Name the bonuses and sub-items too.
7. The value stack never changes. Only the wrapper does.
