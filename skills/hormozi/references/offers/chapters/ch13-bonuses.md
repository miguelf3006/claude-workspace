# Chapter 13: Enhancing the Offer — Bonuses

## Core Idea
A single offer is **less valuable than the same offer broken into component parts and stacked as bonuses**. You are not adding stuff; you are enumerating and pricing what you already do, one item at a time, until the price-to-value discrepancy snaps.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Bonus Stack Mechanic**: Anchor the price to the core offer, then expand the value with each increasingly valuable bonus until the discrepancy is "too big to bear and we snap the rubber band in their mind that is holding their wallet in their pocket."
  - When to use: Always. Every infomercial ever made says "but wait, there's more" — and airtime costs money, so it must be justified by ROI.
- **The Eleven Bonus Bullets**:
  1. **Always offer them.** Use the bulleted bundle from trim-and-stack.
  2. **Give each a special name with a benefit in the title.**
  3. **Explain**: (a) how it relates to their issue, (b) what it is, (c) how you discovered it or what you had to do to create it, (d) how it will specifically improve their life or make things faster/easier/less effort — *the value equation*.
  4. **Provide proof** — a statistic, a past client, personal experience.
  5. **Paint a vivid mental image** of life after they've already used it.
  6. **Always ascribe a price tag and justify it.**
  7. **Tools and checklists beat additional trainings** — lower effort and time, therefore higher value.
  8. **Each should address a specific concern or obstacle** and prove that belief incorrect.
  9. **Or solve their next problem before they encounter it.**
  10. **The value of the bonuses should eclipse the value of the core offer.** (Subconsciously it also implies the core must be even more valuable. It isn't — but the bias works.)
  11. **Add scarcity and urgency to the bonuses themselves.** *Scarcity version:* "Only people who sign up will have access to bonuses 1, 2, 3, never for sale anywhere else." / "I have three tickets left to my $5,000 virtual event — buy this and get one." *Urgency version:* "If you buy today I'll add the XYZ bonus that normally costs $1,000, free, because I want to reward action takers."
- **One-on-One Bonus Sequencing**: Ask for the sale **first**. If yes → reveal the bonuses after they've signed (creates a wow experience and reinforces the decision). If no → present a bonus that matches their specific perceived obstacle, then ask again. Agree with the prospect, add the bonus, ask if that's fair enough. "People have a hard time rejecting reciprocity."
- **Advanced Bonuses — Other People's Products**: Get adjacent, non-competing businesses to contribute services free in exchange for exposure to your customers. Then negotiate a group discount *and* a referral commission to yourself.

## Key Concepts
- **Wow factor test** (what should be a bonus vs. part of the core offer): Take the most distinct pieces that can almost stand on their own and pull them out to highlight them — especially things short in length but high in value. Checklists and infographics condense a lot into a small space; nobody pays a lot for a "product launch map," but as a bonus it's perceived as very valuable.
- **The bonus vault**: The longer you're in business, the more of these assets you accumulate. Keep them in your back pocket to sprinkle in and close deals.
- **Ideal bonus formats**: Information products (high perceived value, low cost, zero operational effort beyond a login), tickets to virtual experiences or events, and a higher fixed-cost service level like a month of VIP — which doubles as an upsell into that tier.

## Mental Models
- **Add bonuses instead of discounting. Always.** Discounting the core offer teaches customers your prices are negotiable — terrible. Bonuses close from a position of strength and goodwill rather than weakness.
- **Think "enumerate, don't add."** You may already do many things; until you list them, they are unknown and therefore worth nothing.
- **Build the asset once, give it away endlessly.** Anything that clearly costs time or money to create but can be given infinitely is a perfect bonus.

## Anti-patterns
- **Discounting to close**: The single most common and most damaging alternative to bonusing.
- **Unnamed, unpriced bonuses**: A bonus without a benefit-bearing name and a justified price tag adds almost nothing.
- **More trainings as bonuses**: Higher effort and time for the buyer, so lower value. Tools and checklists win.

## Worked Example
**The pain-clinic partner stack.** Own a pain clinic with a $400 offer? Assemble bonuses from adjacent businesses at zero cost:

| Partner bonus | Stated value |
|---|---|
| Massage therapist — 1–2 free massages | — |
| Chiropractor — 2 free adjustments | $100 |
| Low-inflammation food company — product discounts | $50 savings |
| Braces and orthotics discounts | $150 savings |
| Local health club — free PT session + free month pool access | $100 |
| Pharmacy — drug discounts | $100/month savings |

Repeat across providers — get 10 chiropractors to each give one free adjustment and you have 10 in the bundle. The bonuses alone now exceed the $400 price.

**Then make it a revenue stream.** Negotiate affiliate commissions for the introductions: chiropractor $100/person · food company free food · orthotics $100/person · health club free membership or $50/signup · pharmacy $100/person. Your $400 offer now carries the possibility of **an extra $350 profit** — from customers you already paid to acquire.

**The real version.** Gym Launch clients who used sister company Prestige Labs as sponsored athletes got a **30% discount**, and the sponsored athlete was paid **40% of all sales netted after the discount**. Their clients pay 30% less than the main site, they get paid for giving away exclusive discounts, and Prestige Labs gets customers in exchange for commission. Everyone wins.

**The habit that builds the vault:** create checklists, tools, swipe files, scripts, and templates — anything that takes lots of time to create but is easy to use once created. Then record *every* workshop, webinar, event, and interview and use them as bonuses to crush a specific perceived obstacle.

## Key Takeaways
1. Break the offer into parts and present them one at a time. The stack is the mechanism.
2. Never discount the core offer. Add value instead.
3. Name, describe, prove, visualize, and price every bonus.
4. Bonus value should exceed core-offer value.
5. Ask for the sale before revealing bonuses one-on-one; use bonuses as objection-specific responses on the second ask.
6. Source bonuses from partners for free, then negotiate commissions on the referrals.
7. Prefer tools and checklists over more content.
